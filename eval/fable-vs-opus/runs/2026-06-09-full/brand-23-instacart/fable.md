**Instacart's delivery growth has stalled. Its ad business just hit $1B**

- **Story type:** contrarian
- **Why a journalist cares:** A public-markets or business-of-tech reporter covering how gig-era marketplaces re-platform their revenue — the kind who writes "what is this company actually now" pieces about publicly traded consumer-tech firms. The prevailing belief is that Instacart is a grocery-delivery company; the evidence against it is that delivery order growth has slowed to single digits while the ad business reaches a $1B annual run rate. Not for product reviewers, grocery shopper-facing consumer press, or ad-trade reporters (they get the next angle).
- **Why now:** The $1B run-rate disclosure is the hook — it's the moment the revenue-mix question becomes concrete and askable.
- **Decay:** week — the disclosure carries the angle past the announcement day because the business-model question stays open, but it goes stale once the next quarter's numbers land.
- **What makes it distinct:** Inversion lens. Every other angle here treats one business line as the story; this one makes the tension between the lines the story — slowing core, accelerating adjacency. The protagonist is the company's identity, argued to a markets desk.
- **Proof it needs:** how the $1B run rate is calculated (annualized quarter or trailing twelve months); the actual delivery-growth figure behind "single digits"; revenue mix or segment growth rates; an exec willing to say on record whether ads and in-store tech are the growth strategy. As a public company, confirm everything cited is already publicly disclosed.
- **Facts it rests on:** ad business at a $1B annual run rate; grocery-delivery order growth slowed to single digits; Instacart is publicly traded and profitable.

**A grocery marketplace built a $1B ad business out of 6,000 brands**

- **Story type:** data
- **Why a journalist cares:** An advertising-trade reporter covering retail media networks — the beat tracking where CPG ad dollars move and which networks have real scale versus deck-slide scale. The two numbers are the story: $1B run rate, 6,000+ consumer brands buying. Not for markets reporters (previous angle), grocery-ops trades, or general tech press.
- **Why now:** The run-rate milestone is being disclosed now; retail-media reporters cover network-scale milestones as news.
- **Decay:** week — a scale milestone supports trade coverage for days, not hours, but it's a one-time number.
- **What makes it distinct:** Data lens, scale lead. The protagonist is the ad buyer and the retail-media market, not Instacart's stock narrative. Same fact as the contrarian angle, structurally different reader: this one answers "should my brand's media plan care," not "what is this company."
- **Proof it needs:** the run-rate calculation method; what counts as a "consumer brand" in the 6,000 figure; ad formats and where the ads run; any performance or growth-rate context Instacart will put on record. Note the facts supplied do not say ads run on the smart carts — do not claim that without confirmation.
- **Facts it rests on:** $1B annual ad run rate; ads from over 6,000 consumer brands; partnerships with more than 1,500 retail banners.

**The checkout line is quietly disappearing from a few hundred grocery stores**

- **Story type:** trend
- **Why a journalist cares:** A grocery-operations or store-tech trade reporter covering front-end automation, checkout labor, and in-store hardware deployments. Caper carts tracking items as shoppers add them and skipping checkout entirely is a concrete, countable instance of the checkout-automation story — with a real expansion underway. Not for ad-trade or markets reporters, and not for consumer gadget press unless a named chain near their readers is live.
- **Why now:** The rollout to more grocery chains is being announced now; expansion news is the peg.
- **Decay:** 24hr — standard company rollout news; the broader checkout-automation trend frame can stretch it to a week if a named chain anchors it.
- **What makes it distinct:** Ladder lens, zoomed down to the store floor. The protagonist is the physical store and the checkout process — hardware and operations, not revenue lines.
- **Proof it needs:** named chains deploying the carts; a real store count behind "a few hundred"; the expansion timeline and target store numbers; any operational data (basket size, throughput, shrink) Instacart will share; ideally a store walk-through or shopper-visible detail a reporter can verify.
- **Facts it rests on:** Caper smart carts track items as shoppers add them and skip the checkout line; live in a few hundred stores; rolling out to more grocery chains with plans to expand.

**Why 1,500 grocery banners are renting their tech stack instead of building it**

- **Story type:** counterposition
- **Why a journalist cares:** A grocery-retail trade reporter covering retailer strategy — the make-versus-buy decision grocers face on e-commerce, in-store tech, and media. Instacart's position (1,500+ banners, carts in stores, ads on top) makes it the vendor side of a live strategic argument: does outsourcing this stack help grocers or hollow them out. Not for consumer press or ad buyers; wrong for markets desks too.
- **Why now:** Honest answer: the strategic question is evergreen for the trade; the cart expansion gives it a current peg, but the angle survives without one.
- **Decay:** month — a strategy piece for the grocery trade, not event-driven; the expansion peg merely makes it timelier.
- **What makes it distinct:** Perspective lens, retailer protagonist. The other angles are about Instacart; this one is about the grocer's decision, with Instacart as evidence. It only works if a grocer is in the story.
- **Proof it needs:** at least one named retail banner willing to discuss why it chose Instacart's carts or ads over building in-house; what the commercial terms look like at a high level; an Instacart exec who will engage with the dependency question rather than dodge it. Without a named grocer, this angle is not pitchable.
- **Facts it rests on:** partnerships with more than 1,500 retail banners; smart carts live in a few hundred stores; ads from over 6,000 brands.

**Refused angles**

- "Instacart revolutionizes grocery shopping with AI-powered smart carts" — `slop`. Press-release headline; "AI-powered" plus "revolutionizes" is the exact copy a journalist deletes.
- "Smart carts become a new ad surface for 6,000 brands" — `hallucinated_fact`. The supplied facts never say ads run on the Caper carts. If that's true and confirmable, it's a strong angle — but it isn't in the facts given.
- "Instacart proves grocery delivery is dying" — `hallucinated_fact`. Single-digit growth is slowing, not dying; the facts also say the company is profitable. Overreach a reporter would punish.
- "What Instacart's milestone means for the AI-in-retail boom" — `no_journalist_shape`. "AI reporter" is not a beat, and the angle is a topic, not a story; it adds nothing the contrarian and store-tech angles don't already carry with real protagonists.
- "Instacart hits $1B ad run rate AND expands smart carts" — `duplicate`. That's the announcement restated, not an angle; both halves are kept separately with distinct protagonists above.

**Uncomfortable questions**

- How is the $1B run rate calculated — annualized from one strong quarter, or trailing twelve months? Reporters will ask first, and the answer changes whether the milestone holds up.
- What is the actual delivery order-growth number? "Single digits" spans 1% to 9%, and the contrarian angle's strength depends on which end.
- Can you name the grocery chains getting the carts, and the real store count? "A few hundred stores" and "more grocery chains" are too vague to anchor the two in-store angles.
- Does ad revenue depend on delivery order volume? If ads are sold against delivery traffic, slowing delivery growth eventually caps the ad story — and a sharp reporter will connect those dots whether you do or not.
- As a public company, is everything here already in your filings or public statements? Pitching undisclosed financial detail is a problem bigger than PR.
