**Wise routes cross-border business payments through a regulated stablecoin, skipping correspondent banks**

- **Story type:** counterposition
- **Why a journalist cares:** A payments-infrastructure reporter at a fintech trade outlet (think a correspondent-banking / cross-border payments desk) covering the rails that move B2B money. The hook is the head-on collision with the correspondent-banking system — a profitable, listed incumbent of cheap transfers now says the multi-hop bank network is the part to route around. That beat will want the mechanics: where the stablecoin sits, what leg of the journey it replaces, and what breaks. Not for crypto-price/markets reporters, consumer-money columnists, or general startup press.
- **Why now:** The launch is live as of today's release; the framing (cheaper/faster than correspondent banking) is the timely claim that desk will want to pressure-test now.
- **Decay:** 24hr — this is standard launch news; the rails-vs-banks framing can stretch a few days but the announcement peg is a one-day event.
- **What makes it distinct:** Perspective lens, incumbent/competitor protagonist — the story is the correspondent-banking system as the thing being displaced, not Wise's product or its customers. The trend angle below zooms up to the category; this one is the direct power-balance confrontation.
- **Proof it needs:** which stablecoin and which partner (the release names neither — a payments reporter will not write the mechanics story without it); a concrete settlement-time and cost comparison versus correspondent banking on at least one named corridor; which leg of the transfer the stablecoin actually replaces.
- **Facts it rests on:** businesses can hold and settle in a regulated stablecoin to move money across borders faster and cheaper than the correspondent-banking system; Wise frames this as extending its mission of cheap, transparent transfers.
