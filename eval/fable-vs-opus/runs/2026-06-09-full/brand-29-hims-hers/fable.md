**A newly profitable telehealth company is selling cheap compounded semaglutide — and branded drugmakers want it stopped**

- **Story type:** counterposition
- **Why a journalist cares:** A pharma-business reporter at a healthcare business outlet covering the GLP-1 market's power balance — who controls supply, who controls price, and what happens when a telehealth platform undercuts branded manufacturers with compounded versions. The manufacturer pushback is already real, per the facts, which makes this a live fight, not a launch announcement. Not for consumer wellness press, lifestyle outlets, or general startup blogs.
- **Why now:** The launch is live and branded manufacturers have already pushed back. The conflict exists today, not hypothetically.
- **Decay:** 24hr — the launch is a one-day peg; the underlying fight has a longer tail, but the news hook is the launch itself.
- **What makes it distinct:** Perspective lens, competitor/incumbent protagonist. The story is the collision between Hims & Hers and the branded manufacturers — not the patient, the regulator, or the stock.
- **Proof it needs:** the actual price versus a branded GLP-1 (a "fraction" is not a number); an on-record company response to the manufacturer pushback; an answer to the supply-source question, because it is the first thing this reporter will ask.
- **Facts it rests on:** compounded semaglutide prescriptions at a fraction of branded-drug prices; branded manufacturers have pushed back.

**Refused angles**

- "Hims & Hers is revolutionizing weight-loss care" — `slop`.
- "Everyone says compounded drugs are unsafe — Hims proves they're fine" — `hallucinated_fact`. A contrarian angle needs evidence against the FDA's flagged risks, and the fact block contains none. A licensed-provider review is a process claim, not safety evidence.
- "The founder's boldest bet yet" — `hallucinated_fact`. No founder names, history, or motivation appear in the facts; the profile would be invented.
- "Weight-loss drugs go mainstream" — `no_journalist_shape`. A topic, not a story — no protagonist, no new fact, no beat that hasn't already written it.
- "The GLP-1 gold rush comes to telehealth" — `duplicate` of the trend angle, with worse framing and the same facts.

**Uncomfortable questions**

- Who compounds the semaglutide? The supply source is unnamed, and it is the first question on the two strongest angles. "We can't say" converts neutral coverage into hostile coverage.
- What does "a fraction of branded-drug prices" mean in dollars? No price, no affordability story and no credible conflict story.
- What is the company's legal basis for selling compounded semaglutide given the FDA's flagged risks — and will counsel let an executive state it on record?
- Is the blood test required before prescribing, or an optional add-on? If optional, the diagnostics trend angle collapses into marketing.
- Are you ready for the version of the conflict story where Hims & Hers is the subject of the manufacturers' complaint, not the challenger hero? The same facts support both framings, and you don't control which one runs.
