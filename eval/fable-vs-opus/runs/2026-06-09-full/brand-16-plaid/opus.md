**Plaid launches Pay by Bank, letting merchants route around card networks**

- **Story type:** counterposition
- **Why a journalist cares:** A payments-industry trade reporter covering the card-network-vs-account-to-account power balance (the desk that writes about interchange, Visa/Mastercard pricing, and merchant acceptance economics). The hook is structural: a 12,000-institution connector is now positioned as a card-network bypass, and that desk can test whether instant bank verification plus FedNow is finally a credible alternative to cards at the point of sale. Not for consumer personal-finance writers, general startup press, or crypto-payments outlets.
- **Why now:** The launch is live today; the bypass framing is only news while the announcement is fresh.
- **Decay:** 24hr — a product launch is a one-day cycle unless a named merchant or a regulatory peg extends it.
- **What makes it distinct:** Perspective lens, incumbent/competitor protagonist — the card networks are the implicit antagonist, and the story is about who loses interchange, not about Plaid's feature set.
- **Proof it needs:** at least one named launch merchant on record (none exists yet); the actual flat per-transaction fee figure; a card-network or merchant-association response; substantiation for "card networks bypassed" beyond the verification/rails description.
- **Facts it rests on:** merchants accept account-to-account payments that bypass card networks; merchants pay a flat per-transaction fee instead of card interchange; Plaid connects to over 12,000 financial institutions; the product uses instant bank verification and FedNow rails.

**The "70% lower than cards" claim — where account-to-account actually beats interchange**

- **Story type:** data
- **Why a journalist cares:** A payments-economics or fintech-data reporter who scrutinizes cost claims and unit economics (the kind who has written interchange-cost breakdowns). The number is the story: "up to 70% lower" is bounded to "large transactions," which is itself the finding — flat fees beat percentage interchange precisely where ticket size is high, and the reporter can pressure-test where the crossover point is. Not for outlets that would reprint the 70% figure uncritically as a headline.
- **Why now:** The figure is being put forward today with the launch; it is freshest as a claim to interrogate now.
- **Decay:** week — a cost-economics breakdown can run past launch day if the underlying numbers hold up.
- **What makes it distinct:** Data lens (scale/relationship), contextualized — this angle is about the conditions under which the saving is real, not about the launch event. It directly engages the asterisk ("up to," "on large transactions") that the counterposition angle treats as background.
- **Proof it needs:** the flat per-transaction fee in dollars; the transaction-size threshold where 70% holds; the methodology and sample behind "early merchants see"; how many merchants that sample represents.
- **Facts it rests on:** flat per-transaction fee instead of interchange; Plaid says early merchants see fees up to 70% lower than cards on large transactions; the company says the saving applies to large transactions.

**FedNow gets its first big merchant-payments use case**

- **Story type:** trend
- **Why a journalist cares:** A payments-infrastructure or fintech-rails reporter tracking FedNow adoption (the beat that has been asking whether anyone would build real consumer-facing volume on the new instant rails). The entry point is the rail, not the company: a recognizable connector building merchant acceptance on FedNow is evidence about whether the rails are reaching commercial scale. Not for company-milestone desks or investor newsletters.
- **Why now:** Honest hook is the launch itself; there is no supplied current FedNow news peg, so this is launch-pegged, not breaking. If a fresh FedNow signal exists, the angle gets sharper — but none was provided.
- **Decay:** week — rails-adoption trend pieces have a longer window than the launch news.
- **What makes it distinct:** Ladder lens, zoomed up to the rail/infrastructure altitude — the protagonist is FedNow as a system, distinct from the card-bypass framing (which is about networks losing) and the cost-data angle (which is about merchant savings).
- **Proof it needs:** confirmation Pay by Bank is live on FedNow in production, not pilot; transaction volume or a named merchant to make the "use case" real; any independent data on FedNow merchant adoption to support the trend claim.
- **Facts it rests on:** the product uses the new FedNow rails; the product uses instant bank verification; Plaid connects to over 12,000 financial institutions.

**Uncomfortable questions**

- Is there a single named merchant willing to go on record? Without one, the counterposition and FedNow angles rest on a launch with no demonstrated adoption, and reporters will notice.
- What is the actual flat per-transaction fee, in dollars? The entire cost story — and the 70% claim — is unverifiable until that number exists.
- At what transaction size does "up to 70% lower" actually hold, and how many "early merchants" is that based on? "Up to" and "large transactions" are doing heavy lifting.
- Does "bypass card networks" mean cards are genuinely out of the flow, or just not the default rail? The counterposition angle lives or dies on the precise answer.
- Is there a current, real news peg (a FedNow milestone, an interchange-regulation move) that this could anchor to? None was supplied — none of these angles should claim a breaking hook it doesn't have.
