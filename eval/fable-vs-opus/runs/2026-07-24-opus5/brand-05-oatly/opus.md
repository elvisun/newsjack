**Oatly's reformulated barista blend cuts climate footprint 31% per liter, verified by third-party LCA**

- **Story type:** data
- **Why a journalist cares:** A sustainability or food-systems reporter at a climate trade outlet or a food-and-beverage business desk who covers product-level carbon claims and scrutinizes them. The hook is a specific, third-party-verified per-liter reduction on a reformulation — the kind of number that desk can pressure-test (baseline, system boundary, who paid for the LCA). Not for general consumer-lifestyle press that will reprint the number uncritically, and not for markets reporters.
- **Why now:** The reformulation and the verified number are being rolled out now; verified product-LCA claims have a short freshness window before they become old packaging.
- **Decay:** 24hr — a product/sustainability-claim launch is a standard same-cycle event; the underlying methodology debate can stretch to a week if a reporter digs in.
- **What makes it distinct:** Data lens (change over time — old formula vs. new formula), with the number as the protagonist. Differs from the partnership and plant-closure angles, which are about distribution and cost, not the footprint claim itself.
- **Proof it needs:** the named third-party LCA firm and the full report; the previous-formula baseline and the system boundary the 31% is measured against (cradle-to-gate vs. cradle-to-grave); whether the LCA was commissioned/paid by Oatly; confirmation the 31% is per liter of finished product, not per ingredient.
- **Facts it rests on:** the new barista blend claims a 31% lower climate footprint per liter than its previous formula; the claim is verified by a named third-party LCA firm.

**A 1,400-location chain makes Oatly the default non-dairy pour across the US Northeast**

- **Story type:** customer-story
- **Why a journalist cares:** A coffee-industry or restaurant/foodservice trade reporter who covers chain menu and supply decisions, plus the regional business desk in the Northeast. The hook is "default" — not "available," but the standard non-dairy option at 1,400 locations, which is a real foodservice supply win and shifts pour volume. Not for national consumer press treating it as a flavor story, and not for sustainability desks (that's a different angle).
- **Why now:** The partnership and default-status rollout are happening now; foodservice menu changes are timely and regional.
- **Decay:** 24hr — a named-partnership launch is a same-cycle event.
- **What makes it distinct:** Perspective lens, customer/partner as protagonist — the chain's decision is the story, not Oatly's product chemistry. Distinct from the data angle (footprint) and the contrarian angle (closing a plant).
- **Proof it needs:** the named chain and confirmation it can be identified publicly; whether "default" means oat is poured unless the customer asks otherwise, or simply the listed non-dairy option; any upcharge or no-upcharge policy at the chain; a quote from the chain's supply or menu lead on why Oatly and why now.
- **Facts it rests on:** the new blend becomes the default non-dairy option at a 1,400-location regional coffee chain in the US Northeast; no price change at retail.

**Oatly wins its biggest pour-volume deal in the same quarter it closes a US plant**

- **Story type:** contrarian
- **Why a journalist cares:** A consumer-packaged-goods or food-business reporter who covers company turnarounds and the gap between growth headlines and operating reality. The prevailing read of a big distribution win is "the brand is winning"; the fact cutting against it is that Oatly is simultaneously shutting one of three US plants and is still unprofitable. The tension — landing more demand while cutting US manufacturing footprint — is the story. Not for sustainability or coffee-trade press; this is a business-strategy beat.
- **Why now:** Both moves — the chain default and the plant closure — are happening in the same window, which is what makes the contrast pitchable now rather than evergreen.
- **Decay:** week — the strategy/turnaround framing can run after the launch day if the reporter gets the financials.
- **What makes it distinct:** Inversion lens — a real, widely-held belief (a marquee distribution deal signals a healthy, expanding brand) set against real evidence (plant closure, still unprofitable). Distinct from the customer-story angle, which sells the win at face value.
- **Proof it needs:** which of the three US plants is closing and its capacity/headcount; whether the new chain volume is served from the remaining plants or imported; the actual operating-loss figure and the size of the narrowing; whether the closure is framed as efficiency or retrenchment. The user has not supplied the loss figure or which plant — these are required before pitching.
- **Facts it rests on:** Oatly remains unprofitable and reported a narrowing operating loss last quarter; the company is closing one of its three US plants to cut costs; the chain default win is happening concurrently.
