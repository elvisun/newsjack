**Discord undercuts the 30% app-store cut with a 10% fee on creator subscriptions**

- **Story type:** counterposition
- **Why a journalist cares:** A platform-economics reporter at a tech business outlet or a creator-economy trade newsletter covering the long fight over platform take rates (the Apple/Google 30% standard, Epic v. Apple, Substack's 10%, Patreon's tiers). The story isn't "Discord launched subscriptions" — it's that Discord priced its cut at a third of the app-store standard and is making the take rate the pitch. This reporter can test whether 10% is a durable position or an introductory number. Not for gaming-culture desks, consumer how-to writers, or general startup blogs.
- **Why now:** The launch is live today, and the take-rate fight is a recurring beat that a fresh, deliberately-low number gives a reporter a reason to revisit.
- **Decay:** 24hr — the launch peg is a standard one-day company-news cycle; the take-rate framing can stretch to a few days if a reporter wants context.
- **What makes it distinct:** Contrarian/counterposition lens — the angle is built on the explicit contrast between Discord's 10% and the 30% app-store standard, both of which are in the facts. It pits Discord against the incumbent platforms, not against other chat apps.
- **Proof it needs:** confirmation that 10% is the standing rate and not a promotional intro rate that rises later; whether the 10% sits on top of, or absorbs, the Apple/Google in-app-purchase cut when a creator pays through a mobile app (this is the question that decides whether 10% is real); any Discord exec willing to say on record why they chose 10%.
- **Facts it rests on:** server owners can charge monthly fees for premium roles and content; Discord takes a 10% platform fee, below the 30% app-store standard; the feature targets the creator and gaming-community economy.

**Discord turns 200M users into a payments business ahead of a reported IPO**

- **Story type:** funding-mechanics
- **Why a journalist cares:** A markets or fintech-adjacent reporter covering pre-IPO companies and how private platforms build defensible revenue before they file. The hook is the timing: a privately held company reportedly preparing for an IPO is launching a recurring-revenue line that takes a cut of creator payments. This reporter can examine whether a payments take-rate business is the revenue story Discord wants to show the market. Not for gaming reporters, product reviewers, or creator how-to outlets.
- **Why now:** The reported IPO preparation makes a new monetization line timely as a signal of how Discord intends to present its business; the launch is the concrete peg.
- **Decay:** 24hr — tied to the launch announcement; the IPO-readiness framing depends on the "reportedly preparing" status, which is not a confirmed event the user can stand on.
- **What makes it distinct:** Perspective lens, the money as protagonist — this is about Discord's revenue model and IPO posture, not the creators or the take rate as a competitive weapon.
- **Proof it needs:** the source and reliability of the "reportedly preparing for an IPO" claim before it goes in a pitch; whether Discord will disclose any revenue contribution target; clarity that there is currently no creator-earnings data, so the revenue story is a projection, not a result.
- **Facts it rests on:** the company says it has over 200 million monthly active users; Discord is privately held and reportedly preparing for an IPO; Discord takes a 10% platform fee; server owners can charge monthly fees.

**Discord opens a paid tier inside community servers built on free membership**

- **Story type:** trend
- **Why a journalist cares:** A creator-economy trade reporter covering how community platforms (Discord, Patreon, Twitch, Geneva) are moving from advertising and one-off tips toward owner-run recurring subscriptions. The angle is the structural shift: Discord servers have historically been free communities, and paid premium roles change the relationship between server owners and members. This reporter covers monetization mechanics for community builders, not Discord's corporate finances. Not for general business desks or fintech reporters chasing the IPO line.
- **Why now:** The feature is launching now; the broader "communities become businesses" trend has a longer window.
- **Decay:** week — the launch is the peg, but the community-monetization trend can carry a context piece for several days.
- **What makes it distinct:** Ladder lens, mid-altitude — the protagonist is the server owner and the changing nature of community membership, not the take rate (counterposition angle) or Discord's revenue (funding-mechanics angle).
- **Proof it needs:** a named server owner willing to describe what they will charge for and why; evidence that server members will pay for premium roles rather than churn; clarification of whether this competes with or complements existing creator tools owners already use. Note: the facts state there is no creator-earnings data yet, so any "creators are already earning" framing is unsupported.
- **Facts it rests on:** server owners can charge monthly fees for premium roles and content; the feature targets the creator and gaming-community economy; the revenue-share program is new with no earnings data yet.

**Uncomfortable questions**

- Is the 10% fee the standing rate, or an introductory number that rises later? And does it stack on top of Apple's and Google's in-app-purchase cut when a creator pays through a mobile app? If it stacks, the "below the 30% standard" pitch collapses on first read.
- How solid is the "reportedly preparing for an IPO" claim? If it's a single unconfirmed report, the funding-mechanics angle is built on sand and a markets reporter will ask for the source.
- With no creator-earnings data yet, every angle is about intent, not results. Is there a single named server owner or pilot creator who can speak to what they'll charge and why? Without one, the trend angle has no concrete floor.
- Of the 200M monthly active users, how many are in servers that could plausibly run paid tiers? A platform-wide number is not a payments-business number.
