The unresolved part — no utility will attach its name — is a legitimate part of the story for this reporter, not a reason to skip it. Not for consumer-gadget desks, general startup press, or drone-hobbyist outlets.
- **Why now:** The launch is live today. That is the whole hook; there is no external event behind it.
- **Decay:** 24hr — standard product-launch news cycle. It stays alive past a day only if a utility goes on record.
- **What makes it distinct:** Perspective lens, customer/operator protagonist — the story sits with the utility's inspection workflow, not with Skydio's product or its geopolitics. The other kept angles move to the money-and-manufacturing frame and to the abandoned consumer business.
- **Proof it needs:** at least one named utility willing to describe the route it flies and what it replaced; how the drone handles regulatory requirements for flight beyond visual line of sight; what a "defect report" actually contains and who signs off on it; flight cadence and how many miles or substations one dock covers.
- **Facts it rests on:** the drone docks, charges, and runs scheduled inspection flights of power lines and substations; it sends defect reports automatically; the company says it has contracts with several US utilities; no named utility customer is disclosed in the release.
