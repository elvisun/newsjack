**The cash register wants to be the bank: Toast bundles lending and payroll for 120,000 restaurants**

- **Story type:** trend
- **Why a journalist cares:** A fintech reporter at a fintech or payments trade outlet covering embedded finance — the pattern of vertical software companies layering financial products on top of their distribution. Toast is a clean case study: a publicly traded POS company saying out loud that lending and fintech carry higher margins than its hardware. The reporter can test whether "every vertical SaaS company becomes a fintech" survives contact with a real P&L. Not for consumer food media, local restaurant press, or general startup blogs.
- **Why now:** The bundling move and the 120,000-location milestone are the current update; the margin admission gives the trend piece its spine.
- **Decay:** week — the bundling news is a 24-hour event, but the embedded-finance trend frame supports a week of follow-up coverage.
- **What makes it distinct:** Ladder lens, zoomed up — the category shift is the protagonist, not Toast. The other kept angles drop to the incumbent banks, the restaurant operator, and the public-market margin story.
- **Proof it needs:** what the bundling concretely changes (pricing, packaging, or just positioning); an attach-rate or revenue-mix figure an exec will say on record; a named executive willing to defend the "fintech is higher-margin than hardware" claim under questioning.
- **Facts it rests on:** Toast is bundling lending and payroll into its restaurant platform; crossed 120,000 locations; the company says lending and fintech are higher-margin than its hardware business; publicly traded.

**Restaurants are borrowing from their point-of-sale system instead of their bank**

- **Story type:** counterposition
- **Why a journalist cares:** A small-business banking or lending reporter at a banking trade or business outlet covering who actually finances Main Street. Toast Capital advances cash repaid as a share of daily sales — underwritten by the payment flow Toast already sees — which positions the POS company against community banks and traditional small-business lenders for restaurant credit. The incumbents are the foil. Not for restaurant trade press, equities desks, or product-review outlets.
- **Why now:** The bundling push makes the lending product front-and-center this week rather than a quiet side business.
- **Decay:** week — the bank-vs-platform framing holds as long as the bundling news is fresh.
- **What makes it distinct:** Perspective lens, incumbent protagonist — the story is the power balance between banks and platforms in restaurant credit, not Toast's margins or the trend writ large.
- **Proof it needs:** a named restaurant that took a Toast Capital advance and will discuss the terms and effective cost; an answer — or at least a prepared response — on default rates, because the company has not disclosed them and a lending reporter will ask first; an on-record explanation of how daily-sales data informs underwriting.
- **Facts it rests on:** Toast Capital offers cash advances repaid as a share of daily sales; Toast processes tens of billions in annual restaurant sales; bundling lending into the platform.

**Tipped wages, split shifts, and the payroll problem restaurant software keeps dodging**

- **Story type:** customer-story
- **Why a journalist cares:** A restaurant-industry trade reporter covering back-office operations and labor compliance. Tipped and hourly payroll — tip pooling, wage credits, variable shifts — is a genuine operational pain, and a payroll product built specifically for it is a workable trade story if a real operator describes the before-and-after. Not for fintech reporters, markets desks, or national business outlets; this is a trade piece.
- **Why now:** The bundling announcement gives a peg, but honestly this angle is close to evergreen — the labor-ops pain doesn't expire. Say so when pitching.
- **Decay:** month — labor-ops trade stories run on the strength of the operator example, not the news cycle.
- **What makes it distinct:** Ladder lens, zoomed down — one operator's payroll pain is the story. Different protagonist (the customer), different altitude, and a different outlet type from the three finance-framed angles.
- **Proof it needs:** a named restaurant operator willing to talk about tipped/hourly payroll on record; specifics on what the product actually handles (the fact block only says it "targets restaurants juggling tipped and hourly staff" — that's positioning, not capability); ideally a concrete time-saved or error-reduced result.
- **Facts it rests on:** the payroll product targets restaurants juggling tipped and hourly staff; bundled into a platform now at 120,000 locations.

**Toast got profitable by selling money, not hardware**

- **Story type:** funding-mechanics
- **Why a journalist cares:** A public-markets or equities reporter covering vertical SaaS business models — specifically the path-to-profitability story where the software-plus-hardware company turns out to earn its margin on financial services. Toast recently reached profitability and says fintech outearns its hardware; the margin mix is the story. Not for restaurant trade press, consumer media, or product reviewers.
- **Why now:** The bundling move plus recent profitability makes the margin-mix question current; without a stated earnings date, this rides the bundling news.
- **Decay:** week — markets analysis pieces can run several days behind the announcement if the numbers hold up.
- **What makes it distinct:** Perspective lens, investor/money protagonist — the public-market economics are the story, not the category trend (angle one) or the bank rivalry (angle two). It's the difference between "what this means for the industry" and "what this means for the stock."
- **Proof it needs:** segment-level margin or revenue figures on record (the fact block claims higher margin but supplies no numbers); the quarter in which profitability was reached; a CFO or exec available to walk through the mix.
- **Facts it rests on:** publicly traded; recently reached profitability; says lending and fintech are higher-margin than the hardware business; processes tens of billions in annual restaurant sales.

**Refused angles**

- "Toast revolutionizes restaurant finance with an all-in-one platform" — `slop`.
- "Toast crosses 120,000 restaurant locations" as a standalone milestone story — `no_journalist_shape`. A bare number with no comparator (share of US restaurants? growth rate?) is a topic, not a story; the milestone works as supporting evidence inside the other angles, not as a lead.
- "Toast's data shows where the restaurant industry is heading" — `hallucinated_fact`. Tens of billions processed implies a data vantage, but no dataset, finding, or data product was supplied. Bring a real number and this becomes the strongest angle in the set.
- "Toast responds to fee criticism" defensive-comment — `no_why_now_but_required`. The fee complaints are a standing vulnerability, not a live news signal; pitching into it proactively invites the default-rate question with no answer prepared.

**Uncomfortable questions**

- Why are default rates on Toast Capital advances undisclosed? Every lending reporter will ask within the first two questions, and "we don't share that" turns angles one and two from a growth story into an opacity story.
- Some restaurant owners have complained about Toast's fees. How do you answer "you're now lending money to merchants who already say you charge them too much"? Decide before pitching any finance angle, because a good reporter will put those two facts in the same paragraph.
- Is the bundling actually new — new pricing, new packaging, new product — or a repositioning of things Toast already sold? The "why now" of three angles depends on the answer.
- Can a single restaurant be named — as a borrower, a payroll customer, or both? Without one, the customer-story angle dies and the lending angle limps.
- 120,000 locations compared to what? Get the denominator before anyone pitches the number.
