**Duolingo's paid subscribers can now video-call an AI cartoon to practice speaking**

- **Story type:** trend
- **Why a journalist cares:** A consumer-AI reporter at a tech outlet covering voice agents and AI companions moving from chatbots into paid products. The hook is that a mainstream consumer app is charging for live spoken video conversation with a synthetic character — and has a beta engagement number (9 minutes per session) to anchor the piece. Not for education-trade press focused on classroom outcomes, and not for gaming press.
- **Why now:** The feature is launching now for Max subscribers; the beta engagement figure gives the launch a number, not just a demo.
- **Decay:** 24hr — standard product-launch cycle; the AI-companion trend framing extends it slightly, but the news is the launch.
- **What makes it distinct:** News-values lens (surprise/relevance) plus the ladder zoomed up — the story is consumer AI voice products getting real paid usage, with Duolingo as the evidence. The other angles take the markets, the category expansion, and the critics as protagonists.
- **Proof it needs:** how the 9-minute figure was measured (beta cohort size, time period, whether it counts repeat sessions); which languages the Lily video calls support; a demo or screen recording a reporter can see; whether Duolingo will say anything about Max subscriber counts.
- **Facts it rests on:** paid Max subscribers can have live spoken video conversations with the character Lily for conversation practice; beta engagement averaged 9 minutes per session.

**Duolingo adds chess, testing whether its habit machine works beyond language**

- **Story type:** category-creation
- **Why a journalist cares:** A consumer-apps or platforms reporter covering how subscription apps expand beyond their original category. Chess is the tell: it has nothing to do with language, so the product thesis becomes "Duolingo is an engagement engine for learning anything." The free-for-all-users detail matters — it's a top-of-funnel move, not an upsell. Not for AI reporters (there's no AI in this part of the story) and not for chess hobbyist outlets unless the course content itself is strong.
- **Why now:** The chess course launches now, alongside the AI feature — the contrast between a free non-language course and a paid AI tier is the same company moving in two directions at once.
- **Decay:** week — the launch is a 24-hour event, but the category-expansion thesis supports a longer analysis piece.
- **What makes it distinct:** Ladder lens, zoomed up to the category — the protagonist is Duolingo-as-platform, not the AI feature or the stock. No other kept angle touches chess.
- **Proof it needs:** who built the chess course and how; whether Duolingo frames chess as the first of more non-language subjects or a one-off; any prior non-language courses to establish the pattern (none were supplied — do not assume music or math exist).
- **Facts it rests on:** a chess course launches free for all users; daily active users grew 40% year over year to a new high.

**Profitable and growing 40%, Duolingo bets AI video justifies its priciest tier**

- **Story type:** data
- **Why a journalist cares:** A markets or earnings-beat reporter covering consumer subscription companies, especially ones trying to convert AI features into paid-tier revenue. The numbers carry it: a publicly traded, profitable company with DAUs up 40% year over year to a record, now shipping a flagship AI feature exclusively behind its premium subscription. The open question that beat will chase is whether AI features move Max conversion. Not for product reviewers or education press.
- **Why now:** The DAU figure and the Max-exclusive launch land together — the growth stat is the context, the paywalled AI feature is the monetization test.
- **Decay:** week — the analysis holds until the next earnings or user-number disclosure resets the conversation.
- **What makes it distinct:** Perspective lens, investor/money protagonist — the story is the business model, not the feature or the pedagogy. The 40% figure does the work here; the 9-minute figure does the work in the AI angle.
- **Proof it needs:** the absolute DAU number behind the 40% growth claim and what period it covers; whether Duolingo will disclose Max subscriber counts or conversion rates; Max pricing (not supplied — confirm before any pitch states it).
- **Facts it rests on:** Duolingo is publicly traded and profitable; daily active users grew 40% year over year to a new high; the video-call feature is exclusive to paid Max subscribers.

**Educators call gamified learning shallow. Duolingo's answer is a talking cartoon.**

- **Story type:** defensive-comment
- **Why a journalist cares:** An education reporter covering the learning-science debate around app-based learning. There is real, on-record criticism (educators calling gamification shallow), and Duolingo is now shipping the feature that either answers it — live spoken conversation practice is exactly what critics say apps lack — or escalates it, because the conversation partner is a cartoon character. The reporter gets genuine tension with named stakes on both sides. Not for tech-launch desks or markets reporters; this only works where the pedagogy question is the beat.
- **Why now:** The Lily launch gives the standing gamification critique a fresh, concrete test case.
- **Decay:** week — the debate piece doesn't expire with the launch day, but it needs the launch as its peg.
- **What makes it distinct:** Conflict lens, critics as co-protagonist — the only kept angle where Duolingo plays defense. The trend angle celebrates the feature; this one interrogates it.
- **Proof it needs:** a Duolingo spokesperson willing to engage the criticism directly rather than talk past it; any learning-outcome evidence for conversation practice with Lily — the 9-minute engagement figure is not outcome data and must not be presented as if it were; the specific educator criticism the user can cite (who said it, where).
- **Facts it rests on:** some educators have publicly criticized gamification as shallow learning; paid Max subscribers can have live spoken video conversations with the character Lily for conversation practice.

**Refused angles**

- "Duolingo revolutionizes language learning with AI video" — `slop`.
- "9-minute sessions prove AI conversation practice works" — `hallucinated_fact`: engagement time is not evidence of learning; presenting it that way invites a debunking, not coverage.
- "Duolingo takes on Chess.com" — `hallucinated_fact`: no supplied facts about chess-platform competitors or any competitive intent; counterposition needs a real opponent.
- "Duolingo rides the AI-companion news cycle" — `no_why_now_but_required`: no current external news signal was supplied; a newsjack peg can't be invented. The trend angle above stands on the launch itself instead.
- "What Duolingo's growth means for edtech startups" — `no_journalist_shape`: "edtech observers" is not a beat, and the facts say nothing about other startups.

**Uncomfortable questions**

- What's behind the 9-minute figure — how many beta users, over what period, and does it count novelty-driven first sessions that won't repeat? A reporter will ask; if the answer is weak, the number hurts you.
- What is the absolute DAU number and will Duolingo disclose Max subscriber or conversion figures? Without them, the markets angle is a press release with a percentage in it.
- Can anyone at Duolingo answer the gamification criticism on record with outcome evidence, or only with engagement stats? If only engagement stats, the defensive-comment angle gets written *about* you, not *with* you.
- Which languages does the Lily video call actually support at launch? "Conversation practice" in one language is a different story than ten.
- Is chess a one-off or the first of a roadmap? The category-creation angle is twice as strong if it's a stated strategy.
