**Chipotle says it won't raise prices this year, even as its food costs climb**

- **Story type:** contrarian
- **Why a journalist cares:** A consumer-prices or food-inflation reporter at a business desk or personal-finance outlet tracking menu-price creep. The prevailing story is that food-cost inflation flows straight into menu prices; a 3,500-location chain publicly committing to a year of flat prices is the exception that tests the rule. Not for robotics or restaurant-tech trade press — for them the robot is the story, not the price hold. Not for labor reporters.
- **Why now:** The flat-price commitment was announced alongside the Autocado rollout and is live now; inflation coverage gives it immediate context.
- **Decay:** 24hr — a pricing commitment is standard company news; the announcement window closes fast even if the inflation context lingers.
- **What makes it distinct:** Data lens, the "stasis" variant — no change where readers expect change. The other kept angles are about the machines and the workers; this one is about the receipt.
- **Proof it needs:** the exact terms of the commitment (all items? all markets? any escape hatch if avocado costs spike?); a CFO or spokesperson on record on whether automation savings fund the hold — the fact block pairs the two but never connects them, and a reporter will ask. Labor-cost figures are undisclosed, so do not claim savings pay for it.
- **Facts it rests on:** Chipotle says it will not raise menu prices in the coming year despite food-cost inflation; the chain has over 3,500 locations.

**Chipotle says its robots aren't cutting jobs — they're moving workers to the counter**

- **Story type:** defensive-comment
- **Why a journalist cares:** A labor-and-automation reporter covering frontline restaurant work — workforce trades, labor desks at business outlets. The default automation story is job loss; a company claiming zero cuts and front-of-house redeployment at 100 stores is a testable counter-claim, and the reporter gets to test it. Not for consumer-prices desks or gadget-focused restaurant-tech press, and not for anyone who'd run the company claim unexamined.
- **Why now:** The 100-restaurant rollout makes the redeployment claim checkable now, store by store, instead of hypothetical.
- **Decay:** week — the labor question outlives the announcement day; it stays live as long as the rollout is underway.
- **What makes it distinct:** Perspective lens, worker protagonist. The trend angle is about the machines, the pricing angle about the customer; this one stands or falls on what happens to the people behind the line.
- **Proof it needs:** store-level staffing or scheduled-hours data from pilot locations; at least one named crew member or GM who was redeployed and can speak; what "front line" redeployment concretely means. Right now "no jobs will be cut" is a company assertion, not evidence — without proof this angle hands a skeptical reporter a takedown.
- **Facts it rests on:** no jobs will be cut, the company says, with staff redeployed to the front line; Autocado is rolling out to 100 restaurants; labor-cost figures not disclosed.

**Restaurant robots leave the pilot phase: Chipotle puts Autocado in 100 kitchens**

- **Story type:** trend
- **Why a journalist cares:** A restaurant-operations or kitchen-automation trade reporter who covers deployments, not demos. Food robotics has been stuck in pilot-program purgatory; a 100-store rollout plus a second system (Digital Makeline) in testing is evidence one major chain is moving from experiment to infrastructure — with a concrete ops number (guac prep time roughly halved in pilots) to anchor it. Not for general business desks, consumer press, or labor reporters.
- **Why now:** The rollout announcement is the hook; 100 stores is the pilot-to-deployment threshold this beat watches for.
- **Decay:** week — trend and ops-deployment pieces hold for the news cycle around the rollout, not just the day.
- **What makes it distinct:** Ladder lens, zoomed up — the category (kitchen automation maturing) is the protagonist, with Chipotle as the evidence. The pricing and labor angles each zoom down to one consequence of the same rollout.
- **Proof it needs:** rollout timeline and markets for the 100 stores; how "roughly halves prep time" was measured and across how many pilot kitchens; Digital Makeline's testing status and scope; whether 100 of 3,500+ stores is a committed first wave or a larger pilot.
- **Facts it rests on:** Autocado rolling out to 100 restaurants; Autocado roughly halves guacamole prep time in pilot kitchens; Chipotle is testing the Digital Makeline robotic bowl-assembly system; the chain has over 3,500 locations.

**Refused angles**

- "Chipotle revolutionizes guacamole prep with cutting-edge robotics" — `slop`.
- "While rival chains hike prices, Chipotle holds the line" — `hallucinated_fact`: no competitor pricing facts were supplied.
- "Autocado halves guac prep time" as a standalone data angle — `duplicate`: same lens and protagonist as the deployment-trend angle; the number is that angle's proof, not a second story.
- "Automation savings let Chipotle protect margins" — `hallucinated_fact`: labor-cost figures are explicitly not disclosed, and the fact block never links the robots to the price hold.
- "Robots and the future of fast food" think piece — `no_journalist_shape`: no specific beat, no why-now, evergreen filler.

**Uncomfortable questions**

- Is the price hold actually funded by automation savings, or is pairing the two announcements just choreography? If Chipotle won't connect them on record, the strongest combined narrative doesn't exist — they're two separate, smaller stories.
- Will "no jobs cut" survive a reporter calling pilot stores? If scheduled hours dropped even where headcount didn't, the labor angle inverts into a liability.
- What does "rolling out to 100 restaurants" mean concretely — installed by when, in which markets? Without dates, this is a press-release pilot, and the trend angle weakens.
- Is the flat-price pledge unconditional for the full year, or hedged against a cost spike? The contrarian angle dies if there's fine print.
- Can the prep-time claim be verified beyond "pilot kitchens" — measured how, by whom, across how many stores?
