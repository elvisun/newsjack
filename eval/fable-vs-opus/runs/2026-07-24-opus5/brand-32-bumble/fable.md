**The app that made women message first will now help write the message**

- **Story type:** contrarian
- **Why a journalist cares:** A consumer-tech or internet-culture reporter covering dating apps and how AI is changing online intimacy. The prevailing belief is live in the facts: some users already call AI in dating inauthentic. Bumble's entire brand is a single authentic human gesture — the woman sends the first message — and the relaunch puts an AI assistant inside that exact gesture. The tension between the company's stated core model and its new feature is the story. Not for markets desks, enterprise-AI reporters, or general business columnists who don't cover consumer dating products.
- **Why now:** The relaunch is the live event, and the user criticism of AI-assisted dating gives the tension a current voice.
- **Decay:** week — the relaunch is the peg, but the authenticity question stays reportable while the redesign rolls out.
- **What makes it distinct:** Inversion lens with the user as protagonist. The other kept angles treat the founder and the public markets as protagonists; this one is about what the product change does to the app's defining ritual.
- **Proof it needs:** a clear answer to whether the AI helps write the woman's first message specifically; a Bumble spokesperson on record about how AI assistance squares with women-message-first; examples or screenshots of what the AI suggestions actually look like; specifics on the user criticism (where, who, how widespread) rather than "some users."
- **Facts it rests on:** the AI feature helps users write opening messages; the company says the women-message-first model remains core; some users have criticized AI in dating as inauthentic.

**A returning founder stakes Bumble's comeback on AI matchmaking**

- **Story type:** founder-profile
- **Why a journalist cares:** A reporter at a business outlet or tech-business newsletter who covers founder returns and turnaround leadership — the boomerang-CEO beat. The shape is specific: founder steps back, paying users decline, stock falls, founder returns this year and answers with a full relaunch built on AI. The person and the decision are the story, not the feature list. Not for product-review outlets, dating-advice press, or AI-research reporters.
- **Why now:** The relaunch is the founder's first big public move since returning as CEO this year.
- **Decay:** 24hr — the relaunch is standard company news; the profile loses its peg once the announcement cycle passes, unless the founder gives fresh access.
- **What makes it distinct:** Perspective lens, founder protagonist. The contrarian angle is about users and authenticity; the markets angle is about investors. This one is about why the founder came back and why this is the bet.
- **Proof it needs:** the founder on record about the decision to return and the reasoning behind the AI relaunch; the timeline of the step-back and return; access or quotes — a founder profile without the founder is a press release.
- **Facts it rests on:** the founder returned as CEO this year after stepping back; the relaunch follows declining paying users and a falling stock price; the relaunch pairs AI-assisted matchmaking with a redesigned app.

**Bumble's AI relaunch is a turnaround bet with no numbers yet**

- **Story type:** funding-mechanics
- **Why a journalist cares:** A markets or public-company tech reporter covering consumer-internet stocks and turnaround stories. Bumble is publicly traded, paying users were declining, the stock fell, and the company's answer is an AI relaunch whose paying-user impact is — by the company's own position — not yet reported. The honest lead is the gap: investors are being asked to price a turnaround before the one metric that proves it exists. Not for lifestyle or dating-culture press, and not for product reporters who don't follow the stock.
- **Why now:** The relaunch is live and the market is pricing it before any post-relaunch paying-user data exists.
- **Decay:** week — the announcement is a 24-hour event, but the wait-for-the-number framing holds until the company reports post-relaunch figures.
- **What makes it distinct:** News-values lens (magnitude plus bad-news/good-news) with the investor as protagonist. It is the only angle whose central fact is the missing metric rather than the product or the founder.
- **Proof it needs:** the actual paying-user and stock-decline figures with dates (the facts say declining and falling, not by how much); when Bumble will next report paying-user numbers; any company guidance tied to the relaunch.
- **Facts it rests on:** Bumble is publicly traded; the relaunch follows a period of declining paying users and a falling stock price; the paying-user trend after the relaunch is not yet reported.

**Refused angles**

- "Dating apps are all turning to AI — Bumble leads the trend" — `hallucinated_fact`. No facts about any other dating app were supplied; a category-wide trend claim can't rest on one company's relaunch.
- "Bumble user finds love through AI matchmaking" — `hallucinated_fact`. No named users, no outcomes, no usage data exist in the facts. A customer-story needs a customer.
- "Bumble's defensive response to the AI-inauthenticity backlash" — `duplicate`. Same lens, same protagonist, same tension as the kept contrarian angle; the contrarian framing is sharper because it names what's at stake (the first-message ritual).
- "Bumble unveils revolutionary AI-powered dating experience" — `slop`. Press-release headline; rewriting it produced the founder and markets angles, which are kept instead.
- "The redesigned app: what's new in Bumble's interface" — `no_journalist_shape`. A redesign with no specifics is a changelog, not a story; no beat covers "new UI" on its own.

**Uncomfortable questions**

- Does the AI help write the *woman's first message*? If yes, every reporter will ask how that coexists with women-message-first, and you need an answer better than "it remains core." If you can't answer this, the strongest angle becomes the most dangerous one.
- Is the founder-CEO available on record? Two of the three angles are thin without direct quotes, and the founder angle is dead without them.
- What are the actual numbers — how far did paying users decline, how far did the stock fall, and when will post-relaunch figures be reported? "Declining" and "falling" won't survive a markets reporter's first follow-up.
- How widespread and specific is the user criticism of AI-as-inauthentic? "Some users" is not evidence a reporter can cite — and if the criticism is loud, pitching the AI feature without addressing it head-on invites the backlash story instead.

**Next step:** `journalist-fit-check` — three viable shapes exist; pick the angle you can actually prove (the founder's availability and the willingness to answer the first-message question decide which one), then resolve real names against it.
