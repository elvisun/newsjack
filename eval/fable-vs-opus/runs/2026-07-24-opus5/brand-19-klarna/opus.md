**Klarna's AI now does the work of 700 agents — and its headcount fell 20%**

- **Story type:** data
- **Why a journalist cares:** A future-of-work or labor-economics reporter at a business outlet or workforce/HR trade publication covering AI's measurable effect on jobs. The hook is the rare pairing of a concrete automation claim (700 full-time agents' worth of work) against a real headcount fall (~20% over the year) at a company that's about to be public — a documented case, not a forecast. Not for general consumer fintech press, BNPL product reviewers, or AI-hype newsletters.
- **Why now:** The headcount number and the IPO filing make this a live "is AI actually cutting jobs" story while the broader debate is unresolved; the company's reticence about cut-versus-unfilled sharpens it.
- **Decay:** week — this is a data/context piece, not a same-day event; the IPO filing keeps it warm but there's no breaking signal supplied.
- **What makes it distinct:** Data lens (scale + change over time) — the protagonist is the workforce number. Different from the operations angle below (which is about resolution time and dispute handling) and from the IPO angle (which is about investor disclosure).
- **Proof it needs:** how Klarna calculated the "700 full-time agents" equivalent (methodology, time period); the actual roles cut versus left unfilled — which Klarna currently won't disclose, so this gap must be acknowledged in any pitch; whether the 20% drop is net of any new hires; an on-record source willing to address the attrition split.
- **Facts it rests on:** the assistant handled the equivalent work of 700 full-time agents in its first months; Klarna froze most hiring and headcount fell about 20% over the year, partly through attrition; the company will not say how many roles were cut versus left unfilled; Klarna has filed to go public.
