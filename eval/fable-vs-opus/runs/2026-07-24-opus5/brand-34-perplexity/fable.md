**Perplexity offers publishers a revenue cut after months of legal threats over its content use**

- **Story type:** counterposition
- **Why a journalist cares:** A media-business reporter covering publisher economics and the AI-content fight — the kind who has already written about publishers' criticism and legal threats against Perplexity. This is the next chapter of a story that desk is actively telling: the company accused of taking publisher content is now proposing to pay for it, and the payout amounts are undisclosed. The undisclosed terms are the reporting hook, not a footnote. Not for consumer-shopping press, gadget reviewers, or general startup desks.
- **Why now:** The publisher program is launching now, and it lands directly on top of live criticism and legal threats — a follow-up with built-in tension.
- **Decay:** 24hr — launch-day news; the publisher-fight context gives a short tail, but the announcement peg is gone within a day.
- **What makes it distinct:** Perspective lens with the publisher as protagonist, sharpened by the conflict news value. The other angles treat the launch as a business-model or product story; this one treats it as a move in an existing fight.
- **Proof it needs:** at least one publisher enrolled in the program and willing to speak; the actual revenue-share terms or a credible reason they're withheld; whether any of the publishers who threatened legal action are eligible for or party to the program.
- **Facts it rests on:** the publisher program shares ad revenue when an article is used in an answer; Perplexity has faced criticism and legal threats from publishers over content use; payout amounts not disclosed.

**An answer engine becomes a checkout: Perplexity bets AI search can monetize like an affiliate**

- **Story type:** category-creation
- **Why a journalist cares:** A commerce or retail-tech reporter at a business outlet or e-commerce trade publication covering where online shopping journeys start and who takes the fee. The story is the model, not the feature: research-to-purchase inside one AI app, with the platform earning a referral fee on the transaction. That beat can test whether AI answer products are becoming shopping front doors. Not for media-business reporters (that's the first angle) and not for AI-research press.
- **Why now:** The in-app shopping launch is the peg; the category question of how AI products make money has a longer runway.
- **Decay:** week — the launch itself is a 24-hour event, but the monetization-model thesis supports a week of trend coverage.
- **What makes it distinct:** Ladder lens, zoomed up — the category and the business model are the protagonist, not the publisher fight or the valuation.
- **Proof it needs:** which merchants and product categories are live at launch; the referral-fee structure or range; any early transaction or usage data — without at least one of these, the angle is an announcement, not a story.
- **Facts it rests on:** users can research and buy products without leaving the app; Perplexity earns a referral fee; the company says it handles hundreds of millions of queries a month.

**Can you trust shopping advice from an AI that gets paid when you buy?**

- **Story type:** contrarian
- **Why a journalist cares:** A consumer-tech or tech-accountability reporter covering platform incentives and recommendation integrity. The prevailing assumption is that an answer engine's job is neutral research; the new fact cuts against it — the same system that recommends a product now earns a referral fee on the purchase. That's a real incentive-conflict question a skeptical reporter can put directly to the company. Not for trade commerce press cheering the launch, and not for media-business desks.
- **Why now:** The incentive conflict is born the day in-app shopping launches; the question is freshest while the launch is news.
- **Decay:** week — the question outlives the launch day, but goes stale once the first wave of skeptical coverage runs.
- **What makes it distinct:** Inversion lens, user as protagonist. The category angle asks whether the model works; this asks whether users should want it to.
- **Proof it needs:** Perplexity's on-record answer on whether referral fees influence ranking or recommendations; any disclosure shown to users at purchase; a company spokesperson willing to take the question. This angle is only pitchable by Perplexity itself as a transparency move — if the company won't engage the conflict question head-on, drop it before a reporter raises it anyway.
- **Facts it rests on:** users can research and buy without leaving the app; Perplexity earns a referral fee on purchases.

**Referral fees and ad-revenue splits: how a multi-billion-dollar AI company plans to pay for itself**

- **Story type:** funding-mechanics
- **Why a journalist cares:** A VC or markets reporter covering AI business models and whether private AI valuations are backed by real revenue mechanics. The hook is that both launches answer the same investor question: a privately held company that recently raised at a multi-billion valuation is now showing its monetization hand — referral commerce on one side, ad-revenue sharing on the other. Not for product reviewers or publisher-beat reporters.
- **Why now:** The two launches arriving together is the peg; the recent multi-billion raise makes the monetization question current rather than academic.
- **Decay:** week — investor-thesis stories can run past the launch day if there's substance behind the model.
- **What makes it distinct:** Perspective lens, the money as protagonist — the unit economics are the story, not the publisher conflict, the category, or the user.
- **Proof it needs:** any revenue figure, take-rate, or margin the company will state; what share of queries are commercial-intent; an executive willing to discuss the monetization path on record. Without at least one number, this collapses into speculation.
- **Facts it rests on:** Perplexity earns a referral fee on in-app purchases; the publisher program shares ad revenue; privately held, recently raised at a multi-billion valuation; the company says it handles hundreds of millions of queries a month.

**Refused angles**

- "Perplexity's hundreds of millions of monthly queries prove AI search has arrived" — `no_journalist_shape`. A bare, self-reported, uncontextualized number with no comparison point is a topic, not a story, and there's no specific beat it lands on.
- "Perplexity ends the war with publishers" — `hallucinated_fact`. Nothing in the facts says any publisher has accepted, enrolled, or dropped a legal threat; payout amounts aren't even disclosed.
- "Perplexity revolutionizes online shopping with seamless AI" — `slop`. Press-release framing; failed the one-rewrite test because there's no product detail to rewrite it around.
- "The retail-industry angle on Perplexity's shopping launch" — `duplicate`. Same lens and protagonist as the category-creation angle; it's the same story addressed to a different inbox.

**Uncomfortable questions**

- Is a single publisher actually enrolled and willing to say so on record? Without one, the strongest angle here reads as a counter-PR move against the legal threats — and reporters on that beat will frame it exactly that way.
- Why are payout amounts undisclosed? That's the first question every media-business reporter will ask; "we can't say" turns the publisher angle from a peace offering into a stunt.
- Are the publishers who threatened legal action eligible for the program, or excluded from it?
- Which merchants are live in the shopping launch, and what is the referral-fee rate? A commerce launch with no named merchant and no fee structure is an announcement, not a story.
- Will Perplexity answer, on record, whether referral fees influence what its answers recommend? If the answer is no comment, the incentive-conflict angle belongs to a critic, not to you.
