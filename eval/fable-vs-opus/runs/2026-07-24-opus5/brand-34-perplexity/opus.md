**Perplexity starts paying the publishers it's been fighting — when their articles fuel an answer**

- **Story type:** counterposition
- **Why a journalist cares:** A media-business reporter at a press/publishing trade outlet (the desk that covers publisher economics, licensing fights, and platform-vs-publisher tension) who has been tracking AI companies' content disputes. The hook is the reversal: a company that has faced legal threats from publishers is now offering those same publishers a revenue cut. That reporter can test whether this is a genuine peace offering or a way to defuse litigation. Not for consumer-tech reviewers, gadget desks, or general startup press.
- **Why now:** The revenue-sharing program launches now, against a standing backdrop of publisher criticism and legal threats; the contrast is what makes it a today story rather than evergreen.
- **Decay:** 24hr — a launch-driven news event; the publisher-economics debate has a longer tail but the "they're paying now" peg is a one-day move.
- **What makes it distinct:** Perspective lens, publisher/incumbent protagonist — the story belongs to the aggrieved publishers, not to Perplexity. Distinct from the shopping angle (different fact) and the valuation angle (different protagonist).
- **Proof it needs:** at least one named publisher enrolled in the program; the actual payout mechanism and a real (even rough) dollar figure, since amounts are undisclosed; confirmation of which publishers had threatened legal action and whether any are participating; an on-record line on whether this resolves any active dispute.
- **Facts it rests on:** the publisher program shares ad revenue when a publisher's article is used in an answer; the company has faced criticism and legal threats from publishers over content use; payout amounts not disclosed.

**Perplexity turns its answer engine into a checkout: research-and-buy without leaving the app**

- **Story type:** category-creation
- **Why a journalist cares:** A commerce/retail-tech trade reporter covering how shopping behavior moves into new surfaces (the desk that tracks marketplaces, conversational commerce, and referral economics). The hook is the surface, not the company: a search-style answer tool becoming a place where purchases close, with the platform taking a referral fee. That reporter can examine whether buying inside an answer engine is a real channel or a feature bolt-on. Not for the media-business desk (that's the publisher story) or general AI-hype coverage.
- **Why now:** In-app shopping launches now; the "where does commerce happen next" question is live in retail coverage.
- **Decay:** 24hr — launch news; the conversational-commerce trend supports a follow-up but the launch peg is same-day.
- **What makes it distinct:** Ladder lens zoomed up — treats the buy-inside-the-answer behavior as a category shift, with a different protagonist (the shopping surface) and a different beat (retail/commerce) than the publisher angle.
- **Proof it needs:** which merchants or product categories are live at launch; the referral-fee structure (percentage or flat); any early data on completed purchases or query-to-purchase conversion; whether checkout is native or hands off to a retailer.
- **Facts it rests on:** users can research and buy products without leaving the app; Perplexity earns a referral fee.

**One AI app, two new ways to take a cut: referral fees from shoppers, ad revenue to publishers**

- **Story type:** trend
- **Why a journalist cares:** A business reporter covering AI company business models and the search-to-revenue question (the desk asking how AI answer tools actually make money, beyond subscriptions). The hook is that Perplexity is wiring up two monetization paths at once — taking a referral fee on the buy side and sharing ad revenue on the supply side — which is a more concrete answer to "how do these things pay for themselves" than most. Not for the pure commerce desk or the pure media desk; this is the model-level synthesis.
- **Why now:** Both mechanisms are being announced together today, which is what makes the "business model" framing a now story rather than analysis.
- **Decay:** week — a model/strategy read can run for several days past the launch if the mechanics hold up.
- **What makes it distinct:** Perspective lens, the money/business protagonist — it sits above both other angles and asks what the two launches say about the company's revenue strategy, rather than covering either feature on its own.
- **Proof it needs:** the referral-fee economics and the publisher revenue-share economics side by side; whether either is expected to be material against subscription revenue; query volume that actually converts to revenue (the "hundreds of millions of queries a month" figure needs a revenue bridge, not just a headline number).
- **Facts it rests on:** Perplexity earns a referral fee on purchases; the publisher program shares ad revenue; the company says it handles hundreds of millions of queries a month; it is privately held and recently raised at a multi-billion valuation.

**Refused angles**

- "Perplexity revolutionizes shopping and publishing with a seamless new platform" — `slop`.
- "Perplexity's hundreds of millions of monthly queries make it a search powerhouse" — `hallucinated_fact` (the query figure is a company-stated volume claim with no verification and no revenue or market-share context; using it as a magnitude story would assert a conclusion the facts don't support).
- "What Perplexity's multi-billion valuation says about the AI bubble" — `hallucinated_fact` (no funding amount, investor, round stage, or market thesis was supplied; this would be invented financial commentary).
- "Perplexity founder profile: the operator behind the answer engine" — `no_journalist_shape` (no founder name, background, or biographical fact was supplied — there is no protagonist to build on).
- "Perplexity disrupts Google with in-app shopping" — `hallucinated_fact` (no competitor, comparison, or incumbent response is in the facts; the Google framing is invented).
- "The publisher-payout angle: how much will publishers actually earn?" — `no_why_now_but_required` and a duplicate of the publisher counterposition angle; payout amounts are explicitly undisclosed, so there is no story until a real number exists — it belongs in proof-it-needs, not as its own angle.

**Uncomfortable questions**

- Payout amounts are undisclosed. Until you can show a publisher a real number — or name one publisher who has signed on — the "we pay publishers now" angle reads as a press-release promise, and a media-business reporter will say so.
- Are any of the publishers who criticized or threatened legal action actually enrolled in the revenue-share program? If not, the peace-offering framing collapses, and the story may instead be "Perplexity offers a deal its loudest critics are ignoring."
- "Hundreds of millions of queries a month" is a company-stated figure. Is it verifiable, and does any of it convert to revenue? A bare volume number with no revenue bridge is not a magnitude story; a reporter will treat it as marketing.
- For the shopping launch: which merchants are live, and is checkout actually native or a redirect? "Buy without leaving the app" is the whole claim — if it hands off to a retailer at the last step, the angle weakens.
- Do you have standing to discuss the litigation backdrop on record? The publisher angle is the strongest, but it sits directly on top of legal threats — confirm with counsel what you can say before pitching it.

**Next step:** `journalist-fit-check` — three structurally distinct, defensible shapes exist (publisher media-business, retail/commerce, AI business-model). Resolve real names once you pick one. If you want the publisher angle to land hardest, first secure one named, enrolled publisher and a real payout figure — without them, the counterposition is a promise, not a story.
