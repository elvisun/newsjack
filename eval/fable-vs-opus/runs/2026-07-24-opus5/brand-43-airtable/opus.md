**Airtable is shipping agents that act on enterprise data — and won't say how often they get it right**

- **Story type:** contrarian
- **Why a journalist cares:** An enterprise-software or AI-reliability reporter at a B2B tech trade outlet or an enterprise-IT newsletter who covers what happens when agents move from suggesting to *doing*. The prevailing belief is that 2025-26's selling point for enterprise AI is autonomy — agents that take actions, not just draft text. Airtable cuts against that confidence: it's launching agents that update statuses and draft emails on live company records, but agent-reliability data is not disclosed. That gap is the story for a reporter who keeps asking vendors "what's your error rate?" Not for product-review blogs, no-code tutorial sites, or general startup press.
- **Why now:** The repositioning and agent launch are live now; the reliability question is sharpest while the messaging is fresh and unchallenged.
- **Decay:** 24hr — this is launch-pegged. The "no disclosed reliability" hook is strongest in the first news cycle; after that it's an evergreen enterprise-AI-governance piece without the Airtable peg.
- **What makes it distinct:** Inversion lens. The prevailing belief is "action-taking agents are ready for enterprise data"; the evidence against is Airtable's own undisclosed reliability numbers on agents that mutate records. The other angles are about competitive position and valuation, not the trust gap.
- **Proof it needs:** an on-record answer from Airtable on agent error/accuracy rates, what a wrong action looks like (a mis-updated status, a bad draft sent), and what guardrails or human-in-the-loop controls ship by default. Without at least the question being put on record, this is a critique, not a reported story.
- **Facts it rests on:** agents read records and take actions like updating statuses or drafting emails; agent-reliability data is not disclosed; the company serves many enterprise teams.
