**REI members can now trade in gear and get repairs at any of its 180+ stores**

- **Story type:** trend
- **Why a journalist cares:** A retail-operations reporter at an outdoor-industry or retail trade outlet covering resale and circular-economy programs at physical chains. The story is the operational shift, not the announcement: taking trade-in and repair from online-plus-select-stores to 180+ locations means every store needs intake space, grading standards, technicians, and inventory handling. That reporter can ask what the fleet-wide rollout actually costs and how it changes store labor. Not for consumer lifestyle desks, gear-review outlets, or personal-finance columnists.
- **Why now:** The expansion is being announced now, alongside the dividend.
- **Decay:** 24hr — standard company-news cycle; the operational reporting can run later if REI gives access to a store.
- **What makes it distinct:** Perspective lens with the category/operations as protagonist. It is about the retail mechanics of scaling resale, not the co-op's finances (dividend angle) or its labor situation (union angle).
- **Proof it needs:** how many stores ran the program before versus the 180+ now; how repairs are staffed — in-store technicians, regional hubs, or third parties; typical turnaround time; what categories are accepted and what gets rejected; a store manager or program lead on record.
- **Facts it rests on:** members can trade used gear for store credit and get repairs at any of REI's 180+ stores; the program previously ran online and in select stores.

**A member-owned retailer just paid out its biggest dividend while pushing customers to buy used**

- **Story type:** counterposition
- **Why a journalist cares:** A retail-business or consumer-companies reporter at a business outlet who covers how ownership structure shapes strategy. The tension is the hook: a public retailer answering to shareholders has a hard time cheering on resale, which cannibalizes new-product margin. REI has no shareholders and just returned a record dividend to members instead. That's a live comparison to how listed outdoor and apparel brands treat resale. Not for sustainability newsletters that will just restate the press release, and not for local news.
- **Why now:** The record dividend and the fleet-wide expansion landed together, which is what makes the ownership argument concrete rather than theoretical.
- **Decay:** week — the ownership-structure argument holds past the announcement day if REI will discuss the economics.
- **What makes it distinct:** Contrarian/inversion lens. The prevailing belief is that resale is a margin problem retailers tolerate for image; the counterposition is that a co-op's structure changes that math. Different protagonist (the ownership model) and different altitude from the operations angle.
- **Proof it needs:** the actual dividend figure and how it compares with prior years; whether REI will state on record that co-op structure lets it accept resale cannibalization; any margin or revenue effect it will discuss. Note honestly: REI has not disclosed the share of sales from used gear, which is the number this angle most wants.
- **Facts it rests on:** the co-op issued its largest-ever annual member dividend this year; REI is a member-owned cooperative, not publicly traded; the trade-in and repair program is expanding to all stores; the share of sales from used gear is not disclosed.

**REI won't say how much of its business is used gear**

- **Story type:** data
- **Why a journalist cares:** A retail-data or sustainability-accountability reporter at a business or trade outlet who covers whether corporate circularity claims are backed by numbers. REI says resale and repair support its sustainability commitments but does not disclose the share of sales from used gear — so there is no way to size the claim. That absence is the story for a reporter who has been chasing the same non-disclosure across apparel and outdoor brands. Not for outlets that will run the sustainability framing uncritically, and not for gear reviewers.
- **Why now:** The claim is being made now, at fleet-wide scale, which is what makes the missing denominator newly relevant.
- **Decay:** week — accountability reporting doesn't expire with the announcement, but it's strongest while the program is being publicized.
- **What makes it distinct:** Data lens, specifically the absence-of-data variant — the story is what the number would show, not what the program does. Structurally opposite to the two angles above, which take the program at face value.
- **Proof it needs:** whether REI will disclose used-gear revenue share, unit volume, or repair counts, even directionally; the specific sustainability commitments the program is said to support and whether they carry measurable targets; how REI counts a resold item against those commitments. If REI won't supply any of this, be aware you are pitching a story that is partly critical of the client.
- **Facts it rests on:** REI says resale and repair support its sustainability commitments; the share of sales from used gear is not disclosed.

**Trade-in counters and repair benches arrive at REI stores as workers organize**

- **Story type:** trend
- **Why a journalist cares:** A labor reporter at a business or retail-trade outlet covering organizing in retail chains. Repair and trade-in are not shelf-stocking: they add skilled tasks, grading judgment, and customer-facing intake work across 180+ stores, at a chain that has seen unionization activity at several stores. A labor reporter can ask whether new job categories, training, or pay bands come with the rollout, and how organized stores are affected. Not for sustainability or gear press, and not a fit for anyone looking for a feel-good circular-economy story.
- **Why now:** The fleet-wide rollout is happening now against an existing organizing backdrop.
- **Decay:** week — labor coverage runs on its own clock, not the announcement's.
- **What makes it distinct:** Perspective lens with the store worker as protagonist — the only angle where the co-op is not the hero. Different beat, different risk profile, and different questions than the operations or dividend angles.
- **Proof it needs:** whether the rollout creates new roles or adds duties to existing ones; training provided; any pay differential for repair technicians; how many stores have organizing activity and what REI's stated position is; whether REI will let a spokesperson answer labor questions at all. Be direct with yourself: this angle invites coverage the company may not want.
- **Facts it rests on:** the program is expanding to all 180+ stores with in-store repairs; the co-op has faced unionization activity at several stores.

**Refused angles**

- "REI revolutionizes the circular economy for outdoor gear" — `slop`. Press-release headline, undefended superlative, no story under it.
- "REI's record dividend proves the co-op model beats public markets" — `hallucinated_fact`. The facts give one dividend, described as largest-ever, with no figure, no comparison, and no evidence about public-market retailers.
- "Resale is the future of outdoor retail" — `no_journalist_shape`. A theme, not a story; no protagonist, no beat, nothing a reporter can report out.
- "REI members save money by trading in gear" — `no_journalist_shape`. Consumer service copy, not news; no data on savings was supplied.
- "The sustainability angle: REI's repair program cuts waste" — `hallucinated_fact`. No waste-diversion figure, no lifecycle data, nothing to measure the claim against.
- "Why REI's expansion pressures Patagonia and other resale programs" — `hallucinated_fact`. No competitor was named or described in the facts; the comparison would be invented.
- "The retail angle on REI's trade-in expansion" — `duplicate`. Same lens and protagonist as the operations angle, different envelope.

**Uncomfortable questions**

- What is the dividend number? "Largest-ever" without a figure is not reportable, and a business reporter will ask in the first reply. If REI won't release it, the dividend angle thins out badly.
- Will REI disclose the used-gear share of sales, even as a range? Refusing it is defensible, but you should pitch knowing the first question you get is the one you can't answer — and that a good reporter may write about the refusal itself.
- Does the co-op want labor questions in its coverage? The union backdrop is in your own fact set. Pitching a store-level operations story to a business desk is an open invitation for a labor reporter to pick it up. Decide now whether that's acceptable, not after the call.
- What does "all stores" actually mean for repairs? Full-service benches everywhere, or intake counters that ship gear to a regional hub? A reporter will visit a store, and if the answer is "intake only," the headline claim breaks.
- What specific sustainability commitments does this support, and do they have numeric targets and dates? "Supports our commitments" is not a claim that survives a fact-check.
- No current news signal was supplied with this update, so nothing here is anchored to a breaking story. Every decay tag above reflects only the company's own announcement clock.
