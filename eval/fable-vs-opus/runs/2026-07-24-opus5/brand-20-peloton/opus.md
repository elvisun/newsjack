**Peloton posts its first profitable quarter in three years — on adjusted EBITDA, not net income**

- **Story type:** data
- **Why a journalist cares:** A consumer-discretionary or fitness-industry markets reporter at a business outlet who covers earnings and follows the connected-fitness comeback story. The hook is the qualified turn: positive adjusted EBITDA and free cash flow for the quarter, against a company that is *still* unprofitable on a net basis and carrying significant debt. That gap between the headline number and the bottom line is exactly what an earnings-literate reporter pulls apart. Not for fitness lifestyle press, gear reviewers, or consumer how-to desks.
- **Why now:** The quarter was just reported; turnaround-quarter framing has a short window before the number is old news.
- **Decay:** 4hr — this is a same-day earnings read; the "adjusted vs. net" tension is most pitchable while the print is fresh.
- **What makes it distinct:** Data lens (change over time) plus the good-news/bad-news news value — the same fact is simultaneously the recovery story and the "not out of the woods" story. Distinct from the rental and churn angles, which are operational, not financial.
- **Proof it needs:** the actual adjusted EBITDA and free cash flow figures for the quarter; the net loss figure and total debt load; the comparable quarter three years ago to anchor "first in three years"; a CFO or IR source who can speak to the path from adjusted EBITDA to net profitability.
- **Facts it rests on:** first profitable quarter in three years; positive adjusted EBITDA and free cash flow for the quarter; remains unprofitable on a net basis and carries significant debt.

**Peloton is selling bikes by the month now — the hardware company is becoming a subscription company**

- **Story type:** trend
- **Why a journalist cares:** A consumer-business or retail-strategy reporter at a business outlet covering how durable-goods brands shift to access-over-ownership models. The story isn't "Peloton has a rental program" — it's that a company that built itself on a $1,000+ hardware sale is nationally expanding a bundle-a-bike-and-membership-for-one-fee, no-commitment model, which reframes the whole unit economics. This reporter covers the access-economy shift across categories. Not for fitness-product reviewers or local retail desks.
- **Why now:** The nationwide expansion is the timely event; the broader access-model trend has a longer tail.
- **Decay:** 24hr — the expansion announcement drives the immediate hook; the trend framing can run for a week if the model shift is documented.
- **What makes it distinct:** Ladder lens, zoomed up — makes the *business-model shift* the story, not the quarterly number. Different protagonist (the company's model) than the earnings angle (the financials) and the churn angle (the subscriber).
- **Proof it needs:** the rental price point and what the bundle includes; how many members are on the rental program versus buying outright; what the program does to per-member margin and lifetime value; whether the no-commitment structure changes how revenue is recognized.
- **Facts it rests on:** the rental program is expanding nationwide; it bundles a bike and membership for one monthly fee with no commitment.

**Peloton's subscriber churn just hit a record low — the retention problem may be the one that's actually fixed**

- **Story type:** data
- **Why a journalist cares:** A subscription-economy or consumer-business reporter who covers retention metrics as the real health signal for membership businesses. Churn is the number that decides whether a connected-fitness company survives, and "record low" is a contextualized data point a reporter can interrogate. The cut-against-expectations element — that the once-doubted retention story is improving — is the value here. Not for hardware reviewers or general tech press.
- **Why now:** Reported alongside this quarter; the retention milestone is freshest now.
- **Decay:** 24hr — tied to the quarterly reporting cycle; the retention-as-survival-metric framing can extend to a week.
- **What makes it distinct:** Data lens (outlier / record value) with the subscriber as protagonist — distinct from the EBITDA angle (company financials) and the rental angle (business model). Same quarter, different number, different "so what."
- **Proof it needs:** the actual churn rate and what "record low" is measured against; the prior peak churn for contrast; whether the rental program's members are included in the churn calculation, since a no-commitment bundle could flatter or distort the number; total subscriber base.
- **Facts it rests on:** subscriber churn fell to a record low; total members exceed 6 million.

**The new CEO cut $200M a year — what got cut, and what it cost**

- **Story type:** exec-spotlight
- **Why a journalist cares:** A management or corporate-turnaround reporter at a business outlet who covers operator-led restructurings and the "new CEO comes in and cuts" playbook. The hook is the executive and the operating decision: over $200M in annual costs removed, with the company now showing positive adjusted EBITDA. The reporter wants the mechanics — what was cut and whether the profitability followed the cuts or the demand. Not for fitness press or product desks.
- **Why now:** The cost program is credited in this quarter's turn, which makes the CEO's playbook timely to examine now.
- **Decay:** 24hr — the spotlight rides the earnings moment; a deeper management profile is closer to a month.
- **What makes it distinct:** Perspective lens, operator protagonist — the person and the operating choices are the story, not the financial print or the subscriber metric.
- **Proof it needs:** the CEO's name, start date, and prior background; the breakdown of the $200M (headcount, marketing, logistics, real estate?); whether the cuts are one-time or structural; an on-record quote from the CEO or a credible account of the decisions; any tradeoff — product, service, or staffing — that the cuts created.
- **Facts it rests on:** Peloton cut over $200M in annual costs under a new CEO; positive adjusted EBITDA and free cash flow for the quarter.

**Refused angles**

- "Peloton's revolutionary rental program transforms connected fitness" — `slop`.
- "Peloton is back and stronger than ever" — `slop` (and unsupported: the company is still net-unprofitable with significant debt, so "stronger than ever" fails the fact check).
- "Why the fitness industry is rebounding" — `no_journalist_shape` (no protagonist, no beat, and no supplied fact about the wider industry — this is a topic, not a story).
- "Peloton proves the connected-fitness skeptics were wrong" — `hallucinated_fact` (a contrarian angle needs a named, widely-held belief *and* evidence against it; the facts here show a partial, adjusted-EBITDA turn while net losses and debt remain, which does not cleanly refute the skeptics — pitching it as vindication would overstate the facts).
- "Peloton's 6 million members make it a fitness powerhouse" — `duplicate` (the 6M figure has no standalone "so what" beyond context for the churn angle, where it already lives).

**Uncomfortable questions**

- The headline turn is on *adjusted* EBITDA while the company is still net-unprofitable and carries significant debt. Are you comfortable leading with "profitable," knowing a sharp reporter will lead their second paragraph with the net loss? If not, the data angle is the honest frame and the others should follow it.
- What is the rental program's effect on the churn number? A no-commitment bundle could make retention look better or worse depending on how those members are counted. Until you know, the "record low churn" claim is exposed.
- Can the new CEO, the CFO, or IR actually speak on record? Three of these four angles (exec-spotlight, EBITDA mechanics, churn definition) need a named human source, not just the release.
- "Record low churn" against what baseline, and "first profitable quarter in three years" against which prior quarter? Both claims are only as strong as the comparison you can document.
- Is the nationwide rental expansion already live, or announced-and-rolling-out? The trend angle's "why now" depends on which.

**Next step:** `journalist-fit-check` — four structurally distinct shapes exist (financial, business-model, retention, executive). Pick the one you can support with the most proof and a named source, then resolve a real journalist against it. If you want to lead with the turnaround number, line up the net-loss and debt facts first or `meanest-editor` will rightly tear the "profitable" framing apart.
