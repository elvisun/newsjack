**Mindbody says texting the waitlist cut studio no-shows 22% in a pilot**

- **Story type:** data
- **Why a journalist cares:** A reporter at a fitness- or wellness-business trade outlet covering studio operations and unit economics. No-shows are direct revenue loss for boutique studios, and a vendor putting a number on automated waitlist fills gives this beat something measurable to interrogate. Not for consumer fitness or lifestyle press, and not for general AI desks, who will see a feature announcement, not a story.
- **Why now:** The assistant is launching now; the pilot number is the data hook attached to the launch.
- **Decay:** 24hr — this is standard launch-day company news; the stat loses its peg once the launch cycle passes.
- **What makes it distinct:** Data lens, the change metric. The pilot number is the protagonist. The other kept angles zoom up to the market, over to the pricing decision, or down to a single studio.
- **Proof it needs:** pilot methodology before pitching — sample size, duration, baseline no-show rate, and whether 22% is a relative reduction or a percentage-point drop; confirmation Mindbody will share that detail with a reporter.
- **Facts it rests on:** the assistant fills last-minute cancellations by texting waitlisted members; the company says no-show rates dropped 22% in a pilot; Mindbody powers booking for over 50,000 studios and salons.

**The software behind 50,000 studios wants AI to solve fitness's empty-slot problem**

- **Story type:** trend
- **Why a journalist cares:** A reporter covering vertical SaaS or small-business technology at a business-tech outlet. The story is distribution: most boutique studios will never buy an AI tool, but they may get one bundled into booking software they already pay for. An empty class slot is perishable inventory, and this is yield-management thinking arriving at Main Street wellness businesses through an incumbent platform. Not for fitness trade press chasing the launch itself, and not for consumer outlets.
- **Why now:** The launch is the occasion, but the angle holds as long as the rollout is recent — it's about how AI reaches small businesses, not the announcement.
- **Decay:** week — trend and context pieces outlive the launch day.
- **What makes it distinct:** Ladder lens, zoomed up — the market is the protagonist, not the product or the pilot number. Differs from the data angle (single metric) and the customer angle (single studio).
- **Proof it needs:** an exec on record framing the distribution thesis; user-supplied evidence of what an unfilled class slot costs a studio; at least one comparable example, supplied by the user, of incumbent vertical software bundling AI for small businesses — without it the "trend" is one data point.
- **Facts it rests on:** Mindbody powers booking for over 50,000 studios and salons; the assistant suggests optimal class times from booking data and fills cancellations from waitlists; pricing is bundled into existing software tiers.

**Mindbody bundles its AI assistant into existing plans, skipping the AI surcharge**

- **Story type:** contrarian
- **Why a journalist cares:** A SaaS-business reporter covering how software companies monetize AI. The prevailing pattern that reporter is tracking is AI as a paid add-on or premium tier; a platform with 50,000 customers folding it into existing pricing cuts against that, and the take-private status gives the reporter a thread to pull — a private owner doesn't have to show public markets an AI revenue line. Not for fitness trades, who don't cover SaaS pricing strategy, and not for consumer press.
- **Why now:** The pricing decision is news only while the launch is; after that it's a footnote.
- **Decay:** week — pricing-strategy stories can run after launch day if an exec defends the decision on record.
- **What makes it distinct:** Inversion lens — the business-model decision is the protagonist, not the product. No other kept angle touches pricing or ownership.
- **Proof it needs:** an exec on record explaining why bundled rather than upsold, and whether that's permanent or introductory; user-supplied examples of competitors charging AI add-ons to make the contrast concrete; on-record confirmation if the take-private structure is to be linked to the pricing choice — that connection is otherwise inference, not fact.
- **Facts it rests on:** pricing is bundled into existing software tiers; Mindbody is privately held after a take-private deal; Mindbody powers booking for over 50,000 studios and salons.

- **Why a journalist cares:** A feature writer at a fitness- or wellness-industry trade outlet who covers how studios actually run. One named pilot studio, its baseline no-show problem, and what changed when the texts started going out is a concrete operations story that beat can verify and readers can act on. Not for VC or markets reporters, and not for AI press.

**Refused angles**

- "Mindbody revolutionizes fitness scheduling with AI" — `slop`.
- "Mindbody's assistant sets up a conflict with its own ClassPass marketplace" — `hallucinated_fact`. The only supplied fact is that Mindbody owns ClassPass; how the assistant interacts with ClassPass inventory was not supplied. The real question moves to the uncomfortable list.
- "Mindbody rides the AI-agents moment" — `no_why_now_but_required`. No current news signal was supplied; pegging to a generic AI wave is forced relevance a reporter sees through in one read.
- "The exec behind Mindbody's AI push" — `hallucinated_fact`. No executive is named in the facts.
- "What this launch means for the future of wellness" — `no_journalist_shape`.

**Uncomfortable questions**

- Is the 22% a relative reduction or a percentage-point drop, over how many studios and how many weeks? If Mindbody won't share the methodology, the data angle is a marketing claim, not a story.
- Can one pilot studio be named and put on record? Without that, the customer-story angle is dead and the data angle is much weaker.
- Did members opt in to the cancellation texts, and at what rate? A reporter will ask the consent question; an unprepared answer turns a product story into a texting-compliance story.
- How does filling slots from a studio's own waitlist interact with ClassPass inventory? You own both. Have the answer before any pitch, because a good trade reporter will ask.
- Is the bundled pricing permanent, or introductory pricing that becomes an upsell later? "Free for now" reads very differently from "free."
