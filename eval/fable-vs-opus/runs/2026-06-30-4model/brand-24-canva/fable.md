**Canva walks its 220 million users into Adobe and Microsoft's enterprise turf**

- **Story type:** counterposition
- **Why a journalist cares:** An enterprise-software reporter at a business or tech outlet who covers the power balance among productivity and creative-software incumbents. The story is the collision: a consumer-scale design tool adding the three things enterprise buyers demand — SSO, admin controls, brand governance — and aiming it at two public giants. This reporter can test whether the incumbents' enterprise moat is features or procurement inertia. Not for consumer-creator press, design-inspiration blogs, or SMB marketing outlets.
- **Why now:** The enterprise suite launch is the live event; competitive-positioning stories run with the announcement.
- **Decay:** 24hr — standard launch-news cycle; after that this becomes a follow-up story that needs new evidence.
- **What makes it distinct:** Perspective lens with the incumbents as the counterparty and conflict as the news value. The other kept angles are about Canva's own numbers, the AI-governance trend, and the money — this one is about who gets hurt.
- **Proof it needs:** at least one named enterprise customer or concrete pipeline evidence (the announcement disclosed none); an on-record Canva exec stating the enterprise strategy; enterprise pricing. Without a named customer this is a roadmap story, and the reporter will say so.
- **Facts it rests on:** Canva Enterprise adds brand controls, admin tools, SSO, and AI design generation; the move pushes into Adobe and Microsoft territory; 220 million monthly users.

**Canva's enterprise push tests whether 220 million free users convert into corporate contracts**

- **Story type:** data
- **Why a journalist cares:** A SaaS business-model reporter at a cloud-software trade outlet or B2B-focused newsletter who covers product-led growth. The two supplied numbers are the lead: 220 million monthly users against 24,000 paying teams. That ratio is the whole question of bottom-up enterprise sales — can consumer ubiquity be converted into procurement-grade revenue, or do the suits buy from whoever already has the contract? Not for general consumer tech desks or design trade press.
- **Why now:** The launch makes the conversion question concrete today, but the numbers support a slower analytical piece.
- **Decay:** week — data and business-model context pieces outlive the launch day.
- **What makes it distinct:** Data lens — the contrast between the two supplied numbers is the protagonist, not the product or the competitors. None of the other angles turn on the ratio.
- **Proof it needs:** the definition of a "team" (size, seats, revenue per team); whether the 24,000 figure predates this launch; any free-to-paid conversion data Canva will put on record.
- **Facts it rests on:** over 220 million monthly users; 24,000 teams on paid plans; the new enterprise suite with admin tools and SSO.

**Enterprises don't want raw generative AI — they want it fenced inside the brand book**

- **Story type:** trend
- **Why a journalist cares:** An enterprise-AI reporter covering how large companies actually deploy generative tools — governance, brand safety, and the gap between AI demos and AI in production. Canva's pairing of prompt-based design generation with brand controls and admin tools is one concrete data point in that story: the pitch is not "AI makes designs," it's "AI makes designs your legal and brand teams will allow." Not for markets desks, consumer AI-art press, or founder-profile writers.
- **Why now:** The launch supplies a fresh, concrete example; the governance trend itself has a longer runway.
- **Decay:** week — trend pieces survive the announcement cycle if the example holds up.
- **What makes it distinct:** Ladder lens, zoomed up — the category-level question (constrained enterprise AI) is the story, with Canva as evidence rather than subject. The counterposition angle is about competitors; this one is about how enterprises buy AI.
- **Proof it needs:** a named customer using AI generation under brand controls, or at minimum a concrete demonstration of how the brand controls constrain outputs; honest answers on what happens when the AI goes off-brand. Without a customer this angle is a vendor claim, and the hole should be shown, not papered over.
- **Facts it rests on:** the AI tool generates on-brand designs from a prompt; the suite includes brand controls and admin tools.

**Profitable, private, and valued at $26B: Canva goes hunting for enterprise revenue**

- **Story type:** funding-mechanics
- **Why a journalist cares:** A late-stage private-markets or markets reporter at a finance or business outlet who covers decacorn financial profiles. The hook is the unusual combination: privately held, profitable, $26B mark — now adding an enterprise motion against two public companies. This reporter can probe whether the enterprise push is about growing into the valuation. Not for product reviewers, design trades, or how-to outlets.
- **Why now:** The enterprise launch is the peg; the financial-profile question runs on a slower clock.
- **Decay:** week — money-desk analysis pieces don't expire with the launch day, but they need the peg while it's warm.
- **What makes it distinct:** Perspective lens, money protagonist — the valuation and profitability are the story, not the product or the AI. No other kept angle touches the financial profile.
- **Proof it needs:** when the $26B valuation was set (a stale mark weakens the lead); any revenue or profitability figures Canva will state on record; what share of revenue the company expects from enterprise. Note: nothing in the facts supports an IPO claim — do not let a reporter put one in your mouth.
- **Facts it rests on:** privately held, profitable, last valued at $26B; over 220 million monthly users; the enterprise suite launch.

**Refused angles**

- "Canva's recent AI acquisition is already powering its enterprise suite" — `hallucinated_fact`. The facts say Canva acquired an AI image-generation startup and separately that the suite includes AI generation. The announcement does not connect them. If Canva confirms the acquired tech powers the tool, this becomes a strong follow-up angle; until then it's speculation.
- "Enterprises are ditching Adobe for Canva" — `hallucinated_fact`. No named enterprise customers were disclosed, and there is no churn or switching evidence of any kind.
- "Canva revolutionizes enterprise design with AI" — `slop`. Press-release headline; a rewrite produced the counterposition angle instead, which is the honest version.
- "Canva adds SSO and admin tools" — `no_journalist_shape`. SSO and admin tooling are table stakes for enterprise software; this is feature news — a topic, not a story.
- "The enterprise design wars: Canva vs Microsoft" — `duplicate`. Same lens, same protagonist as the kept counterposition angle, with a weaker shape.
- "Prompts are the new designers — design software is over" — `hallucinated_fact`. A forced contrarian: no supplied evidence undermines any prevailing belief about design software. Without both the belief and the evidence, it's performance.

**Uncomfortable questions**

- Can you name even one enterprise customer? The announcement disclosed none, and the two strongest angles get materially thinner without one.
- What is a "team" in the 24,000 figure — three people on a starter plan or a 5,000-seat contract? The conversion angle lives or dies on this.
- When was the $26B valuation actually set? "Last valued" can mean last month or two years ago, and a markets reporter will ask in the first email.
- Does the acquired AI startup's technology power the new design-generation tool? A yes unlocks a refused angle; a no means stop implying it.
- Will Canva put any revenue, profitability, or enterprise-pipeline number on record, or is "profitable" the only financial word available?
