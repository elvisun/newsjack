**Canva picks a fight with Adobe and Microsoft over your company's design software budget**

- **Story type:** counterposition
- **Why a journalist cares:** An enterprise-software reporter covering the competitive landscape of productivity and creative suites — the kind of beat that tracks Adobe Creative Cloud, Microsoft 365, and Figma as a single battlefield. The hook is that Canva is explicitly moving onto incumbent turf with the procurement features (brand controls, admin tools, SSO) that make an IT department say yes. Not for consumer-tech gadget writers, design-inspiration blogs, or local business press — none of them cover vendor power struggles.
- **Why now:** The enterprise suite is launching now, and the update itself frames it as a move into Adobe's and Microsoft's territory — that's the peg, not an inference.
- **Decay:** 24hr — standard launch-day company news; the competitive frame goes stale fast once Adobe or Microsoft responds or the news cycle moves on.
- **What makes it distinct:** Perspective lens, competitor protagonist. The stakes here are market power between vendors, not product mechanics (see the SSO angle) or company financial strategy (see the "staying private" angle).
- **Proof it needs:** A feature/price comparison against Adobe Creative Cloud and Microsoft 365 enterprise tiers; a named enterprise customer who evaluated or switched from either incumbent; any on-record Canva exec quote framing this as a competitive move (the update itself doesn't quote anyone); an Adobe or Microsoft response if one exists.
- **Facts it rests on:** Canva Enterprise is "pushing into Adobe and Microsoft territory"; it adds brand controls, admin tools, SSO, and prompt-based AI design generation; Canva has 220 million monthly users and 24,000 teams on paid plans.

**Canva has converted just 24,000 of its 220 million users into paying teams, and it's betting the enterprise push can crack the rest**

- **Story type:** data
- **Why a journalist cares:** A SaaS/business-metrics reporter who covers freemium-to-paid conversion economics — the kind of beat that writes about funnel math at Dropbox, Slack, or Notion, not feature launches. The story is the ratio itself: a company with a nine-figure free user base still converting a comparatively tiny slice into revenue. Not for general tech-launch blogs looking for a same-day feature rundown, and not for consumer personal-finance outlets.
- **Why now:** The enterprise launch is the news event that makes this ratio newly relevant — it's Canva's own answer to "how do we convert more of that base."
- **Decay:** week — this is a context/analysis piece built on arithmetic, not spot news; it holds up as long as the launch is still being discussed.
- **What makes it distinct:** Data lens, with the company's own business model as protagonist — not a competitor, not a customer, not an investor. None of the other angles here turn the raw numbers themselves into the story.
- **Proof it needs:** Whether "24,000 teams" is growing, flat, or new context Canva hasn't disclosed; clarification of what a "team" means (seats per team, pricing tier); any prior disclosure to compare against; comparable conversion figures from rivals if the user wants to benchmark it.
- **Facts it rests on:** Canva has over 220 million monthly users and 24,000 teams on paid plans.

**Canva finally builds the SSO and admin controls IT departments were blocking it for**

- **Story type:** trend
- **Why a journalist cares:** An enterprise IT/security trade reporter covering SaaS procurement — the beat that treats SSO, SCIM, and admin governance as the checklist items that decide whether a tool gets approved past a pilot. The story isn't Canva vs. Adobe, it's "another consumer-rooted tool clears the compliance bar," which matters to CISOs and IT buyers deciding what to greenlight. Not for AI-hype newsletters or general business press without a security/compliance angle.
- **Why now:** These are the specific features shipping in this launch — SSO, admin tools, brand controls — so the hook is the announcement itself.
- **Decay:** 24hr — tied directly to today's feature list; it loses its edge once the announcement isn't fresh.
- **What makes it distinct:** News-values lens (relevance to a specific reader). The protagonist is the enterprise IT buyer deciding whether to approve the tool, not the competitive landscape in the Adobe/Microsoft angle.
- **Proof it needs:** Which SSO protocols are actually supported (SAML, OIDC, SCIM); any compliance certifications (SOC 2, ISO 27001) Canva can point to; an IT admin or CISO willing to comment on what was missing before; confirmation of which admin controls are genuinely new versus previously available.
- **Facts it rests on:** Canva Enterprise adds brand controls, admin tools, and single sign-on.

**How a $26 billion private company keeps expanding without touching public markets**

- **Story type:** trend
- **Why a journalist cares:** A markets or enterprise-tech finance reporter covering why late-stage, richly valued private companies keep delaying an IPO — the beat that tracks Stripe, SHEIN, and similar mega-unicorns for the same question. Canva funding both a major enterprise push and an acquisition while staying private and profitable is fresh evidence for that ongoing story. Not for product reporters covering the feature list, and not for tabloid-style "IPO rumor" outlets without real financial reporting behind them.
- **Why now:** The facts alone (private, profitable, $26B) are closer to evergreen, but the enterprise launch and the recent AI acquisition are new, current evidence of that capital strategy in action — that's what makes it timely rather than a rehash.
- **Decay:** week — this needs a bit more reporting (financials, an exec comment on IPO plans) than a same-day brief allows.
- **What makes it distinct:** Perspective lens, investor/money protagonist — inverted, since there's no funding round here. The story is the absence of outside capital: growth funded by profit and an acquisition instead. Distinct from every product-feature angle above.
- **Proof it needs:** Any comment from Canva on IPO timing or intent; revenue/profit figures beyond the bare claim of "profitable"; details of the AI acquisition (name, price, terms) to show how it was funded; the date and details of Canva's last valuation event.
- **Facts it rests on:** Canva is privately held, profitable, and last valued at $26B; it recently acquired an AI image-generation startup.

**Canva's AI acquisition is a bet that brand-safe image generation beats generic AI tools**

- **Story type:** category-creation
- **Why a journalist cares:** An AI-industry reporter covering enterprise adoption of generative tools and the guardrails companies are demanding around brand consistency and output control — a beat distinct from general AI hype coverage. The story is that Canva bought its way into proprietary AI capability rather than bolting on a general-purpose model, aimed specifically at "on-brand" output. Not for enterprise IT/security reporters (that's the SSO angle) or consumer AI-tool roundups.
- **Why now:** The acquisition is described as recent, and the AI design-generation feature is shipping in this same launch — the two facts are being pitched together right now.
- **Decay:** week — naming the acquired startup and explaining the technology takes more reporting than a same-day brief.
- **What makes it distinct:** Ladder-of-abstraction lens, zoomed up to the category level (enterprise-grade generative AI), combined with the acquisition fact. The stakes here are AI governance and brand control, not market share (the Adobe/Microsoft angle) or IT compliance (the SSO angle).
- **Proof it needs:** The name of the acquired AI image-generation startup and what technology it brought; a technical explanation of how "on-brand" generation actually works; any claim about copyright or brand-safety risk in generic AI tools needs its own source, not assumption; a customer or analyst quote on demand for guardrailed AI.
- **Facts it rests on:** Canva's AI tool generates on-brand designs from a prompt; Canva recently acquired an AI image-generation startup.

**Uncomfortable questions**

- No named enterprise customer is disclosed. The single strongest launch story — a real customer explaining why they chose Canva over Adobe or Microsoft — doesn't exist yet. Can you get one on record before pitching?
- What SSO protocols and compliance certifications does Canva Enterprise actually support? "Single sign-on" alone won't survive a question from an IT-security reporter or a skeptical CISO.
- Who is the acquired AI image-generation startup, and when did the deal close? Right now it's unnamed and undated — that gap will be the first question asked, and it undercuts the AI-governance angle until it's filled.
- Is 24,000 paid teams up, flat, or down from a prior disclosure? Without a trend, the conversion-funnel angle is one data point, not a story about change.
- Does Canva have any comment on IPO timing or plans? Without it, the "staying private" angle is describing a pattern, not stating an intent.
