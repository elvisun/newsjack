**Faire is quietly becoming a lender to 700,000 small retailers**

- **Story type:** category-creation
- **Why a journalist cares:** A fintech reporter covering embedded finance and B2B credit — the kind who writes about platforms turning transaction data into underwriting. The hook is not the payment terms; it's that a wholesale marketplace is underwriting credit itself, using order history instead of traditional credit files, across a network of 700,000 retailers and 100,000+ brands. That's a marketplace crossing into balance-sheet finance. Not for consumer BNPL desks, general startup blogs, or retail-merchandising trade press.
- **Why now:** The financing product is launching now; the launch is the peg for the larger marketplace-as-lender thesis.
- **Decay:** week — the launch itself is a 24-hour event, but the embedded-lending thesis can carry a reported piece for a week if Faire puts the underwriting mechanics on record.
- **What makes it distinct:** Ladder lens, zoomed up — the category shift (marketplaces underwriting their own buyers) is the story, not the product. The other kept angles drop down to the retailer, the brand, and the money.
- **Proof it needs:** an on-record explanation of how order-history underwriting works (what signals, what limits); whether the credit sits on Faire's balance sheet or a partner's; any approval-rate or eligibility detail Faire will disclose.
- **Facts it rests on:** financing is underwritten by Faire using the retailer's order history; the marketplace connects over 700,000 independent retailers with 100,000+ brands; retailers buy now and pay in 60 days, interest-free.

**For independent retailers, the barrier to stocking shelves isn't demand — it's cash flow**

- **Story type:** customer-story
- **Why a journalist cares:** A trade reporter covering independent and main-street retail operations — small-business cash flow, inventory buying, the mechanics of staying stocked. Faire's own claim that small retailers cite cash flow as their top barrier to ordering is the lede; the financing is the response. This reporter wants the shop owner, not the platform. Not for fintech press, VC newsletters, or national business desks chasing valuation stories.
- **Why now:** The financing launch gives the cash-flow story a concrete development to hang on; the underlying cash-flow problem is not time-pressured on its own.
- **Decay:** week — the retailer-pain framing survives the launch day if a real retailer can carry it.
- **What makes it distinct:** Perspective lens, retailer protagonist, bottom rung of the ladder — one shop's buying decision, not the platform or the category. Dies without a named retailer.
- **Proof it needs:** at least one named independent retailer willing to describe ordering decisions constrained by cash flow; the source and methodology behind "cash flow is the top barrier" (survey? internal data?); ideally a before/after order example once the financing is live.
- **Facts it rests on:** Faire says small retailers cite cash flow as their top barrier to ordering; retailers can buy inventory now and pay in 60 days, interest-free.

**Faire pays brands upfront and waits 60 days to collect — who's carrying the risk?**

- **Story type:** funding-mechanics
- **Why a journalist cares:** A fintech-risk or private-markets reporter who covers how privately held companies fund credit products. The mechanics are the story: brands are paid upfront, retailers pay in 60 days, the financing is interest-free, and Faire — privately held, last valued at $12.6B — is the underwriter. Default-rate and financing-cost terms are undisclosed, which is exactly what this reporter will probe. Not for retail trade press or product-launch roundups; this angle only works if Faire is willing to answer the hard questions.
- **Why now:** The launch is the peg; the risk questions are sharpest in the first news cycle, while other outlets run the announcement straight.
- **Decay:** 24hr — the mechanics scrutiny rides the launch coverage window.
- **What makes it distinct:** Perspective lens, money protagonist — the balance sheet and the undisclosed terms are the story, not the retailer benefit or the category shift. This is the angle a skeptical reporter writes anyway; pitching into it with real answers beats dodging it.
- **Proof it needs:** an answer to who funds the receivables (Faire's balance sheet, debt facility, or partner); what Faire will say about expected default rates and how the program makes money if it's interest-free; a spokesperson cleared to discuss credit risk on record. Without these, do not pitch this angle — it invites the question and supplies no answer.
- **Facts it rests on:** brands are paid upfront while retailers pay later; financing is underwritten by Faire; interest-free, 60-day terms; default-rate and financing-cost terms not disclosed; privately held, last valued at $12.6B.

**Small brands on Faire now get paid before the retailer pays**

- **Story type:** trend
- **Why a journalist cares:** A trade reporter covering small consumer brands and wholesale operations — the supplier side of the marketplace. For a small brand, the announcement's buried fact is the payment flow: the brand is paid upfront while the retailer pays in 60 days, with Faire in the middle. That's a working-capital story for the 100,000+ brands, not the retailers. Not for fintech desks or retailer-facing trade press — same launch, opposite audience.
- **Why now:** The financing launches now; brand-side payment terms change with it.
- **Decay:** 24hr — launch-anchored; without a brand voice it goes stale with the announcement.
- **What makes it distinct:** Perspective lens, brand protagonist — structurally different from the retailer customer-story because the beneficiary, the beat, and the pain (getting paid vs. affording inventory) are all different.
- **Proof it needs:** confirmation of what brand payment timing was before this launch (the facts don't say); a named brand willing to talk about wholesale payment terms; whether upfront payment applies to all financed orders or a subset.
- **Facts it rests on:** brands are paid upfront while retailers pay later; the marketplace connects over 700,000 independent retailers with 100,000+ brands.

**Refused angles**

- "Faire revolutionizes wholesale with game-changing financing" — `slop`. Press-release language with no story underneath; a rewrite produced angle one instead.
- "Faire takes on BNPL giants / Shopify Capital" — `hallucinated_fact`. No competitor, market-share, or rival-product fact was supplied; the counterposition has no named opponent in evidence.
- "B2B buy-now-pay-later is having its moment" — `no_why_now_but_required`. A trend piece needs evidence of the trend — other companies, market data, a current signal. None was supplied, and this skill won't invent one.
- "Faire hits 700,000 retailers" — `no_journalist_shape`. A bare platform number with no comparison, no change over time, and no beat that runs marketplace headcounts as news. The number works as context inside angle one, not as a story.
- "Interest-free financing proves Faire puts retailers first" — `slop`. Advocacy, not journalism; no reporter writes that headline.

**Uncomfortable questions**

- Who actually funds the gap between paying brands upfront and collecting in 60 days — Faire's balance sheet, a debt facility, or a partner? Every fintech reporter asks this first, and "underwritten by Faire" doesn't answer it.
- If the financing is interest-free, how does it make money? If the answer is "it doesn't, it drives order volume," say that on record — it's a better story than evasion.
- What's behind "retailers cite cash flow as their top barrier" — a survey with a methodology, or a talking point? The retailer angle leans on it.
- Will Faire disclose anything about expected default rates or eligibility? Refusing to discuss terms while announcing a lending product hands skeptical reporters their lede.
- Can you produce one named retailer and one named brand before pitching? Two of the four angles die without them.
