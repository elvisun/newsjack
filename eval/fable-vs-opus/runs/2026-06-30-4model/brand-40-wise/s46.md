Not for crypto-native press, general consumer fintech reporters, or business-desk generalists who won't know what a correspondent bank charges per leg.

- Customer story — `hallucinated_fact`. No named business customer, no specific use case, no verifiable result appears in the supplied facts. An angle built on an invented customer would be fabricated.

**Uncomfortable questions**

- The stablecoin is unnamed. Every angle above requires this. Without it, the counterposition story has no specifics on what replaces correspondent banking; the regulatory story cannot identify which regime applies; the investor story cannot assess counterparty risk. Who is the stablecoin issuer and what is Wise's relationship to them (built in-house, licensed, co-issued, resold)?
- The settlement partner is unnamed. Is Wise settling with a bank-issued stablecoin, a licensed e-money stablecoin (e.g., under MiCA), or a dollar-denominated third-party stablecoin? That distinction changes every story.
- "Faster and cheaper" is asserted but unquantified. What is the actual fee differential versus a SWIFT correspondent transfer? Settlement time: hours versus days? Without a number, the counterposition and trend angles rest on a claim that a reporter will immediately challenge.
- Which markets are live now and which are pending? The region-by-region fact is compelling for the regulatory angle, but it is unusable without at least two named markets — one approved and one not yet.
- Is there a named business customer willing to speak? Every kept angle is substantially stronger with one. Without a customer, the category-creation and counterposition angles are Wise's word against the correspondent banks' silence.
- Wise frames this as mission-extension, not a new revenue line. Is that accurate? If stablecoin settlement changes the fee structure — or if Wise earns on the stablecoin leg — that changes the investor angle significantly.
