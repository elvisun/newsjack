**The DTC darling that quietly became a wholesale company**

- **Story type:** contrarian
- **Why a journalist cares:** A beauty-industry trade reporter or DTC/retail-strategy columnist covering the collapse of the direct-to-consumer thesis. Glossier was a poster child for DTC-only branding; its own admission that wholesale is now its *fastest-growing channel* is direct evidence against the belief that digitally-native brands win by owning the customer relationship. The story is the reversal, not the door count. Not for general consumer-lifestyle press or fragrance reviewers.
- **Why now:** The 600→1,400 door expansion and the "fastest-growing channel" claim are being announced now, which is the peg for examining the strategy shift.
- **Decay:** week — the expansion is a 24-hour news event, but the DTC-reversal thesis can carry a reported trend piece for about a week.
- **What makes it distinct:** Inversion lens. The prevailing belief (DTC-native brands keep customers off shelves) is real and widely held; Glossier's own statement is the evidence against it. Distinct from the door-count scale angle because the protagonist is the *strategy*, not the retail footprint.
- **Proof it needs:** the actual growth rate or share that makes "fastest-growing channel" defensible (the fact block says Glossier won't disclose revenue — without a number this stays a claim, not a proven contrarian story); a Glossier exec on record explaining why DTC-only ended; ideally one comparable DTC brand that made the same move.
- **Facts it rests on:** wholesale is now Glossier's fastest-growing channel after a decade as DTC-only; the brand was founded in 2014 out of a beauty blog; the expansion takes it from 600 to 1,400 Sephora doors.

**Glossier more than doubles its Sephora footprint to 1,400 doors across North America**

- **Story type:** trend
- **Why a journalist cares:** A retail/beauty-distribution reporter at a trade outlet covering Sephora's prestige assortment and the Sephora-at-Kohl's rollout. The hook is the scale of a single brand's wholesale build-out and what it signals about Sephora's appetite for once-digital-only brands. Not for fragrance critics or founder-profile desks.
- **Why now:** The door expansion and the addition of Sephora at Kohl's locations are the live announcement.
- **Decay:** 24hr — standard distribution-expansion news unless tied to a broader Sephora assortment trend, which extends it toward a week.
- **What makes it distinct:** Magnitude/scale lens (news-values). Protagonist is the retail footprint and the Sephora relationship, not the strategy reversal or the product. Distinct from the contrarian angle because it does not require the DTC-thesis framing to land.
- **Proof it needs:** confirmation of the US/Canada/Sephora-at-Kohl's door split; a Sephora merchandising source willing to speak; context on how 1,400 doors compares to other prestige brands in Sephora (user must supply — not in the fact block).
- **Facts it rests on:** expansion from 600 to 1,400 Sephora doors; includes US and Canada Sephora plus Sephora at Kohl's.

**A brand born from a beauty blog is now betting on the perfume aisle**

- **Story type:** trend
- **Why a journalist cares:** A fragrance-category reporter at a beauty trade outlet covering the wave of beauty brands extending into scent. The angle is the category move: a brand founded in 2014 out of a blog launching its first fragrance line extension as it scales into shelves. Not for the retail-distribution desk (covered above) or hard business reporters who need revenue.
- **Why now:** Three new fragrance products launch alongside the wholesale expansion.
- **Decay:** 24hr — product-launch news; the broader "beauty brands move into fragrance" trend framing could extend it to a week if other brands are cited.
- **What makes it distinct:** Perspective lens, product/category as protagonist. The story is the fragrance bet and its timing alongside physical-shelf expansion, not the founder, the door count, or the DTC reversal.
- **Proof it needs:** what the three fragrance products actually are and how they differ from prior offerings; any reason to believe fragrance belongs on the newly expanded shelf space (e.g. that perfume sells better in-store than online); a fragrance-category source for trend context (user-supplied).
- **Facts it rests on:** three new fragrance products launch alongside the expansion; this is Glossier's first fragrance line extension; founded in 2014 out of a beauty blog.

**Uncomfortable questions**

- "Fastest-growing channel" off a base of zero is trivially true after a decade of DTC-only — what's the actual growth rate or revenue share? Without a number you don't disclose, the contrarian angle is a claim a reporter can't verify, and most business desks will pass.
- You disclose no revenue or profitability. Every angle that implies the wholesale move is *working* financially rests on a number you won't share. Are you prepared for a reporter to ask why?
- Is there a Glossier executive who will go on record on the end of DTC-only? Without an attributed voice, the strongest angle is just a press-release line restated.
- What is the US/Canada/Sephora-at-Kohl's door split, and is 1,400 large or ordinary for a prestige brand in Sephora? The scale angle needs that context to mean anything.
- What are the three fragrance products, and is there any evidence fragrance performs better on a physical shelf than DTC? Right now the fragrance launch and the wholesale expansion are two facts sharing a date, not one connected story.
