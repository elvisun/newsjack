**Anker, the phone-charger giant, is coming for the Tesla Powerwall**

- **Story type:** counterposition
- **Why a journalist cares:** A residential energy-storage reporter at a clean-energy or solar trade outlet covering the competitive balance in home batteries. The hook is conflict: a large, profitable electronics maker entering a category Tesla has anchored, on launch day. This reporter can ask what Anker's entry does to pricing pressure and channel dynamics in US residential storage. Not for consumer-gadget review desks, general startup blogs, or phone-accessory press.
- **Why now:** The US launch is happening now — that is the entire peg.
- **Decay:** 24hr — standard launch news; the competitive comparison goes stale once the announcement cycle passes.
- **What makes it distinct:** Perspective lens, competitor protagonist — the story is the category's power balance, not Anker's identity shift or the install claim. The conflict news value (incumbent vs challenger) carries it.
- **Proof it needs:** the disclosed US pricing and capacity figures (a reporter will line them up against Powerwall spec-for-spec on the first read); confirmation of what is actually orderable today versus announced; a company spokesperson willing to address the Tesla comparison on record.
- **Facts it rests on:** Anker is launching a home battery and solar energy-storage system in the US; it competes with Tesla Powerwall and others; Anker is a large, profitable, publicly traded electronics maker; pricing, installer network, and capacity are partially disclosed.

**The company that powers your phone wants to power your house**

- **Story type:** category-creation
- **Why a journalist cares:** A business reporter covering consumer-electronics strategy at a business or tech-business outlet — the kind who covers how hardware brands move up the value chain. The surprise/contrast is real: a brand known for $20 chargers and power banks now selling whole-home energy infrastructure. The question this beat can test is whether accessory-scale brand trust transfers to a purchase measured in thousands of dollars and decades on the wall. Not for solar trade press (they don't care about Anker's brand journey) and not for gadget reviewers.
- **Why now:** The US launch makes the category leap concrete; without it this is a strategy think-piece with no event.
- **Decay:** week — the strategy story can run after launch day, but it needs the launch as its opening scene.
- **What makes it distinct:** Ladder lens, zoomed up — the protagonist is the company's category move, not the head-to-head with Tesla (angle 1) or the installation claim (angle 3).
- **Proof it needs:** an Anker executive on record about why whole-home energy is the next move; any disclosed figures on the scale of the bet (investment, headcount, product line scope); the partially disclosed pricing, so the reporter can judge whether Anker is competing on price the way it did in chargers.
- **Facts it rests on:** Anker is best known for phone chargers and power banks and is now moving into whole-home energy; it is a large, profitable, publicly traded electronics maker; the system stores solar or off-peak grid power to run a home during outages and cut peak-rate bills.

**Anker says its home battery skips the rewiring — installers will decide if that's true**

- **Story type:** contrarian
- **Why a journalist cares:** A reporter at a solar or home-electrification trade outlet covering installation soft costs and adoption barriers. The widely held position on this beat is that whole-home storage means electricians, panel work, and permitting. Anker's claim — installs without major electrical rewiring — cuts directly against that, and this reporter has the sources (installers, electricians) to stress-test it. Not for business desks or consumer press; this is a technical-claim story for people who can check it.
- **Why now:** The claim ships with the US launch; the verification window is while the launch is being covered.
- **Decay:** week — the claim-check piece works during and shortly after the launch cycle.
- **What makes it distinct:** Inversion lens — the protagonist is the installation barrier itself, not the company or the competitor. It survives only if the claim survives scrutiny, which is the story.
- **Proof it needs:** the technical specifics of how the system avoids major rewiring (this is a company claim, not an established fact); an independent electrician or installer who has seen the system and will speak to it; clarity on code compliance and permitting, since "no major rewiring" does not mean "no permit"; the installer-network details that are only partially disclosed.
- **Facts it rests on:** the company says the system installs without major electrical rewiring; Anker is launching the system in the US; installer-network specifics are partially disclosed.

**Refused angles**

- "Anker unveils a revolutionary whole-home energy solution" — `slop`.
- "Electronics giants are piling into home batteries" — `hallucinated_fact`. Only one company's move is in evidence here. A multi-company trend needs more than one data point, and none were supplied.
- "Should you buy Anker's home battery instead of a Powerwall?" — `no_journalist_shape`. A consumer buying-guide desk needs full pricing, capacity, warranty terms, and ideally a review unit. Pricing is partially disclosed and warranty terms are not detailed — there is no story a product reporter can responsibly write yet.
- "Anker's launch lands just in time for summer blackout season" — `no_why_now_but_required`. No outage, heat-wave, or utility-rate news signal was supplied, and this skill does not invent pegs. If a real one exists, it would materially strengthen angle 1 or 3.
- "A profitable public company takes on Tesla's cash-hungry energy business" — `duplicate`. Same protagonist and lens as the category-leap angle, with a financial wrapper; the profitability fact already does its work inside angles 1 and 2.

**Uncomfortable questions**

- "Partially disclosed" pricing and capacity will not survive a reporter's first question — and the Tesla comparison guarantees a spec-for-spec lineup. What exactly is public, and what happens when a reporter asks for the rest?
- Why are warranty terms not detailed? In a category where the incumbent's warranty is a known purchase factor, "not yet detailed" reads as "not ready," and a trade reporter will say so in print.
- Can anyone outside Anker validate the no-major-rewiring claim? If the answer is no, angle 3 is a press-release assertion, not a story.
- Is the system orderable in the US today, or is this an announcement of intent? Launch coverage treats those very differently.
- Is an executive available on record to explain the move from chargers to whole-home energy? Without a voice, angle 2 has no center.
