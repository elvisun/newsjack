**Sweetgreen is making robot assembly its default store format, not a pilot**

- **Story type:** category-creation
- **Why a journalist cares:** A restaurant-tech or restaurant-operations trade reporter covering kitchen automation deployments — the beat that has watched automation live mostly in pilots and demo units. The hook is the commitment level: most new stores plus retrofits of existing ones, across a chain with locations in the low hundreds. That's a format decision, not an experiment, and it gives this reporter a deployment-at-scale story the beat rarely gets. Not for consumer food press, general AI/tech desks, or local dining reporters.
- **Why now:** The expansion decision and the margin data were just made public; the "pilot to default" turn is the new development.
- **Decay:** week — the announcement peg is a 24-hour event, but the format-shift framing supports a week of trend coverage.
- **What makes it distinct:** Ladder lens, zoomed up — the protagonist is the store format and the category, not the company's stock or its workers. The other kept angles drop to the balance sheet, the crew, and a single store.
- **Proof it needs:** the actual count of planned automated new stores and retrofits; deployment timeline; what "minimal staff" means in concrete roles per store; throughput numbers a reporter can cite rather than characterize.
- **Facts it rests on:** Infinite Kitchen assembles bowls and salads on a conveyor with minimal staff; Sweetgreen plans to put the system in most new stores and retrofit some existing ones; total locations number in the low hundreds; company-reported higher margins and faster throughput at automated locations.

**An unprofitable public salad chain is betting its margin story on machines**

- **Story type:** funding-mechanics
- **Why a journalist cares:** A restaurant-sector markets or earnings reporter at a business outlet covering public fast-casual operators' paths to profitability. Sweetgreen is publicly traded and not yet net-profitable, and it is now publicly attributing higher restaurant-level margins to automation — that's a testable investment thesis, and the fact that the margin figures come as a range rather than precise numbers is exactly the soft spot this reporter will press on. Not for trade ops press, labor desks, or product reviewers.
- **Why now:** The company just shared margin data alongside the expansion plan; markets reporters cover claims like this when they're fresh, not weeks later.
- **Decay:** week — the disclosure is current company news; the profitability-path framing holds until the next earnings event resets it.
- **What makes it distinct:** Perspective lens, money protagonist — the story is whether automation actually closes the gap to net profitability, not what the machine does or who it displaces.
- **Proof it needs:** the exact margin range disclosed and where it was disclosed; restaurant-level versus company-level margin definitions; an executive willing to say on record how automation maps to the net-profitability timeline. Without tighter numbers, expect the story to run as skepticism.
- **Facts it rests on:** Sweetgreen is publicly traded and not yet net-profitable; the company says automated locations run higher restaurant-level margins and faster throughput; margin figures were given as a range, not precise.

**What happens to crew hours when the salad line is a conveyor**

- **Story type:** defensive-comment
- **Why a journalist cares:** A labor or workforce reporter covering food-service automation and scheduling — a beat that will write this story with or without Sweetgreen's participation, because "minimal staff" plus "reduced staff hours" worries is the conflict at the center of the announcement. The play here is getting ahead of it: real before/after staffing numbers and a redeployment plan turn an exposé into a balanced piece. Not for restaurant-tech cheerleading outlets or markets desks; do not pitch this as a positive automation story.
- **Why now:** The expansion announcement puts staffing questions in play immediately; the window to shape the labor framing is while the news is live.
- **Decay:** week — labor follow-ups trail the announcement cycle by days, not hours.
- **What makes it distinct:** Conflict lens, worker protagonist — the cost side of the same margin math the markets angle celebrates. Same fact, opposite ledger column.
- **Proof it needs:** actual staff-hours data per automated store versus traditional; what happens to displaced hours (redeployment, attrition, cuts); whether any crew member or store manager will speak on record. Without these, this angle is a liability, not a pitch.
- **Facts it rests on:** the Infinite Kitchen runs with minimal staff; some worry about reduced staff hours; the system is going into most new stores and some retrofits.

**One retrofitted Sweetgreen, before and after the conveyor**

- **Story type:** data
- **Why a journalist cares:** A data-minded restaurant-business reporter at a trade outlet covering unit economics — the kind who wants one store's throughput, labor hours, and margin before and after retrofit instead of a chain-wide range. Retrofits are the harder, more interesting case than new builds: an existing store with an existing baseline is a natural experiment. Not for national business desks or general food media; this is a trade-depth story.
- **Why now:** Retrofits are planned, so this angle ripens as the first conversions complete; it is not tied to the announcement day.
- **Decay:** month — the unit-economics story holds as long as the data is exclusive and recent.
- **What makes it distinct:** Ladder lens, zoomed all the way down — one named store, one set of numbers. The category angle argues the trend; this one proves or breaks it at floor level.
- **Proof it needs:** one named retrofitted location; its before/after throughput, staffing, and store-level margin; permission to share store-level data publicly. This angle does not exist until that data does.
- **Facts it rests on:** Sweetgreen plans to retrofit some existing stores; company-reported higher margins and faster throughput at automated locations; margin figures currently only available as a range.

**Refused angles**

- "Sweetgreen revolutionizes fast casual with cutting-edge robotics" — `slop`.
- "Restaurant robots were all hype — Sweetgreen just proved the doubters wrong" — `hallucinated_fact`: a contrarian angle needs a documented prevailing belief and evidence against it; neither was supplied.
- "Sweetgreen's automated stores run higher margins" as a standalone pitch — `duplicate`: same money protagonist as the profitability angle, with less story around it.
- "The AI angle: what Sweetgreen's robots mean for artificial intelligence" — `off-beat`: nothing in the facts says AI, and a conveyor-assembly line pitched to AI reporters reads as forced.
- "What this means for the future of work" — `no_journalist_shape`: no specific beat, no specific outlet, no reader who is materially affected.

**Uncomfortable questions**

- Will you disclose exact margin figures? A range invites every markets reporter to assume the low end and write the skeptical version.
- What actually happens to staff hours in an automated store — and can you say it on record? If you can't answer this, the labor story gets written about you instead of with you.
- Is any single retrofitted store able and permitted to share before/after numbers? Without one, the strongest trade angle is theoretical.
- Was this disclosure tied to a dated event (earnings, investor day, press briefing)? The "why now" on the announcement-pegged angles depends on it.

**Next step:** `journalist-fit-check` — four viable shapes exist; pick one and resolve it to a real reporter. But close the margin-range and staff-hours proof gaps first, because the two strongest angles fall apart in a first phone call without them.
