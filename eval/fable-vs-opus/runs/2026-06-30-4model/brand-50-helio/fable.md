**Ex-SpaceX and Planet Labs engineers raise $7M to hunt wildfires from orbit**

- **Story type:** founder-profile
- **Why a journalist cares:** A space-industry reporter at a space-business trade outlet or VC newsletter tracking the second-generation operator-to-founder pipeline out of SpaceX and Planet Labs. The hook is the lineage: launch-and-constellation experience aimed at a public-safety problem instead of comms or imagery resale. Not for fire-service trade press, local news, or general climate desks that don't cover founder backstories.
- **Why now:** The $7M seed announcement is the time hook. Without it this is an evergreen profile nobody runs.
- **Decay:** 24hr — founder-lineage angles ride the funding announcement and die with it unless tied to a launch or a reported trend.
- **What makes it distinct:** Perspective lens, founder protagonist. The people and where they came from are the story — the other kept angles make the investor and the buyer the protagonist.
- **Proof it needs:** confirmed roles and tenure at SpaceX and Planet Labs; a founder on record about what specifically transfers from those companies to a fire-detection constellation; an honest answer on why they left.
- **Facts it rests on:** founders are ex-SpaceX and ex-Planet Labs; $7M seed round; 11-person team; satellites carry infrared sensors to detect wildfire ignitions.

**Why a deep-tech fund put $7M into wildfire satellites that haven't flown**

- **Story type:** funding-mechanics
- **Why a journalist cares:** A VC reporter covering deep-tech and climate-adaptation theses — specifically checks written against pre-revenue, pre-launch hardware. The interesting tension is on the term sheet: nothing in orbit, no operational results, no disclosed revenue, and a fund plus two climate angels still priced the risk. That's a story about how seed investors underwrite climate-adaptation hardware. Not for fire-service press, space-enthusiast outlets, or product reviewers.
- **Why now:** The round is the peg, but the thesis must come from the lead fund on record, not be inferred from the wire.
- **Decay:** week — investor-thesis pieces can run after announcement day if the partner supplies a real argument.
- **What makes it distinct:** Perspective lens, investor protagonist. The bet and its risk math are the story, not the founders or the technology claim.
- **Proof it needs:** an on-record quote from the lead fund's partner explaining the thesis; the fund's name cleared for use; clarity on what milestones the $7M is supposed to reach (first launch? first constellation? the MOU converting to a contract?).
- **Facts it rests on:** $7M seed led by a deep-tech seed fund with two climate angels participating; no satellite in orbit; no operational results; revenue and launch date not disclosed.

**A state fire agency signed an MOU with a satellite startup that hasn't launched anything**

- **Story type:** trend
- **Why a journalist cares:** A govtech or emergency-management trade reporter covering how public-safety agencies procure unproven technology. The MOU is the most journalistically interesting fact in this update: a state agency putting paper — even non-binding paper — on a pre-launch, 11-person company says something about how desperate agencies are for faster detection, or how loose tech MOUs have become. This reporter will probe both readings; the founder must be ready for that. Not for space trade press or founder-profile writers.
- **Why now:** The funding announcement surfaces the MOU. June sits inside the Northern Hemisphere fire season, which raises the beat's general attention — but no current fire event was supplied, so do not claim one.
- **Decay:** week — agency-procurement angles outlive the funding-day cycle if the agency will talk.
- **What makes it distinct:** Perspective lens, buyer/regulator protagonist. The agency's decision is the story, and it's the only kept angle where Helio isn't the main character.
- **Proof it needs:** the agency named and willing to comment on what the MOU actually commits; whether it involves money, data trials, or just intent; what the agency currently uses for detection and how long it takes. Without a nameable agency this angle does not survive contact with a reporter.
- **Facts it rests on:** memorandum of understanding with one state fire agency; satellites alert agencies on ignitions; no satellite in orbit yet; agency name not disclosed.

**Refused angles**

- "Helio's satellites detect wildfires faster than any current system" — `hallucinated_fact`. That's the company's claim with zero operational results behind it; nothing is in orbit. It can appear in a pitch only as an attributed claim with a stated baseline, never as the headline.
- "Newsjack: tie Helio to this season's wildfires" — `no_why_now_but_required`. No current fire signal was supplied, and fabricating urgency off an active disaster is exactly the forced-peg failure mode — and a brand-safety landmine for a company with no operational capability yet.
- "Helio is revolutionizing wildfire detection from space" — `slop`. Press-release framing, undefended superlative.
- "The space-tech angle: small satellites are the next big thing" — `duplicate` / `no_journalist_shape`. It's the founder-profile angle with the people removed and no specific beat left.

**Uncomfortable questions**

- Will the state fire agency agree to be named? The strongest angle in this set dies without it.
- What does the MOU actually commit — money, a pilot, data sharing, or just a signature? A reporter will ask in the first email.
- What is the baseline for "faster than current systems" — GOES satellite detection, lookout cameras, 911 calls? "Minutes" means nothing without the comparison you're claiming to beat.
- When is the first launch? If it's more than a year out, every detection claim is a prospectus, not a product, and reporters will frame it that way.
- Is the lead fund willing to be named and to state its thesis on record? The funding-mechanics angle is inference until then.

**Next step:** `journalist-fit-check` — three viable shapes exist; pick one and resolve it to real reporters. If the agency won't be named, drop the MOU angle before building any list.
