**Substack doesn't want to be a newsletter company anymore**

- **Story type:** trend
- **Why a journalist cares:** A media-business reporter at a media-industry outlet or platforms newsletter who covers platform strategy and the creator economy. The hook is not the video feature itself — it's that native video plus a TikTok-style feed completes a visible identity change from email tool to media app. This reporter can ask what Substack is now competing for: inboxes or attention. Not for consumer gadget press, product-review outlets, or local business desks.
- **Why now:** The native video launch is live now and is the latest concrete evidence of the stated "media app" strategy.
- **Decay:** 24hr — this rides the launch news cycle; the pivot framing buys a little context room but the peg is the announcement.
- **What makes it distinct:** Ladder lens, zoomed up — the category shift is the story, not the feature. The other kept angles drop down to the business model, the funding math, and the earnings number.
- **Proof it needs:** an on-record Substack statement framing video as part of the media-app strategy (not just a feature ship); any usage or adoption signal for the existing TikTok-style feed; a clear answer on whether email remains the core product.
- **Facts it rests on:** writers can record, host, and monetize video and podcasts directly; Substack has leaned into a "media app" strategy with a TikTok-style feed.

**Substack is building a TikTok-style feed without the ad model**

- **Story type:** contrarian
- **Why a journalist cares:** A platforms or social-media reporter covering feed economics and business models. The prevailing belief is real and widely held: short-video feeds are ad businesses. Substack's feed is funded by a 10% cut of paid subscriptions — 4 million of them — which is structural evidence against that belief. The reporter can test whether subscription economics can sustain an attention product. Not for ad-trade press expecting ad-spend data (there is none), and not for general startup blogs.
- **Why now:** The video launch makes the subscription-funded-feed question concrete today rather than hypothetical.
- **Decay:** week — the launch is the peg, but the business-model question supports a context piece beyond the announcement day.
- **What makes it distinct:** Inversion lens — names a real prevailing belief (feeds run on ads) and uses supplied facts (10% subscription cut, 4M paid subscriptions, TikTok-style feed) as evidence against it. The trend angle above says what Substack is becoming; this one says why its version is structurally different.
- **Proof it needs:** confirmation that video carries no ads and no separate fee structure; whether the 10% cut applies identically to video and podcast revenue; any engagement or conversion data linking the feed to paid subscriptions.
- **Facts it rests on:** the platform takes a 10% cut of subscription revenue; over 4 million paid subscriptions across all publications; the TikTok-style feed; native video and podcast monetization.

**Substack bets a 10% cut can pay for a full media platform**

- **Story type:** funding-mechanics
- **Why a journalist cares:** A media-business or private-markets reporter who covers platform unit economics and private-company sustainability. The tension is supplied in the facts: a privately held company with public funding struggles is expanding into hosting video — and its revenue is capped at 10% of subscriptions. The $400M creator-earnings aggregate is the company's proof point; the reporter's question is whether the platform's slice covers the new cost base. Not for creator how-to outlets, product reviewers, or founder-profile desks.
- **Why now:** The video launch raises the cost question now; the funding-struggle history makes it a live business story rather than a hypothetical.
- **Decay:** week — business-model scrutiny pieces run on the analysis, not the announcement hour.
- **What makes it distinct:** Perspective lens, money protagonist — the sustainability of the model is the story, not the product or the category. It is the only kept angle where the funding struggles are load-bearing.
- **Proof it needs:** any disclosable revenue, margin, or infrastructure-cost figure; an on-record answer to how video hosting is financed given the funding history; whether the 10% cut is the only revenue line.
- **Facts it rests on:** 10% cut of subscription revenue; over 4 million paid subscriptions; privately held with public funding struggles; native video hosting is being launched; top creators collectively earned over $400M last year.

**Substack says its top creators earned $400M — the number it won't break down is the story**

- **Story type:** data
- **Why a journalist cares:** A data-minded creator-economy reporter at a business outlet or creator-economy newsletter who covers what platforms pay and to whom. The scale claim is real and pitchable: $400M against 4 million paid subscriptions is a concrete subscription-economy data point. But the supplied facts say individual earnings beyond the aggregate are not disclosed — this reporter will ask how many creators "top creators" means and what the median looks like. Pitch it only if Substack will engage with that question. Not for anyone if the company refuses all breakdown — the angle dies without it.
- **Why now:** The $400M figure was released with the video launch; numbers decay with their announcement.
- **Decay:** 24hr — a headline statistic is freshest in the launch cycle; without follow-up data it doesn't sustain a second-week story.
- **What makes it distinct:** Data lens, scale plus distribution — the number itself is the protagonist. The funding-mechanics angle uses the figure as a proof point for platform viability; this angle interrogates the figure as a labor-market fact about creators.
- **Proof it needs:** how many creators make up "top creators"; median or distribution figures, or an explicit on-record reason for withholding them; whether $400M is comparable to a prior-year figure.
- **Facts it rests on:** top creators collectively earned over $400M last year; over 4 million paid subscriptions; 10% cut of subscription revenue; individual creator earnings beyond the aggregate not disclosed.
