Not for consumer tech press, retail-buyer trade outlets, or general startup blogs that won't parse balance-sheet risk.

- **Decay:** week — the launch itself is 24-hour news, but the risk-mechanics analysis is worth a reporter's time researching this week rather than filing same-day.
- **What makes it distinct:** Perspective lens, "the money" as protagonist — this is about Faire's own balance-sheet exposure and business-model shift, distinct from the retailer-relief angle and the category-trend angle below.
- **Proof it needs:** Faire's disclosed (or refused-to-disclose) default rate and financing-cost structure; how the program is capitalized (own balance sheet vs. a warehouse facility vs. an unnamed bank partner); any Faire comment on loss-reserve exposure.
- **Facts it rests on:** financing underwritten by Faire using the retailer's order history; default-rate and financing-cost terms not disclosed; brands are paid upfront while retailers pay later; company privately held, last valued at $12.6B.

The hook: Faire won't disclose its default rate or financing cost, and brands get paid upfront while retailers carry the deferred obligation — meaning the risk sits somewhere, on undisclosed terms. Not for launch-announcement roundups or retailer-facing trade press looking for a straightforward benefits story.

- **Decay:** week — this needs actual reporting (a request for comment, possibly a follow-up once retailers start using the program), so it isn't same-day spot news.

- **Facts it rests on:** default-rate and financing-cost terms not disclosed; brands are paid upfront while retailers pay later; the financing is underwritten by Faire using the retailer's order history.
