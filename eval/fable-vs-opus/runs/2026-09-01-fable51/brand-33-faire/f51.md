**Faire turns its wholesale marketplace into a lender for independent shops**

- **Story type:** funding-mechanics
- **Why a journalist cares:** A fintech reporter covering embedded finance and B2B credit, at a fintech trade or the fintech desk of a business outlet. The hook is not "pay later"; it is that a marketplace is underwriting its own buyers on their order history rather than a bank score, and is not saying who carries the default risk or what the money costs. That reporter can test whether this is a credit business or a marketing expense. Not for consumer BNPL reporters, retail-lifestyle press, or general startup blogs.
- **Why now:** The financing is launching today.
- **Decay:** 24hr, standard launch cycle. The credit-risk question can outlive the launch if Faire answers it on record.
- **What makes it distinct:** Perspective lens, protagonist is the money. The other angles look at the retailer, the brand, and the company's business model. This one asks how the loan works and who is exposed if it goes wrong.
- **Proof it needs:** whether the financing sits on Faire's balance sheet or with a partner; the underwriting inputs beyond "order history"; credit limits and eligibility rules; a stated position on default-rate expectations, since "not disclosed" will be the second paragraph otherwise.
- **Facts it rests on:** financing lets retailers pay in 60 days, interest-free; underwritten by Faire using the retailer's order history; default-rate and financing-cost terms not disclosed; brands are paid upfront.

**Independent shops say cash flow, not demand, decides what they restock**

- **Story type:** customer-story
- **Why a journalist cares:** A small-business or independent-retail trade reporter covering how indie shops manage inventory and working capital. The story is one shop owner and the order they didn't place because the money wasn't there, with the Faire product as the second paragraph, not the first. Not for fintech reporters, VC newsletters, or business desks covering private-company valuations.
- **Why now:** The launch gives the reporter a peg, but the cash-flow problem is the durable story and can run any time in the next week or two.
- **Decay:** week, because the retailer's problem is not tied to launch day.
- **What makes it distinct:** Ladder lens, zoomed all the way down. One named retailer carries the story. Every other angle here is about Faire.
- **Proof it needs:** at least one named independent retailer willing to describe a specific ordering decision shaped by cash flow; the source behind "cash flow is the top barrier," with sample size, date, and who was surveyed; ideally a retailer who has already used the 60-day terms.
- **Facts it rests on:** Faire says small retailers cite cash flow as their top barrier to ordering; the marketplace connects over 700,000 independent retailers with 100,000+ brands; retailers can pay in 60 days, interest-free.

**Brands get paid on day one. Who carries the 60-day float?**

- **Story type:** defensive-comment
- **Why a journalist cares:** A wholesale or consumer-products trade reporter who covers the economics of selling through marketplaces, writing for the brands on the supply side. "Interest-free" for the retailer means someone else absorbs the cost, and the brand reporter's first question is whether that lands on the brand as a fee, a lower payout, or nothing at all. Faire will be asked to defend the undisclosed terms; the pitch only works if it has the answer. Not for retailer-facing press, fintech desks, or founder-profile writers.
- **Why now:** Launch day. Brand-side reporters will ask this question with or without you.
- **Decay:** 24hr for the launch reaction; week if Faire publishes the brand-side economics and the story becomes about the model.
- **What makes it distinct:** Perspective lens, protagonist is the brand. The fintech angle asks who holds the credit risk; this asks who pays for the retailer's free money and whether the deal changes for the 100,000+ brands on the platform.
- **Proof it needs:** the brand-side economics of the financing, on record; whether brand payout timing or fees change when a retailer uses the 60-day option; whether brands can opt out; one brand willing to say what upfront payment does to their own cash position.
- **Facts it rests on:** brands are paid upfront while retailers pay later; financing is interest-free to the retailer; financing-cost terms not disclosed; 100,000+ brands on the marketplace.

**A $12.6B marketplace decides its next product is credit**

- **Story type:** category-creation
- **Why a journalist cares:** A reporter covering late-stage private companies and platform business models at a business or tech-business outlet. The company is the protagonist: a privately held marketplace with a $12.6B last valuation adding a financial product between buyer and seller. That reporter wants to know whether credit is a growth lever for order volume, a new revenue line, or both. Not for indie-retail trade press, consumer BNPL coverage, or local business desks.
- **Why now:** The launch is the peg; the business-model question has a week of runway if Faire will discuss why it built this.
- **Decay:** week, since the strategy story does not depend on launch-day timing.
- **What makes it distinct:** Ladder lens, zoomed up from the product to the company's strategy, with the power-elite value of the valuation as the entry point. The fintech angle is about the loan; this is about why a marketplace at this scale is making the loan.
- **Proof it needs:** an on-record explanation of how Faire makes money on the financing, or a plain statement that it doesn't yet; any evidence that financed retailers order more (even directional); confirmation of the $12.6B figure and its date. Without other marketplaces doing the same thing, this stays a single-company story, not a trend, and should be pitched that way.
- **Facts it rests on:** privately held, last valued at $12.6B; over 700,000 retailers and 100,000+ brands; Faire underwrites the financing itself; Faire says cash flow is the top barrier to ordering.

**Refused angles**

- "Faire transforms small-business financing" — `slop`.
- "Order history is the new credit score for small retailers" — `duplicate`. Same lens and same protagonist as the lender angle; the underwriting method is already the core of that story, and splitting it out is two pitches for one reporter.
- "Faire proves B2B buy-now-pay-later is the next big fintech category" — `hallucinated_fact`. Nothing in the update speaks to the category beyond one product.
- "700,000 independent retailers just got access to credit" — `hallucinated_fact`. The 700,000 figure is marketplace size, not the eligible base; eligibility and limits were not given.
- "Faire's financing squeezes traditional trade-credit and factoring providers" — `hallucinated_fact`. No competitor, incumbent, or market data was supplied to support the conflict.
- "Consumer BNPL is in trouble, but small-business BNPL is where it works" — `hallucinated_fact`. A contrarian angle needs a real prevailing belief and evidence against it; neither was supplied.
- "Regulators should look at undisclosed terms on marketplace lending" — `no_why_now_but_required`. No regulatory signal was provided, and manufacturing one is forced relevance.

**Uncomfortable questions**

- Who funds the loans and who eats a default: Faire's balance sheet, a bank partner, or the brand? Every fintech reporter will ask this first, and "not disclosed" becomes the headline.
- What does "interest-free" actually cost, and who pays it? If brands take a fee or a lower payout, the brand-side angle turns from a story into a complaint you'd rather not seed.
- Where does "cash flow is the top barrier" come from? A dated survey with a sample size holds up; a sales-team impression does not.
- Is every retailer eligible, or only those with enough order history? What is the credit limit? Without this, the scale of the product is unknowable.
- Do you have one named retailer and one named brand willing to talk? Two of the four angles are thin without them.
- Did brands already extend their own payment terms on Faire before this? If so, the honest story is a change in who carries the credit, not a new benefit.

**Next step:** `journalist-fit-check`, once you have answered the funding-source and cost questions and picked one angle. Until those two answers exist, the lender and brand angles are pitches that invite a question you cannot answer.
