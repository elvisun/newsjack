## Refused angles

- **Named merchant customer story** — `hallucinated_fact`: No launch merchant has gone on record. A customer-story angle requires a real named customer describing real results. Inventing one or building toward it with vague language violates the grounding rule.
- **"Pay by Bank signals the end of credit card rewards"** — `hallucinated_fact`: Interesting thesis, but the supplied facts say nothing about consumer rewards programs, card-issuer response, or consumer behavior. The angle would require inventing evidence.

## Uncomfortable questions

1. **Can any launch merchant go on record?** Every merchant-economics and data angle weakens materially without a real business confirming the savings figure from their own data. "Early merchants say" is plausible; a named merchant is a story.

2. **What is the actual dollar threshold for "large transactions"?** A reporter will ask in the first reply. Without a specific number, the 70% figure is a marketing claim, not a data point. If the threshold is $500 or $5,000, that changes who this product is actually built for.

3. **How does the flat fee compare to existing ACH and same-day ACH options, not just card interchange?** Merchants already have non-card bank transfer options. If the relevant comparison is ACH rates rather than card interchange, the savings narrative is materially different.

4. **Is Plaid competing against Stripe and Adyen or only against card networks?** The positioning answer changes which reporters will care and how the pitch should be framed. If Plaid is undercutting other payment processors, the company-strategy angle becomes much sharper.

5. **Will Plaid share any volume, transaction, or adoption numbers at launch?** Without even rough scale indicators, the FedNow angle lacks a meaningful data peg and the category-creation angle has no proof of traction.
