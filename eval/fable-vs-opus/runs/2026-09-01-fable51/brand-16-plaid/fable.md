**Plaid's Pay by Bank gives merchants a route around card interchange**

- **Story type:** counterposition
- **Why a journalist cares:** A payments-industry reporter at a fintech or banking trade outlet covering the card networks' interchange economics and who is trying to erode them. The story is the challenge to the incumbents: account-to-account payments priced as a flat per-transaction fee instead of interchange, distributed through a company already wired into over 12,000 financial institutions. Not for consumer personal-finance desks, general startup blogs, or crypto reporters.
- **Why now:** The product is launching now; the launch is the event that lets this reporter write the network-vs-challenger story today.
- **Decay:** 24hr — standard launch-news cycle; after the launch day this becomes a trend piece that needs adoption evidence.
- **What makes it distinct:** Perspective lens, competitor/incumbent protagonist — the card networks are the story, not Plaid's product mechanics. The other kept angles zoom up to the FedNow rails and down to the fee math.
- **Proof it needs:** the actual flat fee (number or schedule), on the record; a Plaid spokesperson willing to say plainly that this competes with card interchange; at least one merchant or processing-volume detail beyond "early merchants," since no launch merchant is currently on record.
- **Facts it rests on:** Pay by Bank lets merchants accept account-to-account payments that bypass card networks; merchants pay a flat per-transaction fee instead of card interchange; Plaid says early merchants see fees up to 70% lower than cards on large transactions.

**FedNow gets a merchant-facing on-ramp through Plaid's 12,000 bank connections**

- **Story type:** trend
- **Why a journalist cares:** A payments-infrastructure reporter at a banking-technology or real-time-payments trade outlet tracking whether the new FedNow rails actually reach commerce. The hook is distribution: a launch that pairs instant bank verification with FedNow and rides connectivity to over 12,000 financial institutions is a concrete data point on real-time rails meeting merchant checkout. Not for retail-merchandising trades, consumer tech press, or VC-funding desks.
- **Why now:** The launch is live now, and FedNow is described in the update as new rails — this is an early merchant-payments use of them, which is exactly what an infrastructure beat is watching for.
- **Decay:** week — the rails-adoption story holds past launch day because the beat tracks the trend, not the announcement.
- **What makes it distinct:** Ladder lens, zoomed up — the rails are the protagonist and Plaid is the evidence. The counterposition angle is about who loses; this one is about what the market is becoming.
- **Proof it needs:** confirmation of how the product uses FedNow (which transaction legs, what settlement times); what share of the 12,000 connected institutions can transact over FedNow today; whether Plaid will share early transaction-volume figures.
- **Facts it rests on:** the product uses instant bank verification and the new FedNow rails; Plaid connects to over 12,000 financial institutions; the product is launching now.

**Flat fee vs. interchange: the math behind Plaid's "up to 70% cheaper" claim**

- **Story type:** data
- **Why a journalist cares:** A merchant-payments trade reporter who covers acceptance costs and writes fee-economics breakdowns for retailers and B2B sellers. The lead is the number: a flat per-transaction fee versus percentage-based interchange means the savings concentrate on large transactions — which is exactly what Plaid's "up to 70% lower" claim says, and exactly what this reporter will want to stress-test. Not for general business desks or founder-profile writers; this dies without the underlying math.
- **Why now:** The launch makes the pricing claim public now; the fee analysis stays useful for the week after.
- **Decay:** week — fee-economics explainers run on the trade cycle, not the announcement hour.
- **What makes it distinct:** Data lens, scale/variation — the protagonist is the fee math and the merchant segments it favors (large-ticket sellers), not the card networks or the rails. It only survives if Plaid will defend the number.
- **Proof it needs:** the methodology behind "up to 70% lower" — sample size, transaction-size threshold, which card costs it was compared against; the actual flat fee; a named merchant willing to confirm real savings, since none is on record yet.
- **Facts it rests on:** merchants pay a flat per-transaction fee instead of card interchange; Plaid says early merchants see fees up to 70% lower than cards on large transactions.

**Refused angles**

- "Merchant X cuts payment costs 70% with Plaid's Pay by Bank" — `hallucinated_fact`. No launch merchant has gone on record. A customer-story with an unnamed customer is a press release, not a story.
- "Pay by bank finally takes off in the US" — `hallucinated_fact`. A contrarian angle needs evidence against the prevailing belief; one launch with no named merchant and no adoption data is a claim, not evidence.
- "Plaid revolutionizes payments with seamless account-to-account checkout" — `slop`. Press-release framing; rewriting it just produced the counterposition angle, which is already kept.
- "What Plaid's move means for payments regulation" — `no_why_now_but_required`. There is no live regulatory signal in the supplied facts, and reaching for one would be forced relevance.
- "Why Plaid built Pay by Bank" (founder/exec profile) — `no_journalist_shape`. The update contains no founder, exec, or origin facts; there is no person to hang a profile on.

**Uncomfortable questions**

- Can any merchant go on record? Every kept angle gets materially stronger with one name, and the data angle is borderline without it.
- What does "up to 70% lower" actually mean — what transaction size, what sample, compared against which card fees? If the answer is "a few early merchants on very large transactions," say that before a reporter finds it.
- What is the flat fee? If you won't publish the number, the fee-economics angle is dead and the counterposition angle weakens.
- How much of this runs on FedNow today versus ACH or other rails? An infrastructure reporter will ask in the first email.
