**Uncomfortable questions**

1. The regulated-enterprise angle collapses without a named customer. Can you provide a bank, hospital system, or government agency willing to go on record about why they cannot use closed-API providers? If not, the angle rests entirely on Databricks' own assertion.

2. The benchmark numbers are self-reported. Does Databricks have third-party evaluation results — from an academic lab, an independent red-team, or a standards body — that can be cited alongside the company's own numbers? A tech reporter covering AI model credibility will ask this in the first email.

3. What does the open license actually say? The specific permissions — commercial fine-tuning, data residency guarantees, redistribution rights, use by direct competitors — determine whether the regulated-enterprise framing holds. "Open license" varies enormously in what it permits.

4. The $3B ARR figure: is this from a press release, a data room, or an attributed CFO statement? The accountability angle needs to know how the number was delivered and to whom before a reporter commits to it.

5. The IPO signal is deliberately vague. Is there any more concrete timeline you can share on background? The financial credibility angle sharpens considerably if there is a realistic filing horizon, and weakens if "eventual IPO" is boilerplate.
