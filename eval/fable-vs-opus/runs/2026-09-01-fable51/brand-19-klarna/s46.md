## Uncomfortable Questions

1. **What does "resolved" mean in Klarna's measurement?** A chat that ends in under 2 minutes could mean a fast resolution or a fast deflection. The resolution-time metric is striking; the quality-of-resolution metric is absent. This distinction is what consumer advocates are likely pressing, and it determines whether angles 1, 2, and 4 survive a journalist's first question.

2. **Is the 20% headcount figure net of new hires?** If Klarna hired heavily in engineering, risk, or other functions while shedding customer service, the headline workforce reduction understates what happened in one function and overstates it overall. The labor angle requires clarity on this.

3. **Are the consumer advocates named, on record, and specific about what harms they've documented?** "Some consumer advocates have raised concerns" is thin sourcing. The consumer-protection angle needs a named organization or regulator with a specific complaint pattern before it is pitchable.

4. **Does the IPO filing characterize the AI efficiency gains as recurring operational leverage or as a one-time restructuring benefit?** The distinction matters enormously to the funding-mechanics angle and to how investors price the profitability claim.

5. **What happens when the AI fails or is disputed?** If there is no disclosed escalation path for consumers who believe the AI handled their case incorrectly, that is a materially different story than if a human-review layer exists.
