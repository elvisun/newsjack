**Patagonia will now fix your jacket in every US store — and mail-in repair opens nationwide**

- **Story type:** category-creation
- **Why a journalist cares:** A retail-operations reporter at an apparel or retail trade outlet covering store-level service models and reverse logistics. The story isn't sustainability messaging — it's a specialty retailer converting 70+ stores into repair intake points and standing up a national mail-in workflow, which is a labor, floor-space, and logistics decision other retailers will be asked about. Not for consumer lifestyle desks, gear-review outlets, or general climate press looking for a green-brand feature.
- **Why now:** The nationwide availability is new as of this announcement; the operational story is live while the rollout is fresh.
- **Decay:** 24hr — a program-expansion announcement is standard company news; the operational read on it survives a day or two at most before it's stale.
- **What makes it distinct:** Ladder lens, zoomed up to the category — repair-as-retail-service is the story, not Patagonia's brand values or the resale number. The other angles take the resale data and the ownership structure as their subjects.
- **Proof it needs:** how many repair technicians and how many repair sites actually service the 70+ stores; average turnaround time for a mail-in repair; what "basic" vs "complex" means in practice, with a price example for a complex repair; whether store staff repair on-site or ship to a central facility.
- **Facts it rests on:** repairs available at 70+ US stores plus nationwide mail-in; basic repairs free, complex repairs charged at cost; no new apparel line announced.

**Half a million secondhand Patagonia items later, the company still won't say if resale pays**

- **Story type:** data
- **Why a journalist cares:** A retail or business reporter covering circular-economy economics and resale platforms, at a business outlet or a retail-industry publication that has tracked resale claims from other brands. The hook is the gap: a headline cumulative number offered, and the denominator withheld. That's a reportable tension, and the reporter can press for what 500,000 items means against total units sold. Not for outlets that will simply reprint the 500,000 figure as a sustainability win.
- **Why now:** The company is putting the 500,000 figure forward now as part of the repair expansion, which is what makes the refusal to size it newly askable.
- **Decay:** week — the number is a company-supplied stat, not an event; a reporter has a week to do the contextual work before it reads as old.
- **What makes it distinct:** Data lens (scale, contextualized) crossed with the surprise/contrast news value — the protagonist is the metric and its missing context, not the repair program or the company's structure.
- **Proof it needs:** the time period the 500,000 items accumulated over; total items sold new in that same period, or any denominator the company will release; whether "resold" counts Worn Wear trade-ins only or third-party resale too; someone at the company willing to explain on record why the revenue share isn't disclosed. Without at least the time period, this angle is a bare number and should not be pitched.
- **Facts it rests on:** Worn Wear has resold over 500,000 used items; the company will not say what share of revenue resale represents.

**A company with no shareholders is giving away repairs — nobody else can copy the math**

- **Story type:** counterposition
- **Why a journalist cares:** A business reporter covering ownership structures and corporate governance, at a business or management-focused outlet, who covers how a firm's capital structure changes what it can afford to do. The angle sets Patagonia's private, profit-directed-to-environmental-causes structure against publicly traded apparel peers, and asks whether free repair is a program or a structural privilege. Not for sustainability newsletters that will treat it as inspiration, and not for retail-ops trade press already getting the logistics angle.
- **Why now:** No hard time hook. The ownership structure isn't new; the repair expansion is the occasion. Honest framing: this is a why-this-company-can piece pegged loosely to the announcement, and a reporter will run it when they have room.
- **Decay:** month — structural business analysis with no urgent event under it.
- **What makes it distinct:** Perspective lens with the incumbent/competitor as protagonist — the subject is the publicly traded peer who can't do this, not Patagonia's own program or numbers.
- **Proof it needs:** what free repair actually costs the company annually, or any cost figure at all; an executive willing to say on record that the ownership structure is what makes it affordable; the terms under which profits are directed to environmental causes. Without a cost number this is an assertion about economics with no economics in it.
- **Facts it rests on:** Patagonia is privately held and directs profits to environmental causes; basic repairs are free and complex repairs are charged at cost; no new apparel line announced.

**Refused angles**

- "Patagonia revolutionizes sustainable fashion with nationwide repair" — `slop`.
- "Patagonia's repair expansion signals the death of fast fashion" — `hallucinated_fact`. Nothing in the update says anything about the fast-fashion market, competitors, or industry direction.
- "Consumers are turning away from buying new — Patagonia proves it" — `hallucinated_fact`. A cumulative resale count with no time period and no comparison to new sales cannot support a consumer-behavior claim.
- "Why Patagonia won't sell you a new jacket" — `hallucinated_fact`. "No new apparel line announced" is the absence of news, not a stated strategy.
- "The sustainability angle on Patagonia's Worn Wear expansion" — `no_journalist_shape`. That's a topic label, not a story with a protagonist.
- "What Patagonia's repair program means for the circular economy" — `duplicate` of the category-creation angle, with a vaguer beat and weaker proof.
- "Patagonia customers can now mail in their gear for free repairs" — `duplicate`. Same story as the first angle, written as the press release instead of the reporting.

**Uncomfortable questions**

- Over what period were those 500,000 items resold? Since Worn Wear launched, or in the last year? Without the denominator and the timeframe, the number is decoration, and a good reporter will say so in print.
- If you won't disclose resale as a share of revenue, what will you disclose? A reporter who asks and gets nothing has a story about the refusal — decide now whether you'd rather have that story or a number.
- What does a "complex" repair actually cost a customer? "At cost" is a phrase, not a price. One worked example makes the program credible; without one it reads as a caveat hiding a fee.
- Is this genuinely new, or was Worn Wear repair already available in most stores? "All US stores" and "nationwide mail-in" only carry news value if the prior state was materially narrower. If it wasn't, a reporter will call this a press release about a press release.
- Do you have a customer who can talk about a specific repair — the item, the fix, the wait? Every angle here is stronger with one concrete garment in it, and I have none.
- I could not check what has already been written about Worn Wear. If this program has been covered repeatedly, the distinctness notes above may be overstating what's new.
