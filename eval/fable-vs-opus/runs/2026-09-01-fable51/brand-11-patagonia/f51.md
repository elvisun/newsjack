**Patagonia puts a repair counter in every US store, free for basic fixes**

- **Story type:** trend
- **Why a journalist cares:** A retail-operations trade reporter who covers store labor, service counters, and what retailers do with floor space beyond selling. The story is repair as a store function: what a "basic" repair is, who does it, how long it takes, and what it costs to staff 70-plus locations for it. Not for consumer fashion desks, climate op-ed writers, or general startup press.
- **Why now:** The nationwide store and mail-in rollout is the hook. No outside news signal was supplied, so the announcement itself is the only clock.
- **Decay:** 24hr, since this is standard company news, though a trade reporter can stretch it to a week if you hand them store-level operating detail.
- **What makes it distinct:** Perspective lens with the operator as protagonist. It is about how the program runs inside a store, not about the customer, the number, or the company's ownership.
- **Proof it needs:** the definition of "basic" versus "complex" repair; a sample at-cost price list; average turnaround time; how many stores offered repair before this expansion and what changed; whether repairs are done in-store or shipped to a central facility.
- **Facts it rests on:** repair available at 70-plus US stores plus nationwide mail-in; basic repairs free, complex ones charged at cost.

**Patagonia says Worn Wear has resold 500,000 items. It won't say what that's worth.**

- **Story type:** data
- **Why a journalist cares:** A resale-market or retail-business reporter at a business outlet who tracks whether branded secondhand programs are revenue lines or marketing costs. The 500,000 figure is the entry point, and the refusal to disclose revenue share is the second paragraph. That reporter will write the story with or without your cooperation. Not for gear reviewers, local business desks, or outdoor lifestyle outlets.
- **Why now:** The cumulative milestone was disclosed alongside the rollout, so it is quotable now. There is no other time hook.
- **Decay:** week, because data and resale-market pieces run on the number's freshness, not on the launch day.
- **What makes it distinct:** Data lens, scale value. The protagonist is the number and what it does or doesn't reveal about the business model. No other kept angle depends on the 500,000 figure.
- **Proof it needs:** the time period over which 500,000 items were resold; a current annual or monthly run rate; average resale price or at least an order of magnitude; a considered on-record line about why revenue share isn't disclosed. Without a timeframe the number is bare and this angle is a topic, not a story.
- **Facts it rests on:** Worn Wear has resold over 500,000 used items; the company will not say what share of revenue resale represents.

**Patagonia's newest store rollout has nothing new to sell**

- **Story type:** contrarian
- **Why a journalist cares:** A business-of-fashion or brand-strategy columnist at a business outlet who writes about how ownership structure shapes retail decisions. The prevailing belief is that apparel brands drive store traffic with new product. The evidence against it here is an all-store rollout with no apparel line attached, free basic repairs, and a private owner that directs profits to environmental causes. Not for retail-ops trade press, gear reviewers, or consumer-service desks.
- **Why now:** The rollout gives the strategy piece a peg, but the argument is not time-pressured on its own.
- **Decay:** week. A strategy column can run after launch day, but the peg fades once the rollout is old news.
- **What makes it distinct:** Inversion lens with the company's ownership and strategy as protagonist. The other angles treat the program as a service or a dataset; this one treats it as a choice that most apparel companies would not make.
- **Proof it needs:** an executive on record confirming that repair, not new product, is the priority this season. "No new apparel line announced" is not the same as "no new apparel line." If new product is coming next month, this angle collapses. Also needed: a plain statement of how the private ownership structure affects the repair economics, from someone who can speak for the company.
- **Facts it rests on:** no new apparel line announced; basic repairs free; Patagonia is privately held and directs profits to environmental causes.

**What Patagonia's free repair offer actually covers, and what it charges for**

- **Story type:** customer-story
- **Why a journalist cares:** A consumer-service or outdoor-gear reporter who writes practical pieces for readers with damaged gear: what qualifies as free, what "at cost" means in dollars, how mail-in works, and how long it takes. Outlet type is an outdoor gear publication's service desk or a consumer-affairs section. Not for business desks, retail-ops trades, or strategy columnists.
- **Why now:** The offer is live at launch, and service pieces run on the day readers can act on them.
- **Decay:** 24hr for the launch window. The service piece has a second life only if a real customer's repair story emerges.
- **What makes it distinct:** Perspective lens with the customer as protagonist, at the bottom rung of the ladder. It answers "what do I do with my torn jacket," not "what does this mean for retail."
- **Proof it needs:** a published price list or at-cost examples; who pays mail-in shipping each way; turnaround time; at least one real customer willing to describe a completed repair. The last item is required for this story type.
- **Facts it rests on:** customers can drop off any Patagonia item at 70-plus US stores or mail it in; basic repairs free, complex ones charged at cost.

**Uncomfortable questions**

- Over what period did Worn Wear resell 500,000 items? Ten years and eighteen months are different stories. Without the timeframe, the data angle is not pitchable.
- Why won't you say what share of revenue resale represents? "We don't break it out" is a defensible line. "We won't say" reads as "it's small." Decide which one you're giving reporters, because they will ask.
- Is the absence of a new apparel line a deliberate choice or just not in this release? If new product ships next month, the contrarian angle is dead and pitching it now damages you.
- What does "at cost" mean in dollars, and who pays mail-in shipping? A service reporter cannot write the piece without a price list.
- Repair was already available somewhere before this. How many stores, and what is actually new beyond "all" and "mail-in"?
- Is anything happening right now in resale or repair news that this speaks to? Nothing was supplied, and I did not invent a peg.
