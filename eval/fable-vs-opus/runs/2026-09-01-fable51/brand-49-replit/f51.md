**The platform schools use to teach coding now writes the code for the student**

- **Story type:** trend
- **Why a journalist cares:** An edtech reporter at an education trade outlet or the education desk of a general tech publication, covering computer-science instruction and classroom tooling. The hook is the contradiction inside Replit's own user base: a browser platform used heavily in education is shipping an agent that removes the act of writing code. This reporter can ask what a coding assignment means when the assigned tool will complete it from a sentence. Not for developer-tools reviewers, SaaS pricing reporters, or general startup desks.
- **Why now:** The agent is launching now, and the same product will land in the accounts schools already use.
- **Decay:** week. The launch is a 24-hour event, but the classroom question stays live through the next term-planning cycle.
- **What makes it distinct:** Perspective lens, customer protagonist, with the surprise news value. Every other kept angle treats the buyer as a hobbyist or a paying user. This one makes the teacher and the student the story.
- **Proof it needs:** whether the agent is enabled on education accounts and whether instructors can disable it; one named instructor, school, or district willing to describe how they plan to handle it; any Replit classroom policy or guidance document.
- **Facts it rests on:** Replit is a browser-based coding platform used heavily in education; the agent writes code, sets up a database, and deploys an app without the user writing code.

**Replit charges by usage for an agent it won't say how often gets the app right**

- **Story type:** defensive-comment
- **Why a journalist cares:** A reporter covering AI business models and software pricing at a business-tech outlet or a SaaS-focused newsletter. Usage-based billing on an agent whose success rate is undisclosed raises a concrete question: does a user pay for the attempts that fail? That reporter will put the question to Replit and to customers. Not for education press, product reviewers, or founder-profile writers.
- **Why now:** The pricing model is new with this launch, so the billing question has a live peg.
- **Decay:** week. Pricing-mechanics stories run in the days after a launch, once early users have bills to talk about.
- **What makes it distinct:** Conflict and relevance news values, with the paying user as protagonist. The category angle below is about whether the market exists. This one is about who carries the cost when the agent misses.
- **Proof it needs:** the published pricing sheet and a plain definition of what counts as billable usage; an on-record answer to whether failed or retried generations are billed; an internal reliability figure, or a stated reason for withholding one. Without the reliability answer, this angle becomes a story written about Replit rather than with it.
- **Facts it rests on:** pricing is usage-based on top of subscriptions; reliability and how often generated apps work without fixes are not disclosed.

**Replit's bet: tens of millions of hobbyists would rather describe an app than code one**

- **Story type:** category-creation
- **Why a journalist cares:** A developer-tools or AI-tools reporter at a tech business outlet who covers the no-code and AI-coding market rather than individual products. The hook is distribution: a company claiming tens of millions of users is repositioning from a place to learn coding into a place where non-engineers ship apps by description. That reporter can test whether "vibe coder" describes a real buyer segment or a marketing label. Not for classroom reporters, pricing reporters, or consumer gadget press.
- **Why now:** The launch is the event. The market thesis has a longer window only if Replit can show non-engineers actually shipping.
- **Decay:** 24hr for the launch itself. The segment question can carry a follow-up piece if usage data appears.
- **What makes it distinct:** Ladder lens, zoomed up to the category. The education angle drops down to one buyer group with a conflict. This one asks whether a new market exists at all.
- **Proof it needs:** a definition of "tens of millions" (registered versus active) and any independent verification; a breakdown of how many users are non-engineers; two or three non-engineer users who built and deployed something with the agent and will describe it.
- **Facts it rests on:** the company says it has tens of millions of users; it positions the agent for non-engineers and "vibe coders"; the agent builds and deploys full applications from a plain-English description.

**Replit's agent built our apps. Here is how many ran without a fix.**

- **Story type:** data
- **Why a journalist cares:** A hands-on reviewer or benchmark writer at a tech outlet that tests AI developer tools against real tasks. The story is the number Replit has not published. A reviewer who runs a fixed set of app descriptions through the agent produces the reliability figure the market wants, and the launch is the reason to run the test now. Not for education press, business-model reporters, or anyone who will not run the tool.
- **Why now:** Reviewers test at launch. A month later the test reads as a retrospective.
- **Decay:** week. Hands-on pieces lag the launch by days, not hours.
- **What makes it distinct:** Data lens, with the product itself as protagonist. The pricing angle asks who pays for failures. This one measures how often failure happens. Both rest on the same disclosed hole, but the reporter, the method, and the outcome are different.
- **Proof it needs:** reviewer access with no curated demo; a willingness to let the result publish whatever it is; ideally an internal reliability figure to compare against. If Replit will not be tested, this angle does not exist as a pitch.
- **Facts it rests on:** the agent writes code, sets up a database, and deploys a working app; reliability and how often generated apps work without fixes are not disclosed.

**Uncomfortable questions**

- How often does a generated app run without manual fixes? This one number decides whether the pricing angle is a story you tell or a story told about you, and whether the hands-on angle exists at all.
- Does usage billing count failed or retried generations? A reporter will ask on day one.
- Is the agent live on education accounts, and can instructors turn it off? If you have no answer, the education angle is a liability, not a pitch.
- Is "tens of millions" registered or active users, and will you show any evidence beyond the company's own statement?
- Can you name two non-engineers who built and deployed a real app with it, or is "vibe coder" a segment you hope exists?
- Is "vibe coder" your coinage or a term already in use? Nothing in the update lets me treat it as a live news peg.
