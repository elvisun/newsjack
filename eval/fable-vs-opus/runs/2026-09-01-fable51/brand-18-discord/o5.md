**Discord takes 10% where Apple takes 30% — a platform fee argument aimed at the app stores**

- **Story type:** counterposition
- **Why a journalist cares:** A platform-economics or app-store reporter at a tech policy or business outlet covering the fee wars — the people who have been writing about commission rates, sideloading, and payment rules for years. The number is the story: a large consumer platform publicly pricing itself at a third of the app-store standard is a data point in an argument that's already running. This reporter will want to know whether Discord's 10% is net of payment processing, and whether the subscriptions flow through mobile in-app purchase at all — which is where the 30% actually bites. Not for gaming-culture writers, creator-lifestyle desks, or consumer product reviewers.
- **Why now:** The launch is live today, and the fee comparison is the one claim in the announcement that a reporter can check against a public standard without waiting for earnings data.
- **Decay:** 24hr — the launch peg is a one-day event; the fee-comparison framing lasts longer only if a reporter can get Discord on record about mobile payment flows.
- **What makes it distinct:** Perspective lens, competitor/incumbent protagonist. The story here is the app stores and the fee stack, not Discord's creators or Discord's balance sheet. Every other angle in this set has a different protagonist.
- **Proof it needs:** whether the 10% is gross or net of payment processing; how the subscription is billed on iOS and Android and who collects there; whether the 10% is introductory or permanent; an on-record executive who will defend the number under a follow-up question. Without the mobile-billing answer, the comparison is not apples to apples and a good reporter will say so.
- **Facts it rests on:** Discord takes a 10% platform fee, below the 30% app-store standard; server owners can charge monthly fees for premium roles and content.
