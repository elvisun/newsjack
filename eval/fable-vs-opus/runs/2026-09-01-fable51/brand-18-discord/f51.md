**Discord undercuts the 30% app-store cut with a 10% fee on paid communities**

- **Story type:** counterposition
- **Why a journalist cares:** A platform-economics reporter at a tech business outlet or a newsletter that covers app-store fees, take rates, and developer/creator payouts. The story is the number, not the feature: a large platform pricing its take rate at a third of the app-store norm and saying so out loud. This beat has been tracking take-rate pressure for years and will want to know whether 10% is a real all-in rate or a headline that collapses on mobile. Not for gaming culture press, community-management trades, or general consumer-tech reviewers.
- **Why now:** The launch is live today and the fee comparison is Discord's own framing, so the beat can react immediately.
- **Decay:** 24hr for the launch reaction, stretching to a week if the fee-structure question below gets a real answer.
- **What makes it distinct:** Conflict lens with the incumbent fee model as the antagonist. The protagonist is the fee, not the creator, the investor, or the category.
- **Proof it needs:** written confirmation of whether the 10% applies to subscriptions bought inside the iOS and Android apps, or whether app-store fees stack on top; whether payment processing is included in the 10% or charged separately; an on-record Discord statement on why 10%.
- **Facts it rests on:** Discord takes a 10% platform fee, below the 30% app-store standard; server owners can charge monthly fees for premium roles and content.

**Discord adds a revenue line ahead of a reported IPO**

- **Story type:** funding-mechanics
- **Why a journalist cares:** An IPO or private-markets reporter at a financial outlet or a fintech/markets newsletter covering pre-listing companies. The question this reporter will ask is whether a creator-subscription take rate meaningfully diversifies revenue for a company that is reportedly preparing to go public, and how that reads to prospective investors. Not for creator-economy trades, gaming press, or product reviewers.
- **Why now:** The launch lands while the IPO reporting is live, which is the only reason this beat looks at a product feature at all.
- **Decay:** week. Markets reporters can run the diversification question after launch day if there is something on record to test.
- **What makes it distinct:** Perspective lens, investor protagonist. The story is what this does to Discord's financial profile, not what it does for creators.
- **Proof it needs:** confirmation of whether the IPO preparation is on record or press speculation, and whether Discord is in a quiet period that limits comment; any stated or disclosed revenue mix; a named executive able to speak to monetization strategy on record.
- **Facts it rests on:** Discord is privately held and reportedly preparing for an IPO; Discord takes a 10% platform fee; the company says it has over 200 million monthly active users.

**What a gaming-community server can now charge for, and what stays free**

- **Story type:** customer-story
- **Why a journalist cares:** A creator-economy or games-business reporter at a trade outlet covering how community moderators and streamers actually make money. The concrete rung: one named server owner, what they gated behind a paid role, what they priced it at, and what the free tier still gets. This is the only angle that shows the mechanics from the seller's side. Not for markets reporters, app-store policy reporters, or general business desks.
- **Why now:** The program just launched, so early adopters are new and unreported. Without a named server owner there is no story here, only the announcement.
- **Decay:** week. The launch gives a reason to run now, but a good early-adopter story holds for a few weeks if the numbers are real.
- **What makes it distinct:** Ladder lens, zoomed all the way down to one server. Every other kept angle is about the platform or the market.
- **Proof it needs:** one named server owner who has enabled paid roles and will describe pricing, what is gated, and early subscriber counts; screenshots or a live link showing the paid tier; confirmation the program is available to that server's region and size.
- **Facts it rests on:** server owners can charge monthly fees for premium roles and content; the feature targets the creator and gaming-community economy; no creator-earnings data is available yet.

**The community server becomes the creator's paywall**

- **Story type:** trend
- **Why a journalist cares:** A creator-economy reporter at a business or media-industry outlet who covers where creator revenue is moving. The altitude here is the category: a chat platform built around communities is now a direct-monetization rail, which shifts the question from "which feed pays best" to "can a private community be the product." Not for gaming trades that want mechanics, or markets reporters who want the IPO.
- **Why now:** The launch is the evidence point. The trend framing only works if the reporter can pair it with comparable data on other creator-subscription platforms, which you have to supply or they have to find.
- **Decay:** week. Trend pieces run on the launch's coattails but need more than one company to stand up.
- **What makes it distinct:** Ladder lens, zoomed up to the market. Distinct from the counterposition angle because the fee is a detail here, not the story, and distinct from the customer story because no single server is the protagonist.
- **Proof it needs:** Discord's own definition of who counts as a creator or community for this program; comparable take-rate and creator-earnings data from other subscription platforms with sources; a Discord product lead on record about why communities rather than individual creators are the unit.
- **Facts it rests on:** Discord is launching paid creator subscriptions and a revenue-share program for community servers; the feature targets the creator and gaming-community economy; the company says it has over 200 million monthly active users.

**Uncomfortable questions**

- Does the 10% hold for subscriptions bought inside the iOS and Android apps, or do Apple and Google take their cut on top? If the effective mobile rate is closer to 40%, the headline number is the first thing a reporter will dismantle.
- Is the IPO preparation on record from Discord, or only reported? If the company is in a quiet period, who is allowed to speak, and about what?
- Can you name one server owner who has switched this on today and will share pricing and subscriber counts? Without that, the customer angle is an announcement, not a story.
- What is the source and date for the 200 million monthly active users figure, and how many of those are server owners eligible for the program?
- Is the program available to every server, or gated by size, region, or invitation? Reporters will test the "community servers" claim against who can actually use it.
- Is payment processing inside the 10% or billed separately? "Below the app-store standard" only survives if the comparison is like-for-like.
