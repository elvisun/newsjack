**Discord undercuts the 30% app-store toll with a 10% cut for paid communities**

- **Story type:** counterposition
- **Why a journalist cares:** A platform-economics reporter at a tech business outlet covering take rates and app-store fee fights. The story isn't the feature — it's the fee. Discord pricing its cut at 10%, a third of the 30% app-store standard, is a direct shot at the prevailing platform-fee structure, and that's a beat with an active, ongoing argument. Not for gaming culture writers, consumer how-to outlets, or general startup blogs.
- **Why now:** The program is launching now; the fee comparison only matters while the launch is the news.
- **Decay:** 24hr — standard launch-news cycle; the fee-structure debate gives it a small tail but the peg is the announcement.
- **What makes it distinct:** Conflict lens — the protagonist is the fee structure and the incumbents it cuts against, not Discord's product or its users. The other kept angles are about the IPO math, the market category, and the server owners.
- **Proof it needs:** confirmation the 10% is the all-in take (does it include payment-processing costs, or is processing on top?); whether the rate is permanent or introductory; an on-record Discord statement on why 10% specifically.
- **Facts it rests on:** Discord takes a 10% platform fee, below the 30% app-store standard; server owners can charge monthly fees for premium roles and content.
