**Figma puts its AI code tool behind a new $25 seat**

- **Story type:** trend
- **Why a journalist cares:** A SaaS-business reporter at an enterprise-software trade outlet or subscription-economics newsletter who covers how vendors monetize AI features. The hook is the packaging, not the product: Make is a separate $25/user/month seat stacked on top of existing plans, at a company that says it has over 4 million users and sits inside most of the Fortune 500. That is a procurement story for every design and engineering org already paying Figma. Not for consumer-tech reviewers, design-craft publications, or general startup blogs.
- **Why now:** The launch is live today, and the pricing is new information buyers have to react to.
- **Decay:** week — the launch itself is a 24-hour event, but the add-on-seat pricing question keeps running as buyers and rivals respond.
- **What makes it distinct:** News-values lens, relevance plus magnitude. The protagonist is the buyer's budget, not the product, the developer, or Figma's corporate future. The other angles never touch price.
- **Proof it needs:** the official pricing page or rate card confirming the seat is additive rather than a plan upgrade; what a Make seat unlocks that existing seats do not; whether the AI assistant is included in the $25 or in base plans; an on-record answer on volume or enterprise discounts.
- **Facts it rests on:** pricing adds a new "Make" seat at $25/user/month on top of existing plans; Figma says it has over 4 million users and is used at most Fortune 500 companies.

**Figma's Make exports React, moving the design tool into the developer's toolchain**

- **Story type:** counterposition
- **Why a journalist cares:** A developer-tools reporter at an engineering-focused outlet or front-end newsletter who covers the design-to-code handoff. Design-to-code has been the seam between two professions and two toolsets; a tool that emits React and HTML/CSS directly from the design file claims that seam. This reporter will test one thing: is the output production-grade or a prototype generator? Not for design-craft press, business-of-SaaS desks, or markets reporters.
- **Why now:** Launch day. The code export is the concrete, testable claim, and reporters can evaluate it immediately.
- **Decay:** week — the announcement fades in a day, but a hands-on "we shipped the code, here's what it looked like" piece stays live as long as the tool is new.
- **What makes it distinct:** Perspective lens, developer as protagonist, plus the conflict value. The story is whether Figma is displacing the tools and steps engineers use after handoff. The designer angle below is about the people upstream of the code; this one is about the people downstream.
- **Proof it needs:** a demo or sandbox account so a reporter can export a real file; sample React output the company will stand behind; an engineering lead willing to explain what the export does not handle (state, data binding, accessibility, design tokens); ideally an engineer at a customer who put the code into a real repo.
- **Facts it rests on:** Make exports React and HTML/CSS from a Figma file; Figma is used at most Fortune 500 companies.

The launch is the next chapter of a story they already wrote: a company that was supposed to be absorbed is instead shipping a product that expands its addressable seat count, while saying nothing about IPO timing. That silence is part of the story. Not for developer press, design-trade outlets, or product-review sites.
- **Why now:** Launch day gives a reporter a legitimate reason to ask the "what is Figma now" question on record. Caveat: the date of the Adobe collapse was not supplied, so how warm that peg still is cannot be judged from these facts.
- **Decay:** 24hr — the corporate-path question is anchored to the launch news cycle, and the IPO non-answer gets stale fast unless an executive says something new.
- **What makes it distinct:** Follow-up news value plus the investor-and-money protagonist. It is the only angle where the product is context and the company's independence is the story.
- **Proof it needs:** the date and terms of the Adobe collapse; an on-record executive comment on the standalone strategy and on IPO timing, even if the answer is "no comment"; any disclosed figures on revenue or paying seats, since "4 million users" says nothing about paying customers.
- **Facts it rests on:** the launch follows the collapse of its acquisition by Adobe; no IPO timing disclosed; Figma says it has over 4 million users.

**Figma's AI now draws the layout from a prompt. What that means for the people who used to draw it**

- **Story type:** trend
- **Why a journalist cares:** A design-industry reporter at a UX or design-trade outlet who covers how AI is changing design work, not just design tools. A text prompt that generates layouts inside the tool most of the profession already uses is a different thing from a standalone AI demo: it lands on 4 million desks. The beat cares because its readers are the ones whose work is being generated. Not for markets reporters, enterprise-procurement press, or dev-tools writers.
- **Why now:** Launch day. The assistant is shipping inside a tool designers already use, so the question is practical this week, not hypothetical.
- **Decay:** week — the labor-and-craft conversation outlasts the announcement but needs the launch to justify running now.
- **What makes it distinct:** Perspective lens, practitioner as protagonist, plus the good-news/bad-news value (faster work, fewer hours billed for it). The counterposition angle is about engineers; this is about designers, and it turns on the assistant, not the code export.
- **Proof it needs:** a walkthrough of what the assistant actually produces from a prompt and what it cannot do; a working designer who has used it and will describe the change in their process; Figma's own position on whether it augments or replaces layout work.
- **Facts it rests on:** the AI assistant generates layouts from a text prompt; Figma says it has over 4 million users.

**Uncomfortable questions**

- When did the Adobe acquisition collapse? If it was months ago, the standalone-company angle is a stale peg and should be cut to a sentence of context in the other pitches.
- Is there a real Make customer willing to be named, with code that reached production? Without one, the developer angle is a demo review, and the strongest enterprise story cannot be written.
- How many of the 4 million users pay, and how many Fortune 500 relationships are paid enterprise contracts versus free individual accounts? Reporters will ask; the pricing angle collapses if the answer is unknown.
- What does exported React actually contain? If it is static markup with no state or data binding, say so before a reporter discovers it.
- Will an executive answer the IPO question on record, even with "no comment"? A pitch that dangles the topic and then dodges it reads as bait.
- No current news signal was supplied. Every decay tag here rests on the launch itself, not on anything breaking in the wider market.
