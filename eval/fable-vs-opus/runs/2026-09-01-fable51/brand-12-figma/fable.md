**Figma Make turns design files into React — and squeezes the designer-developer handoff**

- **Story type:** trend
- **Why a journalist cares:** A reporter covering AI's effect on software work — dev-tools trade outlet or a tech outlet's future-of-engineering beat. The honest hook is structural: if a design file exports working React and HTML/CSS, the handoff between designers and front-end engineers — a whole job boundary — is what's being automated. This reporter can call working engineers and designers and test whether the output is production-grade or scaffolding. Not for business/M&A reporters, consumer tech press, or general AI hype desks.
- **Why now:** The launch is the peg; the handoff question has a longer window because it's about the work, not the announcement.
- **Decay:** week — the launch dates quickly but the labor-boundary question supports a reported trend piece for days after.
- **What makes it distinct:** Conflict lens plus ladder zoomed up — the story is what happens between two job functions, not Figma's corporate arc (angle 1) or its price list (angle 3).
- **Proof it needs:** a demo or sample export a skeptical engineer can inspect; an honest answer on whether exported code is shippable or a starting point; ideally one team willing to describe how the handoff actually changed. Without the code-quality proof this angle gets torn apart on contact.
- **Facts it rests on:** Make exports React and HTML/CSS from a Figma file; the AI assistant generates layouts from a text prompt.
