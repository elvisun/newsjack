## Uncomfortable questions

1. **What is the rental price?** Without a monthly number, the customer-story angle cannot be pitched with conviction. "Low-cost" is a claim, not an anchor.
2. **What specifically did the CEO cut?** "Over $200M in annual costs" is a result, not a story. The exec-spotlight angle collapses without knowing whether this was layoffs, content, retail, hardware R&D, or some combination — and what sequence the cuts happened in.
3. **What is the actual debt figure and when does it mature?** The accounting-quality angle depends on whether "significant debt" is a manageable refinancing situation or a near-term solvency question. Those are different stories.
4. **Is the 6 million member count growing or down from the pandemic peak?** This materially changes both the contrarian and the churn angle. "6 million still" is a different story than "6 million and growing." The facts don't say.
5. **What is churn as a percentage?** "Record low" is relative; without a number, a journalist cannot assess whether this is a material improvement or a statistically small move off a bad baseline.
