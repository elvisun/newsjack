**Peloton would rather rent you a bike than sell you one**

- **Story type:** trend
- **Why a journalist cares:** A consumer-subscription or retail-business reporter at a business outlet or consumer-tech trade covering the shift from selling hardware to renting it. The hook is not the earnings line; it is a hardware company taking a nationwide bet that no-commitment rental beats a big upfront purchase. This reporter can test whether rental is a growth engine or a way to move unsold inventory. Not for fitness-lifestyle press, markets desks, or general startup blogs.
- **Why now:** The nationwide expansion is announced alongside the first profitable quarter in three years, so the company is scaling the program from a position it can call strength.
- **Decay:** week. The expansion itself is a 24-hour item, but the rent-versus-own thesis holds for a week of follow-up pieces.
- **What makes it distinct:** Ladder lens, zoomed up. The category and the business model are the protagonist, not the quarter's numbers, the CEO, or the debt.
- **Proof it needs:** the monthly rental price and what the bundle includes; how many markets the program was in before and how many it reaches now; rental sign-ups or the share of new members coming through rental; what happens to a returned bike; whether rental members churn at a different rate from purchasers.
- **Facts it rests on:** the rental program bundles a bike and membership for one monthly fee with no commitment; it is expanding nationwide; the quarter was the first profitable one in three years on an adjusted basis.

**Peloton is generating cash again. Its debt is still the story**

- **Story type:** funding-mechanics
- **Why a journalist cares:** An earnings or credit reporter at a financial outlet who covers consumer-hardware balance sheets and distressed-to-recovering names. This beat will separate adjusted EBITDA and free cash flow from net profit within one paragraph, so the honest story is the gap: positive cash generation against a net loss and a significant debt load. Not for fitness press, product reviewers, or local business desks.
- **Why now:** The quarter just closed with positive adjusted EBITDA and free cash flow for the first time in three years, which changes the debt conversation from survival to servicing.
- **Decay:** 24hr. Earnings reaction is a same-day cycle, but no supplied signal justifies a tighter tag. If the results posted today, move fast; if they are older than a day, this angle is already thinning.
- **What makes it distinct:** Perspective lens, investor and creditor protagonist. The money is the story, and the angle openly carries the net loss and debt rather than burying them.
- **Proof it needs:** the actual adjusted EBITDA and free cash flow figures; the net loss figure; total debt, maturities, and interest cost; whether management gave any refinancing or paydown guidance on record.
- **Facts it rests on:** positive adjusted EBITDA and free cash flow for the quarter; first profitable quarter in three years; the company remains unprofitable on a net basis and carries significant debt.

**Peloton's members are staying: churn hits a record low as base tops 6 million**

- **Story type:** data
- **Why a journalist cares:** A subscription-economy or DTC-metrics reporter at a business outlet or a retention-focused trade newsletter. Record-low churn on a base above 6 million is the one number here that speaks to the durability of the business rather than one quarter's cost line. This reporter will ask what changed and whether the base is growing or just leaving more slowly. Not for markets desks focused on the debt, and not for consumer fitness press.
- **Why now:** The churn figure is a record low for this reporting period and is being disclosed alongside the profitable quarter.
- **Decay:** week. Retention data supports analysis pieces beyond the earnings day.
- **What makes it distinct:** Data lens, change-over-time lead. The metric is the protagonist. The rental angle is about a product bet; this is about whether existing members stay.
- **Proof it needs:** the actual churn rate and the prior-period and prior-year comparisons; how Peloton defines churn and whether the definition changed; whether "members" includes app-only or paused accounts; net member adds versus gross adds.
- **Facts it rests on:** subscriber churn fell to a record low; total members exceed 6 million; first profitable quarter in three years.

**The $200 million diet: how Peloton's new CEO reached an adjusted profit**

- **Story type:** exec-spotlight
- **Why a journalist cares:** A management or leadership reporter at a business outlet who covers corporate turnarounds and new-CEO first-year scorecards. The story is what got cut, in what order, and what the CEO chose not to cut. Not for fitness press, and not for credit reporters who want the debt story instead.
- **Why now:** The first profitable quarter in three years is the first hard evidence the cost program worked, which gives the CEO tenure a natural checkpoint.
- **Decay:** week. Turnaround profiles run for a week or more after the earnings date if the executive is available.
- **What makes it distinct:** Perspective lens, operator protagonist. The person and their decisions are the story rather than the model, the metric, or the balance sheet.
- **Proof it needs:** the CEO's name and start date; a breakdown of where the $200M-plus in annual savings came from, including headcount, real estate, marketing, and supply chain; an on-record CEO interview or quote; what the next cost targets are, if any.
- **Facts it rests on:** Peloton cut over $200M in annual costs under a new CEO; the quarter was the first profitable one in three years on an adjusted basis.

**Refused angles**

- "Peloton is profitable again" — `hallucinated_fact`. The company is unprofitable on a net basis. Any headline that drops the word "adjusted" is false.
- "Adjusted profit is not profit: a Peloton earnings fact-check" — `duplicate`. Same money protagonist as the cash-and-debt angle, with a weaker shape. Folded into that angle's proof list.
- "Connected fitness is not dead after all" — `hallucinated_fact`. A contrarian angle needs a documented prevailing belief and category-wide evidence. Neither is in the facts.
- "Meet the renters: who pays monthly for a Peloton with no strings" — `hallucinated_fact`. No customer, price, or rental demographic was supplied. Any renter profile would be invented.
- "6 million members is more than [comparison]" — `hallucinated_fact`. A scale angle needs context to compare against, and none was provided.
- "Peloton's cost cuts hit [city or plant]" — `hallucinated_fact`. No facts on where or what was cut.
- "Peloton outpaces rivals in the fitness-hardware race" — `hallucinated_fact`. No competitor data or current news signal was supplied.
- "Peloton transforms the future of home fitness" — `slop`.

**Uncomfortable questions**

- When were these results actually released? Every decay tag above assumes today. If the report is days old, the earnings angle is gone and only the rental and churn angles survive.
- The rental program has no commitment. Record-low churn is the headline metric. Do rental members churn faster than purchasers, and does the churn figure include them? If the answer is unfavorable, the two strongest angles undercut each other.
- Is the "first profitable quarter in three years" claim based only on adjusted EBITDA? A reporter will ask whether any quarter in that period had positive free cash flow already.
- How large is the debt relative to the quarter's free cash flow? If the answer is embarrassing, the money angle will get written with or without you, and it is better to supply the numbers.
- Will the CEO speak on record about specific cuts, including layoffs? Without that, the exec-spotlight angle is a press release with a name on it.
