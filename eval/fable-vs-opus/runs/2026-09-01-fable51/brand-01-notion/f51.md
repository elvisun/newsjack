**Notion says four of the five largest US banks are piloting it after it added customer-held encryption keys**

- **Story type:** customer-story
- **Why a journalist cares:** A bank-technology trade reporter covering how large US banks procure and vet SaaS. The story is the buyer, not the feature list: what a top-five bank's security review demanded before a tool with a consumer footprint could get a pilot, and what a pilot at that tier actually covers. Not for consumer productivity reviewers, general startup press, or developer outlets.
- **Why now:** The features that the company says unblocked these pilots shipped today, so the bank pilots have a fresh reason to be reported now rather than as an old customer list.
- **Decay:** 24hr for the launch hook; if a bank goes on record, the story holds for a week.
- **What makes it distinct:** Perspective lens, customer protagonist, bottom rung of the ladder. The other kept angles are about the compliance category, the regional rules, and the engineering. This one lives or dies on one named bank.
- **Proof it needs:** at least one of the four banks confirming the pilot on record, or Notion's written permission to describe the pilot with the bank's sign-off; pilot scope (seats, business units, timeline); what the bank's security review required. Without a bank willing to be identified, this becomes a claim the reporter cannot check and the angle should be shelved.
- **Facts it rests on:** four of the five largest US banks now run pilots; enterprise customers can hold their own encryption keys via AWS KMS; the company says this unblocks regulated buyers in finance and healthcare that previously could not adopt.

**Notion's 100 million users could not get it past bank and hospital compliance. It just shipped the checklist.**

- **Story type:** trend
- **Why a journalist cares:** An enterprise IT or security trade reporter covering the gap between bottom-up SaaS adoption and regulated procurement. The contrast is the story: enormous user scale on one side, a locked door in finance and healthcare on the other, and a specific set of controls (customer-managed keys, data residency, offline) built to open it. Not for bank-tech reporters who want the bank as protagonist, and not for consumer tech press.
- **Why now:** The controls shipped today. The broader "consumer-scale tools hitting the compliance wall" thread has a longer window if Notion can show what regulated buyers actually blocked on.
- **Decay:** week. The launch is a one-day event; the segment story runs for about a week if the proof arrives.
- **What makes it distinct:** Ladder lens, zoomed up. The regulated-buyer *category* is the protagonist, and the scale figure supplies the contrast. The bank angle above is the same fact zoomed down to one customer; this one is the market thesis.
- **Proof it needs:** specific examples of what regulated buyers previously blocked on, from Notion or from a buyer; evidence that healthcare buyers exist, since the only cited pilots are banks; clarity on whether the controls are included in existing Enterprise pricing or sold as add-ons for new buyers.
- **Facts it rests on:** ~100M users; customer-managed keys via AWS KMS; data residency options for the EU and Australia; the company says this unblocks regulated buyers in finance and healthcare that previously could not adopt; no pricing change for existing Enterprise plans.

**Notion adds EU and Australian data residency for regulated buyers**

- **Story type:** trend
- **Why a journalist cares:** A data-sovereignty or privacy-policy reporter at an EU or Australian business or IT outlet. The beat's standing questions are concrete: where the data sits, who can access it, whether keys held via a US cloud provider satisfy local expectations, and whether residency covers processing or only storage. Notion is a useful case because it now has a stated answer. Not for US general tech press or bank-tech reporters.
- **Why now:** The residency options launched today. There is no supplied regulatory event to peg this to, and none should be implied.
- **Decay:** 24hr for the launch; the sovereignty questions are longer-lived but need a real regional hook to run past that.
- **What makes it distinct:** Perspective lens, regulator-and-rules protagonist, with a regional audience the other angles do not serve. The compliance angle above is about US finance and healthcare buyers; this one is about jurisdiction.
- **Proof it needs:** which regions host the data and whether processing stays in-region; which regulatory frameworks the residency and key controls are designed to satisfy, stated by Notion, not inferred; whether AWS KMS key custody is available in-region for EU and Australian customers.
- **Facts it rests on:** data residency options added for the EU and Australia; enterprise customers can hold their own encryption keys via AWS KMS.

Not for compliance, banking, or policy reporters.

**Refused angles**

- "Notion beats the incumbent enterprise suites inside the banks" — `hallucinated_fact`. No competitor or incumbent appears in the facts, and nothing says what those banks use today.
- "Notion ships enterprise security without the security tax" — `hallucinated_fact`. The facts say no pricing change for *existing* Enterprise plans. They do not say the new controls are included at no cost for anyone, and they do not say what new buyers pay.
- "Offline-first is a rebuke to the always-online cloud" — `no_why_now_but_required`. A contrarian angle needs a real prevailing belief and evidence against it. The facts give a feature, not evidence about the industry, so this is performance.
- "Notion unlocks healthcare" as its own angle — `duplicate`. Same lens and same protagonist as the regulated-buyer angle, with weaker proof: the only cited pilots are banks, and no healthcare customer is given.
- "100 million users" as a standalone scale story — `no_journalist_shape`. A bare number is not a story. It survives only as the contrast inside the regulated-buyer angle.
- "Notion's founders bring collaboration to Wall Street" — `hallucinated_fact`. No founder or executive facts were supplied.
- A news-peg angle tied to a current regulation or breach — `no_why_now_but_required`. No signal was supplied, and a reporter will see a reached-for peg in one read.

**Uncomfortable questions**

- Will any of the four banks confirm the pilot on record, or allow itself to be named? If not, the strongest angle is a claim reporters cannot check.
- What does "pilot" mean at each bank: a team of ten, or a business unit? A reporter will ask, and "pilot" at a top-five bank can be very small.
- Are customer-managed keys, residency, and offline included for all Enterprise customers, or are they add-ons for new buyers? "No pricing change for existing plans" leaves that open.
- When a bank employee's laptop with offline Notion data is lost, what protects that local cache, and is it covered by the customer's keys? If there is no crisp answer, the offline and compliance angles conflict with each other.
- Is there a single healthcare customer or pilot you can point to? "Finance and healthcare" is currently half-supported.
- Does EU and Australian residency cover processing and key custody in-region, or storage only?
