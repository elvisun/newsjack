**Chipotle says it won't raise menu prices this year, even as its food costs climb**

- **Story type:** contrarian
- **Why a journalist cares:** A consumer-prices or food-inflation reporter at a national business outlet or a personal-finance desk that tracks what a burrito costs. The prevailing belief is that chains pass food-cost inflation straight to the menu. A large chain committing on record to a year of flat prices is evidence against that, and the reporter can test whether the commitment has conditions. Not for restaurant-technology trades, labor reporters, or local news.
- **Why now:** The commitment is being made now, in the same breath as a robot rollout. Reporters will write the price story the day it lands.
- **Decay:** 24hr. A price commitment is standard company news; after the first cycle it only comes back if a competitor raises prices or Chipotle breaks the promise.
- **What makes it distinct:** News-values lens, surprise and contrast, with the diner as protagonist. Every other angle is about the kitchen or the P&L. This one is about the receipt.
- **Proof it needs:** An on-record executive statement of the commitment with its exact scope (all markets, delivery menus, all sizes); whether it is unconditional or has a food-cost escape hatch; the user's own evidence of food-cost inflation in Chipotle's basket, since the reporter will ask what "despite inflation" means in numbers.
- **Facts it rests on:** Chipotle says it will not raise menu prices in the coming year despite food-cost inflation; the chain has over 3,500 locations.

**The guacamole robot is coming to 100 Chipotles. The people who made guac are headed to the counter.**

- **Story type:** trend
- **Why a journalist cares:** A labor or workforce reporter at a restaurant-industry trade, or a workplace correspondent at a business outlet covering automation and frontline jobs. The claim "no jobs cut, staff redeployed" is the exact claim this beat is paid to test. Chipotle's size makes it a usable case study for what redeployment means in practice. Not for consumer-prices reporters, equities reporters, or gadget-focused tech press.
- **Why now:** The rollout is moving from pilot to 100 stores, so the redeployment claim is about to be tested at scale rather than in a few kitchens.
- **Decay:** week. The automation-and-jobs question runs as a context piece well after the rollout announcement, as long as a real worker or manager can speak.
- **What makes it distinct:** Perspective lens, worker protagonist. The rollout-math angle treats the robot as the story; this one treats the person who used to hold the knife as the story.
- **Proof it needs:** What "front line" concretely means (roles, shifts); whether scheduled hours per store change even if headcount does not; a pilot-store manager or crew member willing to describe the change on record; a plain answer on whether labor-cost savings will ever be disclosed, because the reporter will ask.
- **Facts it rests on:** No jobs will be cut, the company says, with staff redeployed to the front line; Autocado is rolling out to 100 restaurants; labor-cost figures not disclosed.

**Chipotle's avocado robot goes from pilot to 100 stores, still under 3% of the chain**

- **Story type:** data
- **Why a journalist cares:** A restaurant-technology or back-of-house operations reporter at a foodservice trade covering kitchen automation deployments. This beat has watched kitchen robots stall at the demo stage. Chipotle's numbers let the reporter size it honestly: prep time roughly halved in pilots, 100 stores out of more than 3,500, and a second system (Digital Makeline) still in test. That is both "a real deployment" and "still an experiment," and the reporter gets to say which. Not for consumer press, labor reporters, or general business desks.
- **Why now:** The move from pilot to a 100-store rollout is the event. The prep-time figure is only news attached to that rollout.
- **Decay:** 24hr. Rollout news is standard company news; the throughput data keeps a slightly longer tail only if the user can supply real pilot numbers.
- **What makes it distinct:** Magnitude lens, contextualized. This is the only angle where the numbers are the protagonist. It also absorbs the zoom-down version (one pilot kitchen's prep shift), which is the same story at a lower rung.
- **Proof it needs:** Actual before-and-after prep minutes and avocado volume per shift from the pilot kitchens, not "roughly halves"; the number of pilot stores and how long they ran; the rollout timeline for the 100 stores; what stage the Digital Makeline test is at and in how many stores.
- **Facts it rests on:** Autocado roughly halves guacamole prep time in pilot kitchens; rollout to 100 restaurants; the chain has over 3,500 locations; the Digital Makeline bowl-assembly system is in testing.

**Flat prices, rising food costs, undisclosed labor savings: the math behind Chipotle's freeze**

- **Story type:** funding-mechanics
- **Why a journalist cares:** A restaurant-sector markets or equities reporter at a financial outlet who covers margins, not menus. The reporter can do the arithmetic from the supplied facts alone: 100 robot-equipped stores out of more than 3,500 cannot offset chain-wide food-cost inflation, so a year of flat prices is being funded by something else, and the company has not said what. That is a skeptical story, and it gets written whether or not Chipotle participates. Participating with a real answer is the pitch. Not for restaurant trades, consumer reporters, or labor desks.
- **Why now:** The price commitment and the rollout land together, which is what invites the question. Without an earnings event there is no harder clock.
- **Decay:** week. Margin-mechanics pieces run as analysis over the following days rather than as same-day news.
- **What makes it distinct:** Perspective lens, money protagonist. The consumer-prices angle asks what the diner pays. This one asks how the company can afford it. Same two facts, opposite reader, opposite question.
- **Proof it needs:** A CFO or finance executive on record explaining how the freeze is funded (mix, volume, procurement, automation, or margin compression); any disclosed capital cost per Autocado unit; a decision on whether labor-cost figures will be disclosed, because "not disclosed" is the hole this reporter will lead with.
- **Facts it rests on:** Chipotle will not raise menu prices in the coming year despite food-cost inflation; Autocado is rolling out to 100 restaurants; over 3,500 locations; labor-cost figures not disclosed.

**Refused angles**

- "Robots are paying for Chipotle's price freeze" — `hallucinated_fact`. The update lists the rollout and the price commitment side by side but never links them causally. A headline that asserts the link is a claim Chipotle has not made.
- "Chipotle revolutionizes the fast-casual kitchen with cutting-edge robotics" — `slop`. Rewrote it once, it became the rollout-math angle above, so the original dies.
- "Chipotle beats rival chains to kitchen automation at scale" — `hallucinated_fact`. No competitor facts were supplied, so there is no counterposition to write.
- "Chipotle's avocado robot answers the avocado supply squeeze" — `hallucinated_fact`. No current supply or price signal was handed in. A news-peg angle without a signal is forced relevance.
- "Inside one pilot kitchen's morning prep shift after Autocado" — `duplicate`. Same lens and protagonist as the rollout-math angle at a lower rung; folded into it.
- "Chipotle's next robot builds the bowl: the Digital Makeline" — `no_why_now_but_required`. The only fact is "testing." No store count, no timeline, no result, so nothing for a reporter to write this week.
- "The executive behind Chipotle's automation push" — `hallucinated_fact`. No executive is named in the update; an exec-spotlight with no exec is invented.
- "Which 100 restaurants are getting the robot" — `no_journalist_shape`. A real local-news angle if the store list existed, but without cities there is no beat to hand it to.

**Uncomfortable questions**

- Does Chipotle itself connect the price freeze to automation? If not, two of the four angles rest on an inference reporters will make without you, and you need a scripted answer for it.
- Is the price commitment unconditional, and does it cover every market and channel? "Will not raise prices" reads differently if there is a food-cost trigger buried in it.
- "No jobs cut" is a headcount claim. Do scheduled hours per store change? A labor reporter will ask a crew member, not a spokesperson.
- Why are labor-cost figures undisclosed, and will that hold through the rollout? "Not disclosed" is the lede for the markets angle whether you pitch it or not.
- What did the pilots actually measure? "Roughly halves" will not survive a data reporter's first follow-up question without minutes and volumes.
- Can a pilot-store manager or crew member speak on record? Without one, the labor angle is a corporate assertion, not a story.

**Next step:** `journalist-fit-check`. Four distinct shapes exist. Pick one, then resolve real names against it. Do not pitch the price-freeze or margin angles until a finance executive has answered the funding question on record.
