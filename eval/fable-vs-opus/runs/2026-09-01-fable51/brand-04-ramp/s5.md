**Ramp's new AI agent emails vendors to cancel the software nobody's using**

- **Story type:** category-creation
- **Why a journalist cares:** An enterprise-AI reporter at a business-tech outlet who's been sorting which "agentic AI" launches actually take autonomous action versus which just add a chatbot on top of a dashboard. This is a concrete example of an agent initiating a real-world business transaction (a vendor email), not just summarizing data. Not for consumer tech press, general startup-funding blogs, or reporters who only cover Ramp's corporate-card business.
- **Why now:** The feature is launching now, rolled out free to existing customers.
- **Decay:** month — this is a category-thesis angle (where agentic AI is headed), not tied to a single-day news cycle beyond the launch itself.
- **What makes it distinct:** Ladder lens, zoomed up to the category level. Protagonist is the technology/category, not a customer, a vendor, or a worker — that's what separates it from the other angles below.
- **Proof it needs:** clarity on how autonomous the agent really is given finance still approves every email; examples of comparable agentic-AI products elsewhere in enterprise software to argue this is part of a broader shift, not a one-off; confirmation of whether the agent works across arbitrary SaaS vendors or a limited catalog.
- **Facts it rests on:** the agent scans spend, flags duplicate/unused seats, and drafts cancellation or renegotiation emails that finance approves; the feature is included for existing customers at no extra cost.

**Uncomfortable questions**

- Is the $342,000 figure realized savings (vendor actually cancelled or renegotiated), or just flagged opportunity the finance team hasn't acted on yet? The fact that finance still has to approve each email suggests this may be more "identified" than "captured."
- Were the 200 beta companies a representative sample, or self-selected customers who already suspected they had bloat — which would inflate the average?

- Can any of the 200 beta companies be named on the record? Without one, every angle here stays aggregate-only, and a reporter will ask for the concrete floor under the trend.
