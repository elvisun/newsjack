**Instacart's delivery growth is stalling. Its ad business just topped $1 billion.**

- **Story type:** contrarian
- **Why a journalist cares:** A business/markets reporter covering e-commerce and digital-ad economics at a general business outlet or a trade publication that tracks public tech companies' revenue mix. The hook is a public company's core growth line slowing while a secondary line becomes material enough to reshape the investment story. Not for consumer lifestyle press, shopping-deals writers, or outlets that only cover grocery recipes/deals content.
- **Why now:** Instacart states the $1B run rate and the single-digit delivery-growth slowdown as current facts as of this update; there's no external event behind it, so the "why now" is the milestone crossing itself.
- **Decay:** 24hr — this is a specific milestone figure (run rate crossing $1B) that reads like standard company news and goes stale once the next disclosure supersedes it.
- **What makes it distinct:** News-values lens (surprise/contrast) at the top rung of the abstraction ladder — the protagonist is the company's overall financial trajectory, not a single retailer's operations or a product-strategy decision. Distinct from the operational angle and the infrastructure-vendor angle below.
- **Proof it needs:** the exact delivery order-growth percentage (facts only say "single digits"); what share of total revenue or profit the ad business represents; a spokesperson quote confirming that advertising is becoming the primary growth engine.
- **Facts it rests on:** advertising business hit a $1B annual run rate; grocery-delivery order growth has slowed to single digits; Instacart is publicly traded and profitable; ads come from over 6,000 consumer brands.

**More grocery chains are letting Instacart's smart carts run their checkout line**

- **Story type:** trend
- **Why a journalist cares:** A grocery/retail-operations trade reporter covering store technology, front-end staffing, and loss prevention. The hook is operational: a third party's hardware is taking over a function — checkout — that used to be entirely in-house. Not for national tech-desk reporters chasing a single splashy launch, and not for ad-industry press.
- **Why now:** smart carts are live now in a few hundred stores with active plans to expand into more chains — an ongoing rollout, not a one-day launch.
- **Decay:** week — this is a trend/context piece about an ongoing deployment, not a single-day news event.
- **What makes it distinct:** Perspective lens, retailer/operator as protagonist — the story is what changes inside the store for the grocery chain, not Instacart's revenue mix or its industry-wide vendor positioning.
- **Proof it needs:** a named grocery chain or store willing to describe the rollout; data on checkout-time savings, staffing changes, or shrink/loss-prevention impact; comment from a grocery-chain operations executive.
- **Facts it rests on:** Caper smart carts track items as shoppers add them and skip the checkout line; smart carts are live in a few hundred stores with plans to expand to more grocery chains; the company partners with more than 1,500 retail banners.

- **What makes it distinct:** Ladder-of-abstraction lens, zoomed up to the category/business-model question — the protagonist is Instacart's product-strategy choice, not its revenue mix or any single retailer's day-to-day operations.

- **Decay:** week — this needs reporting time to confirm or rule out the data link before it can run.

The user needs an explicit statement from Instacart, on or off record, on whether cart-level shopping data is used in ad products, plus Instacart's shopper-data/privacy policy language, before this angle is pitchable at all.
