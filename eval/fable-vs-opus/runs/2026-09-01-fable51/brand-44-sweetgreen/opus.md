**Sweetgreen says its robot-assembled stores run higher margins than human-run ones**

- **Story type:** data
- **Why a journalist cares:** A restaurant-industry trade reporter covering unit economics and store-level margins (the desks at the trade outlets that track restaurant-level margin, throughput, and labor as a percent of sales). The story is the comparison itself: a public chain putting a margin delta between automated and traditional stores on the record. Not for consumer food/lifestyle writers, and not for general "robots are coming" tech-trend desks who won't interrogate the number.
- **Why now:** The company is actively expanding the system to more locations and has just shared margin data — the comparison is freshly on the record.
- **Decay:** 24hr — this is company-disclosed data tied to an expansion announcement; the margin-comparison hook is a standard company-news cycle. The deeper economics piece can stretch toward a week if the reporter gets real numbers.
- **What makes it distinct:** Data lens (variation / relationships) — the angle is the *spread* between two store types within one operator, not the expansion news. The trend angle below zooms up to the category; this one stays at one company's books.
- **Proof it needs:** the actual margin range for automated versus traditional stores (the fact block gives a range, not figures — a reporter will demand the numbers); how "restaurant-level margin" is defined and whether it nets out the capital cost of the equipment; throughput figures behind the "faster" claim; the sample size (how many automated stores the margin claim is drawn from).
- **Facts it rests on:** automated locations run higher restaurant-level margins and faster throughput than traditional stores; margin figures given as a range, not precise; the system is being expanded to more locations.

**A still-unprofitable public chain is betting the path to profit runs through automation**

- **Story type:** counterposition
- **Why a journalist cares:** A markets/equities reporter or restaurant-sector analyst-desk covering publicly traded chains and the road to profitability. The tension is sharp and on the record: the company is not yet net-profitable, yet says its automated stores carry better store-level margins — so the question is whether automation is the profitability thesis or a costly distraction. Not for HR/labor desks (that's a separate angle) and not for product reviewers.
- **Why now:** The expansion plan plus shared margin data give an equities reporter a concrete peg to test the profitability narrative against today.
- **Decay:** 24hr — anchored to the expansion/data disclosure; an investor-thesis piece can run a few days out if the reporter gets management on the record.
- **What makes it distinct:** Perspective lens, investor/money protagonist — the story is whether the capital case holds up for a company still in the red, not the operational margin number itself. Different protagonist (the shareholder) and different beat (markets) from the trade-data angle.
- **Proof it needs:** the capital cost to install or retrofit a unit and the payback period; how many of the "low hundreds" of locations are or will be automated; whether store-level margin gains survive once equipment depreciation and the company's overall losses are counted; management willing to state the profitability timeline on record.
- **Facts it rests on:** the company is publicly traded and not yet net-profitable; automated locations run higher restaurant-level margins; it plans to put the system in most new stores and retrofit some existing ones; total locations number in the low hundreds.

**When the salad line is a conveyor: what automation does to fast-casual jobs**

- **Story type:** trend
- **Why a journalist cares:** A labor or workforce reporter covering automation's effect on service-sector hours and headcount, at a business or labor-beat outlet. The fact block itself names the concern — reduced staff hours — and a chain explicitly planning to put minimal-staff assembly in *most new stores* is a concrete, named instance of a much-discussed shift. Not for the markets desk (covered above) and not for pure tech-gadget coverage of the machine.
- **Why now:** The expansion-to-most-new-stores plan turns an abstract "automation will cut jobs" worry into a present, specific rollout decision worth reporting now.
- **Decay:** week — this is a workforce-trend piece; it doesn't expire with the day's announcement, but it's strongest while the expansion is the live news.
- **What makes it distinct:** Ladder lens, zoomed up to the category — automation and service-sector labor — using this chain as the concrete floor. Different altitude and a different protagonist (workers) than the margin and markets angles.
- **Proof it needs:** actual staffing data per store type — hours, headcount, or roles per automated versus traditional location; whether automated stores cut total hours or shift workers to different tasks; any worker, union, or labor-economist source willing to speak; whether the company disputes the reduced-hours concern. Without staffing numbers this stays a topic, not a story.
- **Facts it rests on:** the Infinite Kitchen assembles bowls and salads on a conveyor with minimal staff; the company plans to put the system in most new stores; some worry about reduced staff hours.

**Uncomfortable questions**

- What are the actual margin numbers? You have a range, not figures, and "higher margins" is the entire spine of two of these angles — a reporter will ask within one email, and so will an editor.
- Does the higher store-level margin survive the cost of the equipment? Restaurant-level margin often excludes capital expenditure; if the machine's cost and depreciation erase the gain, the profitability angle inverts on you.
- How many of the low-hundreds of locations are actually automated today? The strength of every angle changes if this is five stores versus fifty.
- Are you willing to address the staff-hours concern directly, or only the margin upside? Pitching the margin story while ducking the labor question invites the labor reporter to write the version you didn't want.
- Is there any current news peg here? None was supplied. None of these angles rests on a breaking signal, so none earns a 30min/4hr decay; if a labor-automation or restaurant-earnings story is live right now, the trend angle would sharpen considerably.
