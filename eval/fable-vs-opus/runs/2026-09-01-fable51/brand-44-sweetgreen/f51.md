**Sweetgreen says its automated stores earn more. The margin comes as a range, not a number.**

- **Story type:** data
- **Why a journalist cares:** A restaurant-finance reporter at a business outlet or a restaurant trade that covers publicly traded chains and unit economics. The hook is the disclosure itself: an unprofitable public company is telling investors that automation lifts restaurant-level margin, but only as a range. That reporter's job is to ask what the range hides, how many stores it is drawn from, and whether it closes the gap to net profitability. Not for consumer food press, robotics enthusiast outlets, or local business desks.
- **Why now:** The expansion plan and the margin range are the live disclosure. Treat the date the range was shared as the clock.
- **Decay:** 24hr, because a margin disclosure is company news and gets absorbed within a day unless analysts or a filing extend it.
- **What makes it distinct:** Data lens on the margin range, with the P&L as protagonist. The other two angles are about the industry pattern and about workers. This one is about whether the number holds up.
- **Proof it needs:** the actual margin range and the comparable range for traditional stores; how many automated locations the range is based on; capex per new-build and per retrofit; a company statement on how the automated-store margin relates to the path to net profitability.
- **Facts it rests on:** automated locations run higher restaurant-level margins and faster throughput than traditional stores, per the company; margin figures were given as a range, not precise; Sweetgreen is publicly traded and not yet net-profitable; total locations number in the low hundreds.

**Sweetgreen moves the automated kitchen from pilot to standard spec for new stores**

- **Story type:** trend
- **Why a journalist cares:** A restaurant-operations or kitchen-technology reporter at a foodservice trade outlet covering how chains build and equip units. The story is the shift in posture: a chain with locations in the low hundreds is making a conveyor-assembly system the default for most new stores and retrofitting some existing ones. That is a different story from a single flagship test, and it gives an operations reporter a concrete case for where fast-casual build specs are heading. Not for labor desks, markets desks, or general consumer press.
- **Why now:** The commitment to put the system in most new stores is the news. Without that, this is an old pilot story.
- **Decay:** week, because trend pieces about build specs can run through the week after the announcement.
- **What makes it distinct:** Ladder lens, zoomed up. The company's rollout decision is evidence of where the segment is going. The data angle stays on the number; this one stays on the operating model.
- **Proof it needs:** the number of new stores planned with the system and the number of retrofits; what "minimal staff" means in headcount per shift versus a traditional store; throughput figures, since "faster" without a number will not survive a trade editor; a retrofit timeline and whether stores close during conversion.
- **Facts it rests on:** the Infinite Kitchen assembles bowls and salads on a conveyor with minimal staff; the company plans to put the system in most new stores and retrofit some existing ones; total locations number in the low hundreds; the company claims faster throughput.

**Fewer hands per bowl: what Sweetgreen's automation push does to store hours**

- **Story type:** defensive-comment
- **Why a journalist cares:** A labor reporter covering service-sector automation and scheduling at a business outlet, a workforce trade, or a regional paper with Sweetgreen stores. The company has volunteered that automated stores run with minimal staff, and there is already stated worry about reduced hours. That reporter will write this story with or without the company. The angle here is to answer it on the record before someone else frames it. Not for restaurant-tech trades, investor desks, or anyone who wants the margin story.
- **Why now:** The expansion to most new stores and some retrofits turns a contained pilot into a workforce question at scale.
- **Decay:** week, because the labor question follows the rollout rather than the announcement day.
- **What makes it distinct:** News-values lens, conflict and bad news. Workers are the protagonist, not the P&L or the build spec. It is the one angle where the company is the respondent, not the source of good news.
- **Proof it needs:** headcount and scheduled hours at an automated store versus a traditional store; what happens to existing staff when a store is retrofitted; whether the company has any redeployment or retraining commitment it can state; a spokesperson willing to take the hours question directly.
- **Facts it rests on:** the Infinite Kitchen operates with minimal staff; the company plans to retrofit some existing stores; some worry about reduced staff hours.

**Refused angles**

- "Sweetgreen expands its Infinite Kitchen to more locations" — `no_journalist_shape`. A rephrasing of the update with no so-what. Every beat above needs a reason beyond "more stores."
- "Retrofitting a live salad shop with a conveyor" as a restaurant-development story — `duplicate`. It rests on the same two facts as the trend angle. It becomes its own story only with a retrofit count, downtime, and cost per store.
- "The robot restaurant was supposed to be a gimmick. Sweetgreen just made it the default." — `duplicate`. The contrarian framing collapses into the trend angle, and the only evidence against the supposed belief is company-reported margins given as a range.
- "Diners can't tell the difference" — `hallucinated_fact`. Nothing in the update covers order accuracy, wait time at the counter, or customer perception.
- "Sweetgreen pulls ahead of rivals on kitchen automation" — `hallucinated_fact`. No competitor facts were supplied. A counterposition angle needs a named incumbent and their position.
- An executive or founder profile built around the automation bet — `hallucinated_fact`. No executive is named in the update.
- "Sweetgreen's automation is a game-changer for fast casual" — `slop`.
- A newsjack peg to current automation or labor headlines — `no_why_now_but_required`. No current signal was supplied, and reaching for one without it is forced relevance.

**Uncomfortable questions**

- What is the margin range, and how many automated stores is it drawn from? A range from a handful of new units in strong markets is a different story from a range across a mature cohort.
- Does the automated-store margin lift actually move the company toward net profitability, or is it a store-level improvement swamped by corporate costs? A markets reporter will ask this first.
- How many staff hours does an automated store use per week versus a traditional one? If you will not put that number on the record, the labor story gets written without you.
- What does "faster throughput" mean in bowls per hour or minutes per order? Without a figure the trend angle has no floor.
- How many retrofits, where, and do those stores close during conversion? Right now "some existing ones" is not a fact a reporter can use.
- Who is the spokesperson for the hours question, and are they authorized to answer it directly rather than pivot to the margin story?
