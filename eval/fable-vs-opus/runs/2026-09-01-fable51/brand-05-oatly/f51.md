**Oatly says its new barista oat milk has a 31% smaller footprint. What did the audit count?**

- **Story type:** data
- **Why a journalist cares:** A data or sustainability reporter at a food-and-beverage trade outlet or a climate newsletter that audits corporate carbon claims. The hook is not the launch; it is a verified per-liter number from a named third-party life-cycle assessment firm, which is rarer than a self-reported one and invites scrutiny of what changed in the recipe and where the baseline sits. Not for coffee-shop consumer press, general startup press, or business desks that only want the P&L.
- **Why now:** The reformulated blend is rolling out now, and the LCA is fresh evidence a reporter can request and read this week.
- **Decay:** week. Product news fades in a day, but a verifiable footprint claim supports a reported explainer for about a week.
- **What makes it distinct:** Data lens, "change" lead. The number itself is the protagonist, and the story is how it was measured. The other angles use the number as background, not as the thing being tested.
- **Proof it needs:** the full LCA report or summary with system boundary and functional unit; the LCA firm's name and willingness to be quoted; the absolute per-liter figures for old and new formula; a plain-language account of what changed in the recipe or sourcing to produce the cut.
- **Facts it rests on:** 31% lower climate footprint per liter than the previous formula; verified by a named third-party LCA firm; reformulated barista blend now rolling out.

**A 1,400-store Northeast coffee chain makes oat milk the default non-dairy pour**

- **Story type:** customer-story
- **Why a journalist cares:** A foodservice or specialty-coffee trade reporter covering beverage programs, menu defaults, and dairy-alternative economics at chains. The chain is the protagonist: why an operator of that size changed its default, what it means for upcharges and waste, and how baristas handle the switch. Not for climate press, financial reporters, or Oatly-brand consumer coverage.
- **Why now:** The partnership is being announced now, and a 1,400-location default switch is a same-cycle operator decision.
- **Decay:** 24hr. Standard partnership news; a trade desk needs it on or near announcement day.
- **What makes it distinct:** Perspective lens, customer protagonist, zoomed to the bottom rung. The chain's operational decision is the story, not Oatly's product or finances.
- **Proof it needs:** the chain named and a chain executive on record; what "default" means operationally (auto-substituted, first listed, no upcharge); what the previous default was; store count confirmed by the chain; any volume or cup-count figure the chain will share.
- **Facts it rests on:** the new blend becomes the default non-dairy option at a 1,400-location regional coffee chain in the US Northeast.

**Oatly's greener oat milk costs the same at the shelf**

- **Story type:** contrarian
- **Why a journalist cares:** A consumer-packaged-goods or grocery trade reporter who covers the "green premium," the assumption that lower-footprint products cost more. The fact set gives evidence against it: a verified footprint cut with no retail price change. That reporter can ask whether the reformulation is cheaper to make, margin-neutral, or subsidized by cost cuts. Not for coffee trade press or local business reporters.
- **Why now:** The no-price-change decision lands alongside the launch this week; the question is live while retailers restock.
- **Decay:** week. The price point and the footprint claim both hold for a week-long reported piece.
- **What makes it distinct:** Inversion lens. The prevailing belief is that sustainability costs consumers more; the fact undercuts it. The data angle asks whether the 31% is real; this angle asks who is paying for it.
- **Proof it needs:** on-record confirmation that shelf price is unchanged across major retailers; a statement on whether the new formula's unit cost is higher, lower, or flat; evidence the user can cite for the green-premium belief; whether the chain's wholesale price also stayed flat.
- **Facts it rests on:** no price change at retail; 31% lower climate footprint per liter verified by a third-party LCA firm.

**Oatly launches its lowest-footprint product while closing a US plant**

- **Story type:** trend
- **Why a journalist cares:** A business reporter covering plant-based category economics and consumer-brand restructurings. The story is the contradiction: a company still unprofitable, narrowing its loss, shutting one of three US plants, and simultaneously shipping a reformulation and a large default-placement deal. That reporter tests whether this is a category correction or a company-specific turnaround. Not for coffee trade press or sustainability desks, which will treat the closure as a footnote.
- **Why now:** Both halves are current: the closure is being executed to cut costs and the launch is rolling out at the same time.
- **Decay:** 24hr for the closure and launch as news; a further week for a category-shakeout piece if the user supplies comparable data.
- **What makes it distinct:** News-values lens, good news and bad news in one fact set, zoomed up to the category. The company's financial position is the protagonist. No other angle carries the loss and the closure as its center.
- **Proof it needs:** last quarter's operating loss and the prior comparison; which plant is closing, its capacity, and timeline; whether remaining plants absorb the volume; a spokesperson willing to discuss the closure on record rather than deflect.
- **Facts it rests on:** Oatly remains unprofitable and reported a narrowing operating loss last quarter; closing one of three US plants to cut costs; reformulated blend and chain partnership rolling out now.

**What happens to the workers when Oatly shuts the plant**

- **Story type:** defensive-comment
- **Why a journalist cares:** A local business or labor reporter in the metro where the closing plant sits. This is not a pitch to send; it is the story that will be written whether Oatly participates or not, and the launch gives that reporter a sharper contrast to write against. Oatly needs a prepared, factual comment ready. Not for national business press (covered by the angle above), and not for any outreach list.
- **Why now:** The closure is underway now, and the launch coverage will surface it in local search within days.
- **Decay:** 24hr once the closure becomes public locally.
- **What makes it distinct:** Perspective lens, worker and community protagonist, zoomed to the bottom rung. The national restructuring angle asks what the closure means for the category; this asks what it means for the town.
- **Proof it needs:** plant location; headcount affected; closure date; severance or transfer terms; whether any production shifts to the other two US plants; a named executive who will take the call.
- **Facts it rests on:** closing one of three US plants to cut costs; company remains unprofitable.

**Refused angles**

- "Oatly beats a rival for the default slot at a major coffee chain" — `hallucinated_fact`. No fact names a displaced incumbent or a competitive bid.
- "Northeast coffee drinkers get greener oat milk by default" — `duplicate` of the customer-story angle; same protagonist, same fact, only the inbox changes.
- "Oatly's turnaround proves plant-based is back" — `hallucinated_fact`. A narrowing loss at one company is not category evidence.
- "Oatly reinvents sustainable coffee" — `slop`. Rewritten once, still a press-release headline.
- "The founders behind Oatly's reformulation" — `no_journalist_shape`. No founder, executive, or R&D facts supplied; nothing to profile.
- "Oatly's reformulation ties to this week's climate news" — `no_why_now_but_required`. No current news signal was supplied, and reaching for one would be forced relevance.

**Uncomfortable questions**

- Can you hand a reporter the actual LCA report, the firm's name, and the system boundary? A 31% claim with a sealed report is a claim a reporter will decline to print.
- What does "default" mean at the chain: automatic substitution, first-listed, or no upcharge? Each is a different-sized story, and the chain must confirm it on record.
- Which plant, how many jobs, and when? Every launch story will carry a closure paragraph, and a "no comment" there poisons the launch coverage.
- Did the plant closure or a cheaper formula fund the no-price-change decision? If the reformulation is cheaper to make, say so; if not, expect the question.
- Does the footprint cut come from the recipe alone, or from sourcing or manufacturing changes that the closure also affects? Reporters will look for a link.
