**Perplexity will pay publishers it cites. It won't say how much.**

- **Story type:** defensive-comment
- **Why a journalist cares:** A media-business reporter at a publishing trade outlet or a media-industry newsletter who has been tracking publisher complaints and legal threats over AI content use. This is the company's first structural answer to that fight, and the undisclosed payout is the reporter's first question. Not for consumer-tech reviewers, e-commerce trade press, or general startup coverage.
- **Why now:** The program launches now, on top of criticism and legal threats that are already on the record.
- **Decay:** 24hr. Launch-day reaction is the window. After that the story only survives if a publisher signs or a payout figure leaks.
- **What makes it distinct:** Perspective lens with the publisher as protagonist, sharpened by the conflict and follow-up news values. It is the next chapter of a fight the press is already covering. The other kept angles are about merchants, investors, and users, not publishers.
- **Proof it needs:** the payout formula or at least its structure (percentage, per-citation, floor); at least one named publisher enrolled; a clear statement on whether enrollment is tied to dropping or settling legal claims; a spokesperson willing to be quoted on the criticism.
- **Facts it rests on:** the program shares ad revenue when a publisher's article is used in an answer; the company has faced criticism and legal threats from publishers over content use; payout amounts are not disclosed.

**Perplexity turns its answer engine into a checkout and takes a referral fee on every sale**

- **Story type:** category-creation
- **Why a journalist cares:** An e-commerce trade reporter covering affiliate commerce, marketplaces, and retail media, or a retail-industry newsletter reader who buys and sells traffic. The question is whether an answer engine is a new affiliate channel with its own economics, and what merchants pay to be in it. Not for media-business reporters, VC reporters, or general AI coverage.
- **Why now:** In-app shopping is live now. Merchants deciding whether to list will want the terms this week.
- **Decay:** 24hr for the launch itself. The channel-economics question can run for a week if merchant terms are available.
- **What makes it distinct:** Ladder lens zoomed up, with the merchant side of the transaction as the protagonist. This is about commerce plumbing and take rates, not about what the user sees or how the company gets valued.
- **Proof it needs:** the referral-fee rate or range; which merchants or product categories are live; whether merchants can pay for placement; whether purchase and return handling happens inside the app or at the merchant.
- **Facts it rests on:** users can research and buy products without leaving the app; Perplexity earns a referral fee on those purchases; the company says it handles hundreds of millions of queries a month.

**How Perplexity plans to make money: referral fees and an ad business it will split with publishers**

- **Story type:** funding-mechanics
- **Why a journalist cares:** A VC or private-markets reporter covering AI-company unit economics and late-stage valuations. A privately held company that recently raised at a multi-billion valuation has just shown two revenue lines, and both depend on third parties: merchants for fees and publishers for a share of ad revenue. Not for publishing trade press, e-commerce trade press, or product-review outlets.
- **Why now:** The launch is the first concrete monetization signal since the raise. Investors and rivals will read it as the thesis.
- **Decay:** week. Monetization-thesis pieces can run after launch day if there are numbers or an investor on record.
- **What makes it distinct:** Perspective lens with the money as protagonist. The publisher angle asks what publishers get. This angle asks what the company keeps and whether that justifies the valuation.
- **Proof it needs:** any revenue figure, ad revenue included; the referral take rate; the publisher share as a percentage of ad revenue; an investor willing to explain the monetization thesis on record; confirmation of the valuation figure and date.
- **Facts it rests on:** the company is privately held and recently raised at a multi-billion valuation; it earns a referral fee on in-app purchases; it shares ad revenue with publishers whose articles are used in answers; payout amounts are not disclosed.

**Perplexity now earns a fee when you buy what it recommends**

- **Story type:** trend
- **Why a journalist cares:** A consumer-tech accountability reporter or a product-trust columnist at a general tech outlet who covers how recommendation systems get paid. The moment an answer engine profits from the purchase it advises, the integrity of the advice becomes the story. Not for media-business reporters, VC reporters, or retail trade press.
- **Why now:** The referral fee starts today, so the question is live for the first time rather than hypothetical.
- **Decay:** week. This is a follow-up piece rather than a launch-day headline, and it stays alive until the company answers the question.
- **What makes it distinct:** Perspective lens with the user as protagonist, surfaced by the surprise news value. The e-commerce angle is about merchant economics. This one is about whether the fee changes the answer a person sees.
- **Proof it needs:** a company statement on whether fee-bearing products are ranked or labeled differently; whether product answers disclose the referral relationship in the interface; any policy separating answer generation from commerce incentives.
- **Facts it rests on:** users can research and buy products without leaving the app; Perplexity earns a referral fee on those purchases.

**Refused angles**

- "Perplexity's copyright peace offering to publishers" as a separate legal-beat story. `duplicate`. Same lens, same protagonist as the publisher-payout angle. Kept the version with the sharper hole.
- "Perplexity's shopping launch, the retail-media angle." `duplicate`. Same merchant protagonist as the checkout angle in a different envelope.
- "Hundreds of millions of queries a month: the scale of Perplexity's new ad economy." `no_journalist_shape`. A self-reported, uncontextualized query count with undisclosed payouts gives a data reporter nothing to pull.
- "Perplexity proves AI and publishers can coexist." `hallucinated_fact`. No signed publisher, no payout number, no evidence of coexistence.
- "The founders behind Perplexity's pivot to commerce." `hallucinated_fact`. No founder or executive facts were supplied.
- "Perplexity takes on the search and retail giants." `hallucinated_fact`. No competitor is in the facts. A counterposition angle would need a named incumbent and a reaction.
- "Perplexity reimagines the future of shopping and publishing." `slop`.

**Uncomfortable questions**

- What is the publisher payout? A percentage, a per-citation rate, a floor? The undisclosed number is the hole in the strongest angle, and every reporter will ask it first.
- Is any publisher actually enrolled, and are any of them the ones who sent legal threats? Is enrollment conditional on dropping those claims?
- Do you have ad revenue to share today, at a scale where a share means anything to a publisher?
- What is the referral fee rate, which merchants are live, and can merchants pay for placement?
- Does a fee-bearing product get ranked or labeled differently in an answer? If you cannot answer that plainly, the trust angle turns hostile fast.
- Can the query figure be audited, or is it a company claim you would not want fact-checked?
- Who speaks on record about the publisher criticism? Without a named spokesperson the defensive-comment angle has no comment.

**Next step:** `journalist-fit-check`. Four viable shapes exist. Pick one, then resolve real names for that beat. Do not pitch the publisher angle until the payout question has an answer you can say out loud.
