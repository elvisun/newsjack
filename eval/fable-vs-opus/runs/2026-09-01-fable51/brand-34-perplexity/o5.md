**Perplexity starts paying publishers it was being sued over**

- **Story type:** counterposition
- **Why a journalist cares:** A media-business reporter at a publishing trade outlet or media newsletter covering the AI-vs-publisher licensing fight. The hook is not the program, it's the sequence: this is the company that drew legal threats from publishers now offering those same publishers a cut of ad revenue. That reporter can call the publishers who threatened legal action and ask whether a revenue share changes anything, or whether it's an attempt to settle out of court with product features. Not for consumer tech reviewers, shopping/retail desks, or general startup press.
- **Why now:** The program is being announced now, and the legal threats it responds to are unresolved as far as these facts show. The story is strongest in the window where publishers are being asked to react on record.
- **Decay:** 24hr — launch news, and the publisher reaction quotes get gathered in the first day. The underlying dispute is longer-running, but the peg is the announcement.
- **What makes it distinct:** Perspective lens, aggrieved-counterparty protagonist. The story belongs to the publishers, not Perplexity, and its energy comes from conflict rather than from the product.
- **Proof it needs:** the actual terms — what share of ad revenue, calculated against what, paid to whom, on what cadence; which publishers have signed; whether signing requires waiving or pausing any claim; a statement from at least one publisher that previously raised legal threats. Without disclosed payout amounts this angle can be pitched but will be reported skeptically.
- **Facts it rests on:** the publisher program shares ad revenue when a publisher's article is used in an answer; Perplexity has faced criticism and legal threats from publishers over content use; payout amounts not disclosed.

**The AI answer box becomes a checkout counter**

- **Story type:** category-creation
- **Why a journalist cares:** An e-commerce or retail-tech reporter at a commerce trade outlet, or a platforms reporter covering where product discovery happens. The story is the structural move: a research interface taking a referral fee means the AI assistant is now positioned between the shopper and the merchant, in the slot affiliate links and search ads have held. That reporter can test what it means for merchants' acquisition costs and for who owns the customer relationship. Not for AI model-capability reporters, enterprise software desks, or personal-finance columnists.
- **Why now:** In-app buying is launching now; the broader shift in where purchases start supports a longer reported piece.
- **Decay:** week — the launch is a 24-hour item, but the "where commerce starts" thesis holds for a reported feature.
- **What makes it distinct:** Ladder lens, zoomed up. This makes the transaction layer the story, not Perplexity's dispute with publishers or the economics of any one deal.
- **Proof it needs:** which merchants or commerce platforms are integrated and on what terms; the referral fee structure; whether product results are ranked by relevance, by fee, or both, and how that's disclosed to users; any live purchase volume. Merchant names are the difference between a reported story and a product blurb.
- **Facts it rests on:** users can research and buy products without leaving the app, with Perplexity earning a referral fee; Perplexity says it handles hundreds of millions of queries a month.

**Perplexity is now paid twice on the same answer — and the publisher gets a slice of one payment**

- **Story type:** contrarian
- **Why a journalist cares:** A media-economics or creator-economy reporter at a business outlet that covers how content gets monetized, or an analyst-facing newsletter on AI business models. The prevailing belief is that revenue sharing solves the AI-content dispute by aligning incentives. These facts cut against it: the ad-revenue pool is shared, the referral-fee pool apparently is not, and the referral fee is the one attached to the answer where a purchase happens. If a publisher's product review drives a purchase, the shared pot may not be the pot that grew. Not for reporters who cover AI model releases, and not for general consumer tech.
- **Why now:** Both mechanisms launched together, which is what makes the comparison reportable rather than speculative — but the claim is only as good as the terms.
- **Decay:** week — this is an analysis piece that needs the terms in hand, and it survives past launch day.
- **What makes it distinct:** Inversion lens. Every other angle treats the two launches separately; this one is only a story because they shipped on the same day and are structured differently.
- **Proof it needs:** confirmation of whether referral revenue is inside or outside the shared pool — if it is shared, this angle dies and should be dropped, not softened; the definition of "ad revenue" in the program; whether product-review and buying-guide publishers are treated any differently from news publishers. This angle must not be pitched on inference alone.
- **Facts it rests on:** Perplexity earns a referral fee on in-app purchases; the publisher program shares ad revenue when a publisher's article is used in an answer; payout amounts not disclosed.

**What "hundreds of millions of queries a month" is worth to a publisher, exactly**

- **Story type:** data
- **Why a journalist cares:** A data or media-business reporter at a publishing trade outlet who covers referral traffic and audience economics, the kind who already tracks search-referral decline for publishers. The company has put a query volume in public and a revenue share in public but no payout figure — that gap is the story, and it's arithmetic a reporter can attempt with an ad-revenue estimate and a citation rate. Not for reporters who need a finished number handed to them, and not for anyone covering AI capabilities.
- **Why now:** The volume claim and the program were disclosed together; the number is quotable now and will be contested now.
- **Decay:** week — a calculation piece that holds until the company publishes real payout data.
- **What makes it distinct:** Data lens, scale rung. Distinct from the counterposition angle because the protagonist is the number, not the litigants — this one runs even if no publisher will comment.
- **Proof it needs:** how "hundreds of millions of queries a month" is counted and whether it's verified by anything outside the company; what fraction of answers cite publisher content at all; the share percentage; any per-publisher payout figure, even a range. Without at least the share percentage, a reporter cannot do the math and this angle is not pitchable.
- **Facts it rests on:** Perplexity says it handles hundreds of millions of queries a month; the publisher program shares ad revenue when a publisher's article is used in an answer; payout amounts not disclosed.

**A multi-billion-dollar valuation now has two revenue lines to defend it**

- **Story type:** funding-mechanics
- **Why a journalist cares:** A private-markets or venture reporter covering AI company valuations and the gap between raised capital and actual revenue. The launch is the peg; the question is whether referral fees and ads are the business model that justifies the last round, and what it says that both arrived at once. Not for product reviewers, not for publishing trade press, not for shopping desks.
- **Why now:** The recent raise at a multi-billion valuation and the first visible consumer-revenue mechanisms landing together is the honest hook. It thins out fast once other AI companies ship similar features.
- **Decay:** 24hr — tied to the announcement; without disclosed revenue numbers there's little to sustain a longer piece.
- **What makes it distinct:** Perspective lens, investor/market protagonist. The story is the valuation math, not the publisher fight or the commerce shift.
- **Proof it needs:** the valuation figure and round date on record; whether either program is expected to be material revenue this year; an investor willing to state the monetization thesis on record. This is the thinnest angle in the set — with no revenue figures and no investor quote, it is a topic, not a story.
- **Facts it rests on:** the company is privately held and recently raised at a multi-billion valuation; users can buy in-app with Perplexity earning a referral fee; the publisher program shares ad revenue.

**Refused angles**

- "Perplexity transforms how people shop online" — `slop`. Press-release framing, no protagonist, no beat.
- "Perplexity ends its war with publishers" — `hallucinated_fact`. Nothing in the facts says any dispute was resolved, any claim withdrawn, or any publisher accepted the deal.
- "Publishers finally get paid for AI citations" — `hallucinated_fact`. Payout amounts are undisclosed and no publisher is confirmed enrolled; "get paid" asserts an outcome not in evidence.
- "The future of AI-powered commerce" — `no_journalist_shape`. A theme, not a story; no sub-beat would own it.
- "Perplexity's shopping launch is a threat to Amazon and Google" — `hallucinated_fact`. No competitor named in the facts, no competitor response, no share data. Real angle if you bring a competitor's reaction or merchant data; not before.
- "How AI search changes SEO strategy" — `off-beat`. Marketing-trade content, not a news beat, and the facts don't support it.
- "Perplexity's referral fee model explained" — `duplicate` of the commerce angle with the analytical edge removed.

**Uncomfortable questions**

- The payout amount is undisclosed. Every media reporter who takes this call will lead with that question, and "we're not sharing terms" is a quote that makes the program look like a press move rather than a payment. Do you have the share percentage or a payout range you can give on record, even under embargo? If not, the publisher angles are pitchable but will be written with a raised eyebrow.
- Has any publisher who previously threatened legal action agreed to join? One name changes the counterposition angle from "company announces program" to "adversary signs deal." Zero names is also an answer, and reporters will find it.
- Are publishers required to release, pause, or forgo claims to participate? If yes, that is the story, and it is not the one the announcement is telling. Get ahead of it.
- Does a publisher whose review drives a purchase share in the referral fee, or only in ad revenue? Your answer determines whether the contrarian angle is a real story or gets cut.
- Are shopping results ranked or influenced by the referral fee, and is that disclosed in-product? This is the question that turns a commerce feature story into a consumer-trust story. Have the answer before you pitch, not during the interview.
- How is "hundreds of millions of queries a month" measured, and would you stand behind it in print? Round unaudited numbers from private companies get attributed as claims ("Perplexity says"), which weakens them — decide if you want that in the piece.
- Given the unresolved legal criticism, has counsel cleared what a spokesperson can say about content use? Several of these angles put a reporter one question away from the litigation.
- No current news signal was supplied with this update, so nothing here carries a breaking-news hook. The decay tags reflect the announcement itself, not an external event.

**Next step:** `journalist-fit-check` once you pick an angle — the counterposition and commerce angles are the two strongest and they go to entirely different desks, so resolve real names one at a time rather than building a list across both. Before that, though, settle the payout-terms question internally: if you cannot disclose a number or name a signed publisher, pitch the commerce angle first and hold the publisher angles until you can.
