**Rivian's next revenue line is selling electricity to everyone else's EVs**

- **Story type:** funding-mechanics
- **Why a journalist cares:** An auto-industry equities or EV business reporter at a financial outlet or automotive business trade who tracks Rivian's path to profitability. The hook is not the plug; it is that an unprofitable public automaker is reclassifying a network it built for its own owners as a revenue stream, and pricing outsiders at a premium to do it. That reporter can ask whether charging revenue is material, how it will be reported, and whether "improves utilization" is a polite way of saying stalls sit empty. Not for consumer gadget reviewers, outdoor-lifestyle press, or local news.
- **Why now:** The opening is announced today, and it lands as a business-model statement from a company under public-market scrutiny for losses.
- **Decay:** week. The announcement is a 24-hour event, but the "can charging pay" question stays live until the next earnings report gives numbers.
- **What makes it distinct:** Perspective lens, money as protagonist. The other kept angles are the driver at the stall and the infrastructure strategy; this one is the P&L.
- **Proof it needs:** current network utilization figures or at least a stated baseline; expected revenue contribution or per-kWh pricing for both tiers; whether charging revenue will be broken out in financial reporting; a CFO or finance-side spokesperson willing to go on record about margins.
- **Facts it rests on:** the company is publicly traded and not yet profitable; Rivian says opening the network creates a new revenue stream and improves charger utilization; pricing for non-Rivian drivers is set at a premium to Rivian owners; 3,500+ chargers.

**Rivian opens 3,500 chargers to all EVs, at a higher price for outsiders**

- **Story type:** customer-story
- **Why a journalist cares:** A consumer EV reporter at an automotive enthusiast outlet, a consumer-advice desk, or an EV owner-focused publication covering the real cost and friction of public charging. The story is the two-tier price and the app requirement: what a non-Rivian driver actually pays versus a Rivian owner, and whether opening the network dilutes a perk existing owners bought into. Not for markets reporters, energy-policy desks, or general business columnists.
- **Why now:** Non-Rivian drivers can use the chargers via the app starting now, so the price gap and onboarding experience are testable today.
- **Decay:** 24hr. Standard product-availability news; it only extends if the reporter can hand-test pricing and the app flow.
- **What makes it distinct:** Perspective lens with the driver as protagonist, zoomed to the bottom rung of the ladder: one person, one stall, one bill. The money angle asks whether this pays Rivian; this asks what it costs the person plugging in, on both sides of the price line.
- **Proof it needs:** the actual per-kWh or per-session prices for both tiers; whether non-Rivian drivers must use the app or can pay another way; at least one non-Rivian driver who has completed a session and can describe it; confirmation of whether Rivian previously marketed the network as an owner-exclusive perk.
- **Facts it rests on:** non-Rivian EVs can now use 3,500+ Rivian chargers via the Rivian app; pricing for non-Rivian drivers is set at a premium to Rivian owners; all new stalls include both CCS and NACS connectors.

**Rivian adds Tesla-style plugs and opens its network with no automaker partner in sight**

- **Story type:** counterposition
- **Why a journalist cares:** An EV charging-infrastructure reporter at an energy, utility, or charging trade outlet covering connector standards and network interoperability. The hook is the shape of the move: a single automaker opening its own network to every EV, putting both CCS and NACS on all new stalls, and doing it without a partnership with any other automaker. That reporter will want to know what happens to the existing installed base, whether access is app-only or supports roaming and plug-and-charge, and why no automaker deal was struck. Not for consumer reviewers, equities desks, or lifestyle press.
- **Why now:** The NACS commitment applies to all new stalls from this announcement forward, and the network is open today.
- **Decay:** week. Standards and interoperability pieces run on a trade cycle, not a same-day cycle, as long as the rollout details are fresh.
- **What makes it distinct:** Perspective lens, competitor and category-structure as protagonist. It is about how the network connects to everyone else's cars and networks, not about revenue or the price at the stall.
- **Proof it needs:** whether existing stalls will be retrofitted with NACS and on what timeline; the mechanism for non-Rivian access (app only, roaming agreements, plug-and-charge); an on-record answer to why there is no automaker partnership; site counts by stall type so the reporter can size the installed base.
- **Facts it rests on:** all new stalls will include both CCS and Tesla-style NACS connectors; non-Rivian EVs can use the network via the Rivian app; no partnership with another automaker announced; 3,500+ chargers.

**Refused angles**

- "Rivian revolutionizes EV charging for every driver" — `slop`.
- "Automaker-owned charging networks are becoming public utilities" — `duplicate`. Zoomed-up version of the money and infrastructure angles; supporting it as an industry shift would require examples from other companies that are not in the facts.
- "Rivian now runs one of the largest open fast-charging networks" — `hallucinated_fact`. The 3,500+ figure is uncontextualized; any ranking claim needs comparison data not supplied.
- "Opening the network will lift Rivian's charger utilization by double digits" — `hallucinated_fact`. Rivian says utilization improves; no number exists.
- "Rivian owners revolt over losing their private network" — `hallucinated_fact`. No evidence of owner reaction was supplied. The tradeoff lives inside the driver angle as a question, not a claim.
- "Any EV can now reach the trailhead on Rivian's Adventure Network" — `off-beat`. The network name implies outdoor locations, but nothing in the facts says where the chargers are.
- "The executive behind Rivian's charging bet" — `no_journalist_shape`. No executive is named in the facts, and an exec-spotlight with no person is not a story.

**Uncomfortable questions**

- What is the actual price gap between Rivian owners and everyone else? Without per-kWh numbers, the consumer angle is a press release with a question mark.
- What is current utilization? If Rivian will not say, "improves utilization" reads as an admission that stalls are underused, and a markets reporter will write it that way.
- Will existing stalls get NACS, or only new ones? "All new stalls" leaves the installed base ambiguous, and the infrastructure reporter will ask first.
- Is non-Rivian access app-only? If so, the friction is part of the story, not a footnote.
- Did Rivian previously sell the network as an owner-exclusive benefit? That decides whether the owner tradeoff is a fair question or a backlash waiting to be reported.
- Did Rivian seek an automaker partnership and fail, or choose not to? "No partnership announced" invites the reporter to guess; better to answer.
- Is charging revenue material enough that finance will speak on record? If not, the money angle is a thesis without a sponsor.
