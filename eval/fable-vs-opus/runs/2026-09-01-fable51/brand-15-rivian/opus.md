**Rivian opens 3,500+ fast chargers to rival EVs, betting its network earns more as a utility than a perk**

- **Story type:** funding-mechanics
- **Why a journalist cares:** An EV-business or auto-finance reporter at an industry trade or markets outlet covering how unprofitable EV makers find revenue beyond the car. The hook is the money: a still-unprofitable automaker turning a customer-perk network into a third-party revenue line, and whether charging margin can actually move the P&L. They'll want to test the utilization-vs-revenue claim against the cost of opening stalls. Not for consumer car-buying guides, gadget desks, or general startup press.
- **Why now:** The open-access announcement is live today; the financial framing is freshest while the news is moving.
- **Decay:** 24hr — standard company-news event. The "can charging revenue matter for an unprofitable EV maker" question can stretch to a week as a business-analysis angle if a number appears.
- **What makes it distinct:** Perspective lens, the money as protagonist. This is the only angle that treats the network as a business unit rather than a charging-access story for drivers. The customer-access and competitive angles below have different protagonists.
- **Proof it needs:** any disclosed or projected charging revenue, utilization rate, or margin figure; the premium pricing delta for non-owners in dollars or percent; cost to retrofit stalls with dual connectors; an executive on record framing this as a revenue strategy, not a goodwill move.
- **Facts it rests on:** non-Rivian EVs can now use 3,500+ Rivian chargers via the Rivian app; Rivian says opening the network creates a new revenue stream and improves charger utilization; the company is publicly traded and not yet profitable; pricing for non-Rivian drivers is set at a premium to Rivian owners.
