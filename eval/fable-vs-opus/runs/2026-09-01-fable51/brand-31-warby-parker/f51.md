**Warby Parker's expansion bet is on eye exams, not frames**

- **Story type:** category-creation
- **Why a journalist cares:** A retail-health or healthcare-business reporter at a business outlet or optometry trade publication covering who delivers primary eye care. The hook is that most of the 40 new stores will be staffed by optometrists. That moves Warby Parker from a glasses retailer toward an eye-care provider, and this reporter can test whether a fashion-led eyewear brand can run clinical services at scale. Not for consumer style or fashion press, retail real-estate reporters, or general startup desks.
- **Why now:** The 40-store expansion and the nationwide exam rollout are live now. The category question stays open for as long as the rollout is under way.
- **Decay:** week. The announcement is a 24-hour event, but the "retailer becomes provider" question supports a reported piece for about a week.
- **What makes it distinct:** Perspective lens with the exam and the optometrist as protagonist. The other kept angles are about the money, the channel, and the metric. This one is about the service being sold.
- **Proof it needs:** how many of the 40 stores will have an optometrist on staff, and how many of the existing 300 already do; whether optometrists are employees or independent practitioners; exam pricing and whether exams are sold alone or bundled with frames; a spokesperson who can talk about the clinical model on record.
- **Facts it rests on:** 40 new stores; most new stores will offer optometrist-staffed comprehensive eye exams; the exam expansion is nationwide; exam revenue is not broken out.

**Warby Parker got profitable. Now it is spending on 40 stores and optometrists.**

- **Story type:** funding-mechanics
- **Why a journalist cares:** A consumer-stocks or retail-equities reporter at a financial outlet or markets newsletter who covers public retailers' capital allocation. The tension is that a company that only recently reached profitability is committing to physical stores plus clinical staffing, and it will not say what exams contribute to revenue. This reporter can ask whether exams are a profit center or a loss leader for frame sales. Not for optometry trade press, local news, or lifestyle outlets.
- **Why now:** The expansion is being announced while the profitability milestone is recent. Markets reporters react on announcement day.
- **Decay:** 24hr. Public-company capital-allocation stories run on the day of the news unless a filing or earnings call gives them a second window.
- **What makes it distinct:** Perspective lens, investor protagonist. The story is the money and the sequencing, not the exams themselves. It also carries the visible hole in the update, the missing exam-revenue breakout, as its central question rather than hiding it.
- **Proof it needs:** the profitability figure and the period it refers to, as stated in filings; capex or cost-per-store for the new locations; whether the company will break out exam revenue and when; an executive or investor-relations contact who can speak on record about payback expectations.
- **Facts it rests on:** Warby Parker is publicly traded; it recently reached profitability; it is opening 40 stores, taking the count past 300; retail customers spend more than online-only customers; exam-revenue contribution is not broken out.

**Warby Parker built its brand on skipping the middleman. Now it is testing drugstore shelves.**

- **Story type:** counterposition
- **Why a journalist cares:** A retail-strategy reporter at a retail or drugstore trade outlet who covers channel decisions and store-within-a-store deals. The company that made direct-to-consumer eyewear a category is piloting distribution through a national pharmacy chain at the same time it opens 40 of its own stores. That is a channel contradiction worth explaining, and it puts pressure on whoever currently sells eyewear in pharmacies and on incumbent optical chains. Not for healthcare or optometry press, and not for markets reporters until the chain and terms are public.
- **Why now:** The pilot is happening alongside the store expansion. If the pharmacy chain is named publicly, this becomes same-day news for the trade desk.
- **Decay:** week. As an unnamed pilot it is a strategy piece with a week-long window. Once the chain is named it collapses to 24hr.
- **What makes it distinct:** Conflict lens. The protagonist is the distribution channel, and the story is Warby Parker's own-store model versus third-party retail. Neither the exam angle nor the investor angle turns on the pharmacy pilot.
- **Proof it needs:** the name of the pharmacy chain and confirmation both sides will discuss it; the number of pilot locations; what is sold there (frames only, prescription fulfillment, or exams); whether the pilot has a defined end date or success metric.
- **Facts it rests on:** Warby Parker is piloting a partnership to sell glasses through a national pharmacy chain; it is simultaneously opening 40 of its own stores; store count is past 300.

**Warby Parker says store shoppers spend more than online buyers. It will not say how much.**

- **Story type:** data
- **Why a journalist cares:** A data or operations reporter at an e-commerce or retail-operations trade outlet covering omnichannel economics. The company has put a directional claim on record: retail customers outspend online-only customers. That is the one metric in the update, and it is the reason the whole expansion exists. This reporter will want the number, the definition, and whether it survives a selection-effect challenge. Not for general business desks, fashion press, or healthcare reporters.
- **Why now:** The claim is being used to justify the 40-store expansion now. It goes stale once the stores are old news.
- **Decay:** week. Data and context pieces run for about a week after the announcement.
- **What makes it distinct:** Data lens, variation across channel. The protagonist is the metric itself. The investor angle uses this fact as one input among several. This angle stands or falls on the number alone.
- **Proof it needs:** the actual spend differential; how it is defined (per order, per year, lifetime, and whether exam revenue is included); the cohort and time period; whether the company controls for the fact that store visitors may already be higher-intent buyers.
- **Facts it rests on:** the company says retail customers spend more than online-only customers; it is opening 40 stores on the strength of that claim; exam revenue is not broken out.

**Refused angles**

- "Warby Parker is transforming eye care in America" — `slop`.
- "Direct-to-consumer brands are all going physical, and Warby Parker proves it" — `duplicate`. Same fact and same protagonist as the data angle, with a weaker proof requirement and a category claim the update does not support.
- "Warby Parker is hiring optometrists at scale as the profession shifts to retail" — `duplicate`. Same lens and same fact as the eye-exam angle. The workforce version needs hiring numbers and an employment model the update does not include.
- "Warby Parker comes to your city" — `no_journalist_shape`. The update gives no store locations, so there is no local desk to shape it for.
- "E-commerce is over for eyewear" — `hallucinated_fact`. The company's claim is about spend per customer, not channel decline. A forced contrarian without evidence.
- "Warby Parker's exams put it on a collision course with state optometry regulators" — `hallucinated_fact`. No regulatory fact was supplied.
- "Warby Parker's expansion is the answer to this week's retail news" — `no_why_now_but_required`. No current signal was supplied, so there is no honest peg.

**Uncomfortable questions**

- What is the actual retail-versus-online spend gap, and how is it defined? If you cannot share the number, the data angle is a topic, not a story, and the investor angle loses its strongest line.
- Why is exam revenue not broken out? An investor reporter will ask whether exams are a loss leader for frame sales. Have an answer before pitching the money angle.
- Can you name the pharmacy chain and the number of pilot locations? If the partner will not be named, the channel angle cannot be pitched.
- How many of the 40 stores will actually have an optometrist on staff, and are those optometrists employees or independents? "Most" will not survive a follow-up question.
- Where are the 40 stores? Without locations there is no local coverage, and the expansion number is abstract.
- Has any of this already been disclosed in a filing or earnings call? If so, the 24-hour clock on the investor angle may already have run.

**Next step:** `journalist-fit-check`. Four viable shapes exist. Pick one, then resolve it to real reporters. The pharmacy angle should wait until the chain is named.
