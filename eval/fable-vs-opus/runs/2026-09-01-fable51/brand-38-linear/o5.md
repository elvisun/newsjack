**Linear opens the product-management market it spent years refusing to enter**

- **Story type:** category-creation
- **Why a journalist cares:** A productivity-software reporter at a B2B software trade outlet or an enterprise-tools newsletter that covers the Jira/Asana/Notion competitive set. The hook is the boundary crossing: a tool with a hard-won reputation as *the engineering tool* is now claiming roadmapping and stakeholder surface area, which is the part of the workflow product managers currently buy elsewhere. This reporter can test whether the product-management tooling category is consolidating into engineering-first tools rather than the other way round. Not for consumer tech desks, general startup blogs, or design-press outlets that only cover visual tooling.
- **Why now:** The launch is live today, and category-boundary stories run best on the announcement day when the competitive set is forced to react.
- **Decay:** 24hr — the launch itself is a one-day event; a reporter who wants the broader category thesis will need more than the launch to hold a story past this week.
- **What makes it distinct:** Ladder lens, zoomed up — the protagonist is the category, not Linear. The other angles drop to the product decision, the buyer, and the company's go-to-market.
- **Proof it needs:** what roadmapping and stakeholder views actually do that a PM cannot already do in Linear today; whether existing customers were already forcing Linear into PM workflows and asked for this; the pricing for the new tier, since a category claim without a price is unfalsifiable; any named team using it in a design partner or beta capacity.
- **Facts it rests on:** Linear is launching Linear for Product; the new product adds roadmapping and stakeholder views aimed at product managers and designers, not just engineers; tens of thousands of teams use Linear.

**A keyboard-first tool built for engineers now has to serve people who don't live in keyboard shortcuts**

- **Story type:** contrarian
- **Why a journalist cares:** A software-design or product-craft reporter at a design-and-technology publication or a product-craft newsletter covering interface opinionation and its limits. The prevailing belief in this beat is that Linear's speed and opinionation *are* the product — the reason engineers defected from heavier trackers. Stakeholder views are, by definition, for people who open the tool weekly, not hourly, and that cuts against the design philosophy the company is known for. This is a real tension a craft reporter can interrogate rather than a manufactured one. Not for enterprise-IT desks, procurement press, or anyone covering the tool as a line item.
- **Why now:** The tension only becomes reportable at the moment the product ships; before today it was speculation about a roadmap.
- **Decay:** week — design-philosophy stories are reported over days, not hours, and don't die with the launch cycle.
- **What makes it distinct:** Inversion lens — this is the only angle where the fact is evidence *against* the company's own reputation rather than in support of it. The category angle treats the expansion as market movement; this one treats it as a design bet with a cost.
- **Proof it needs:** an on-record designer or founder explaining what was cut, slowed, or compromised to serve occasional stakeholder users; screenshots or a walkthrough of the stakeholder view; whether the keyboard-driven interaction model survives intact in the new surfaces or is replaced by something more conventional; a PM or designer who used it in beta and can say whether it feels like Linear.
- **Facts it rests on:** Linear is known for a fast, opinionated, keyboard-driven interface; the new product adds roadmapping and stakeholder views aimed at product managers and designers, not just engineers.

**Selling to a second buyer without a sales team**

- **Story type:** counterposition
- **Why a journalist cares:** A go-to-market or SaaS-business reporter at a startup-business outlet or an operator newsletter covering bottom-up software distribution. Word-of-mouth growth works when the buyer is an engineer who adopts a tool personally; roadmaps and stakeholder views are bought by product leaders and often approved by someone above them. The story is whether a company that reached tens of thousands of teams on almost no marketing can reach a second buyer the same way, or whether this launch quietly forces the sales motion it has avoided. Not for product-craft or design press, and not for engineering trade outlets that don't cover distribution.
- **Why now:** The expansion is announced today and the go-to-market question is unanswered in public — the window to ask it before the company settles on a narrative is now.
- **Decay:** week — a go-to-market analysis can run several days after launch if a founder or executive supplies the argument on record.
- **What makes it distinct:** Perspective lens, company-as-seller protagonist, at the distribution altitude. The category angle is about the market, the contrarian angle is about the interface; this one is about whether the business model survives the expansion.
- **Proof it needs:** an on-record statement on whether Linear will hire enterprise sales for this tier; the pricing model for the new tier; how the company defines "tens of thousands of teams" and whether it will give an exact number; evidence of how the word-of-mouth channel currently converts, since the facts assert it without a metric; whether product-manager adoption to date has been organic or engineer-led.
- **Facts it rests on:** the company says tens of thousands of teams use it, growing mostly through word of mouth with little marketing; Linear is privately held and deliberately small; no enterprise sales team is mentioned; pricing for the new tier is not yet announced.

**What product managers actually lose when their roadmap lives outside the engineers' tool**

- **Story type:** trend
- **Why a journalist cares:** A product-management trade reporter or a practitioner-facing publication covering how PM teams work — the beat that runs "how we structure our roadmap" reporting, not company news. The angle is the split-stack problem: roadmap in one tool, execution in another, and the translation cost between them. Linear's launch is the peg; the reported story belongs to the PMs who live with the split today. Not for VC or funding reporters, not for general business desks, and not for anyone who needs a company milestone to justify the piece.
- **Why now:** Honestly weaker than the others — the launch supplies a peg, but the underlying workflow problem has no clock on it. Treat this as a launch-adjacent trend pitch, not a news pitch.
- **Decay:** week — the peg fades quickly; the workflow question does not, but it stops being tied to Linear after that.
- **What makes it distinct:** Perspective lens with the customer as protagonist, at the ground rung of the ladder. Linear is a supporting character here, which is exactly what makes it a different story from the three above — and the only one a reporter can write without Linear's cooperation.
- **Proof it needs:** at least one named product manager or design lead who will describe the split-stack cost on record — without this the angle is a topic, not a story; any internal data Linear has on how teams currently work around the missing roadmapping layer; what specifically the stakeholder view replaces in a PM's current toolchain. The facts given contain no customer names and no usage data, so this angle is the least ready to pitch.
- **Facts it rests on:** the new product adds roadmapping and stakeholder views aimed at product managers and designers, not just engineers; tens of thousands of teams use Linear.

**Uncomfortable questions**

- Pricing is unannounced. Every reporter who takes this seriously will ask what the new tier costs, and "we haven't said" is a weak answer on launch day for a product whose whole claim is a new buyer. Is the price actually undecided, or is it decided and unannounced? Those are different conversations.
- "Tens of thousands of teams" is a range, not a number, and it's about the existing product. Will you give an exact figure? Will you give any figure at all for product-manager or designer usage? Without one, the expansion has no measurable starting line.
- Is there a single named customer — a PM, a design lead, a team — who used this before launch and will talk? Right now there are zero named customers in these facts. That single gap is what separates the two strongest angles from the two thinnest ones.
- Was this built because customers asked, or because the market for engineering-only tools is bounded? Both are legitimate answers, but they produce opposite stories, and a reporter will find the second one more interesting than the first.
- Does the keyboard-driven model actually extend to stakeholder views, or does the new surface look and behave like the tools Linear's users left? If the honest answer is the latter, the contrarian angle is stronger than the category angle — and you should decide now whether you want that story reported.
- No current news signal was supplied with this update, so nothing here carries a same-day peg beyond the launch itself. If a competitor moves or a category story is already running this week, the decay tags above shorten.
