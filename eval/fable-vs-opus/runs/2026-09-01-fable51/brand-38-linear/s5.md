The real test here is whether an interface built and hardened for engineers — fast, opinionated, keyboard-first — can serve PM and design workflows that have very different tooling habits. Not for enterprise-sales trade press, hardware reporters, or general consumer-tech blogs with no B2B software beat.

- **Decay:** week — the launch itself is a 24-hour news event, but the "does this design philosophy travel" thesis has runway for about a week before it's stale.
- **What makes it distinct:** Ladder lens, zoomed up to the category/design-philosophy level — about whether an interface style crosses functional boundaries, not the company's business model (below) or its competitive position (below).
- **Proof it needs:** a working demo or screenshots of the roadmapping/stakeholder views so a reporter can judge whether it still "feels like Linear" or reads as bolted-on; comment from a PM or designer who has tried it; any figure on how many of the "tens of thousands of teams" already had non-engineers using Linear informally before this launch.
