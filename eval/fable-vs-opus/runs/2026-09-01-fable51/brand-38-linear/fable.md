**Linear moves beyond engineers, taking on the product-management stack**

- **Story type:** counterposition
- **Why a journalist cares:** An enterprise-software reporter at a tech business outlet covering the productivity-tools category and its power balance. The hook is not the feature list — it's that a tool engineers chose for themselves is now reaching into territory owned by incumbent product-management suites, with roadmapping and stakeholder views aimed at PMs and designers. Not for consumer tech press, design blogs, or general startup desks that don't track category competition.
- **Why now:** The launch of Linear for Product is the live event.
- **Decay:** 24hr — standard launch-news cycle; the category-fight framing dies with the announcement unless an incumbent responds.
- **What makes it distinct:** Conflict lens, incumbent protagonist — the story is who's threatened, not what Linear shipped. The other kept angles drop to Linear's growth model and its design bet.
- **Proof it needs:** which incumbent tools Linear expects PMs to leave, on record; any honest head-to-head evidence (a team that switched, a feature comparison Linear will defend); pricing for the new tier, which is currently unannounced and a reporter will ask for first.
- **Facts it rests on:** Linear is launching Linear for Product, expanding beyond engineering teams; the product adds roadmapping and stakeholder views aimed at product managers and designers.

**Linear is expanding into a new market without the SaaS growth playbook**

- **Story type:** contrarian
- **Why a journalist cares:** A business-of-software reporter or B2B SaaS strategy newsletter writer covering go-to-market models. The prevailing belief: expanding beyond your core persona requires a sales-led motion — enterprise reps, marketing spend, headcount. Linear's stated counter-evidence: tens of thousands of teams, growth mostly by word of mouth, little marketing, deliberately small, and no enterprise sales team mentioned for this launch. Whether that survives a multi-persona expansion is a real open question this reporter can test. Not for product-review outlets, daily launch-news desks, or design press.
- **Why now:** The expansion is the moment the word-of-mouth model gets stress-tested; the launch is the peg.
- **Decay:** week — GTM-strategy pieces can run through the launch week; after that the angle needs adoption evidence.
- **What makes it distinct:** Inversion lens, the company's growth model as protagonist — this is about how Linear sells, not what it sells or to whom. Structurally separate from the category-fight and design-bet angles.
- **Proof it needs:** on-record confirmation that there is no enterprise sales team and none planned for the new tier (the facts only say none is mentioned — do not assert the absence); headcount or a "deliberately small" specifics the founder will defend; any real adoption number beyond "tens of thousands of teams," which won't survive a reporter's first follow-up.
- **Facts it rests on:** tens of thousands of teams use Linear, growing mostly through word of mouth with little marketing; the company is privately held and deliberately small; Linear is expanding beyond engineering teams with Linear for Product.

**Linear bets product managers want a tool that feels built for engineers**

- **Story type:** trend
- **Why a journalist cares:** A product-design or design-tools reporter at a product/design trade outlet covering interface craft and how opinionated tools handle audience expansion. Linear's identity is a fast, opinionated, keyboard-driven interface tuned for engineers; stakeholder views and roadmaps serve people who don't live in that interface. Whether the opinionated design survives a broader audience — or gets diluted by it — is a genuine craft story. Not for business desks, VC newsletters, or enterprise-software market reporters.
- **Why now:** The launch makes the design tension concrete for the first time; before it, this was hypothetical.
- **Decay:** week — the craft question runs through the launch window; it needs hands-on access or a design lead interview to run later.
- **What makes it distinct:** Surprise/contrast lens, the product's design philosophy as protagonist — different beat and different reader from both business angles.
- **Proof it needs:** access to the product or a demo of the stakeholder views; a design lead or founder on record about what they refused to add for PMs; ideally one beta team with both engineers and PMs using it — none is supplied today.
- **Facts it rests on:** Linear is known for a fast, opinionated, keyboard-driven interface; Linear for Product adds roadmapping and stakeholder views aimed at product managers and designers, not just engineers.

**Refused angles**

- "Dev tools everywhere are expanding into full-company suites" — `hallucinated_fact`. One company is not a trend; no other examples were supplied. If the user brings two or three comparable expansions, this becomes a real zoom-up angle.
- "Tens of thousands of teams now run on Linear" — `no_journalist_shape`. A vague, uncontextualized number with the exact count withheld is a press-release line, not a data story. No beat would run it as a lead.
- "How [customer] ships faster with Linear for Product" — `hallucinated_fact`. No named customer, beta user, or result was supplied; the angle would have to be invented whole.
- "Linear revolutionizes product management for modern teams" — `slop`. Press-release framing; killed on the first read, not rewritten.
- "Linear's new tier undercuts the incumbents on price" — `hallucinated_fact`. Pricing is explicitly unannounced.

**Uncomfortable questions**

- Will Linear put a real number behind "tens of thousands of teams" for this launch? The exact count is withheld, and every business reporter above will ask in the first reply.
- Is there a single named team — even a design partner — already using Linear for Product? Without one, the design-bet angle is a hypothesis and the counterposition angle has no switching evidence.
- Is "no enterprise sales team" actually true and durable, or is a sales motion quietly arriving with the new tier? If a reporter finds sales job postings, the contrarian angle collapses on you.
- Why launch without pricing? "Not yet announced" reads as either unfinished or strategic — decide which story you're telling before a journalist decides for you.
