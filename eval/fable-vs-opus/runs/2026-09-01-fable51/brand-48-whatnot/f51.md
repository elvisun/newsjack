**Whatnot moves $3B a year in live sales and is betting the US will shop the way Asia already does**

- **Story type:** trend
- **Why a journalist cares:** An e-commerce reporter at a retail-tech trade outlet or a business publication's consumer-internet desk, specifically one covering marketplace formats rather than individual startups. The story is the format, not the company: live auctions as a US shopping behavior, with the $3B figure as the first hard evidence at scale. The Asia comparison gives this reporter a frame they can test with outside data. Not for collectibles hobby press, general startup-funding desks, or gadget reviewers.
- **Why now:** The $3B disclosure is the news hook. The "does the US catch up to Asia" question has a longer window if the reporter can get comparative numbers.
- **Decay:** week. The milestone itself is a one-day item, but the format-adoption trend supports a week of follow-up reporting.
- **What makes it distinct:** Ladder lens, top rung. The category is the protagonist. The other kept angles drop down to the sellers, the incumbents in new categories, and the collectors who built the platform.
- **Proof it needs:** a precise definition of "$3B annual sales" (gross merchandise value or revenue, calendar year or trailing twelve months); a prior-year figure so the reporter can see growth rather than a snapshot; a third-party source for the "far larger in Asia" claim; an on-record executive stating the US bet in their own words.
- **Facts it rests on:** crossed $3B in annual livestream-commerce sales; sellers run live video auctions and shoppers buy in real time; the company says live commerce is far larger in Asia and it is betting the US catches up.

**Whatnot wants to sell you sneakers and phones the way it sells trading cards**

- **Story type:** counterposition
- **Why a journalist cares:** A retail trade reporter covering marketplace competition in fashion resale or consumer electronics, the beats Whatnot is walking into. Those categories have established marketplaces with fixed-price and bid formats, and this reporter's readers are the sellers and platforms about to feel it. The question they will ask is whether a live-auction format built for cards changes anything about how sneakers or electronics move. Not for VC reporters, hobby press, or general tech news desks.
- **Why now:** The category expansion is the announcement. It is standard company news with a short window unless the user can show the new categories already carry real volume.
- **Decay:** 24hr. Category-expansion news goes cold fast without numbers behind it.
- **What makes it distinct:** Perspective lens, incumbent protagonist. Conflict news value. This is about who is threatened in fashion and electronics, not about live commerce as a trend or about Whatnot's original base.
- **Proof it needs:** what share of the $3B already comes from fashion and electronics; when those categories opened; at least one named seller or brand active in them; a concrete explanation of what the live-auction format does in these categories that a fixed-price listing does not. Name the incumbents you believe you are taking share from, with evidence, or the reporter will pick them for you.
- **Facts it rests on:** the platform started with trading cards and collectibles and is now adding fashion and electronics; sellers run live video auctions; Whatnot takes a commission on sales.

**Whatnot's sellers moved $3B in a year. What they kept is still a secret.**

- **Story type:** data
- **Why a journalist cares:** A creator-economy or platform-labor reporter at a business outlet, or a data reporter covering how marketplace earnings distribute across sellers. The $3B number invites the obvious next question: how many sellers, how concentrated the earnings, and what the platform takes. This reporter will write that story with or without the company. Pitching it means opening the numbers. Not for product reviewers, hobby press, or general consumer-shopping columnists.
- **Why now:** The $3B disclosure makes the undisclosed half of the picture newly conspicuous.
- **Decay:** week. Earnings-distribution pieces run on the milestone's tail, not the milestone itself.
- **What makes it distinct:** Data lens, variation and ranking. Seller protagonist. Every other kept angle treats the $3B as a headline figure; this one treats it as a distribution to be broken down.
- **Proof it needs:** the take rate; the seller-earnings distribution (median seller earnings, share of sales from the top decile of sellers, number of active sellers); a decision from the company that it is willing to publish these. Without that decision this angle does not exist as a pitch.
- **Facts it rests on:** crossed $3B in annual livestream-commerce sales; Whatnot takes a commission on sales; seller-earnings distribution and take-rate not fully disclosed.

**The trading-card app that outgrew trading cards**

- **Story type:** trend
- **Why a journalist cares:** A hobby trade reporter at a collectibles or trading-card outlet whose readers sell on the platform and want to know what the pivot means for them. The relevance is direct: card sellers built the $3B, and the company is now publicly pointing its growth story at other categories. This reporter needs to hear whether collectibles remain a priority or become a legacy line. Not for business-press e-commerce desks, VC reporters, or fashion trade.
- **Why now:** The expansion announcement is the first public signal that collectibles are no longer the whole story.
- **Decay:** week. Hobby press moves slower than business press, and the question stays live until the company answers it.
- **What makes it distinct:** News-values lens, good news and bad news at once, with the original collector community as protagonist. It is the same expansion fact as the counterposition angle, read from the side that stands to lose attention rather than the side that stands to gain a competitor.
- **Proof it needs:** the share of the $3B that is still collectibles; an on-record commitment or roadmap for the collectibles category; a card seller willing to speak about what the expansion changes for them.
- **Facts it rests on:** the platform started with trading cards and collectibles; it is now adding fashion and electronics; crossed $3B in annual livestream-commerce sales.

**Refused angles**

- "Whatnot hits $3B milestone in live shopping" as its own story: `slop`. A bare number with no context is a press release, not a story. The number only works inside the trend, data, or expansion frames above.
- "Whatnot is redefining how America shops": `slop`. Undefended superlative with no fact behind it.
- "Live shopping isn't dead in America after all": `hallucinated_fact`. A contrarian angle needs a documented prevailing belief that US live shopping failed. None was supplied. If you can cite that belief with sources, it becomes a sharper version of the first angle rather than a separate one.
- Founder profile: `hallucinated_fact`. No founder names, backgrounds, or decisions were supplied. Anything written here would be invented.
- "What a $5B valuation says about Whatnot's take rate": `no_why_now_but_required`. The valuation is a past mark with no new round attached, and the take-rate question already belongs to the seller-earnings data angle.
- "One seller who went from cards to sneakers": `duplicate`. Until you have a named seller with real numbers, this is the proof item for the counterposition angle, not a separate customer story. Bring the seller and it comes back as a customer-story angle.
- Consumer-protection or auction-rules angle on live selling: `hallucinated_fact`. No regulatory fact was supplied.

**Uncomfortable questions**

- Is $3B gross merchandise value or revenue, and over what period? Every angle above changes shape depending on the answer, and a reporter will ask in the first reply.
- What was the figure a year ago? A single number with no growth rate is a snapshot, and the trend angle needs a direction.
- Will you disclose the take rate and the seller-earnings distribution? If the answer is no, the seller-earnings story still gets written, just without you in it.
- What share of the $3B is fashion and electronics today? If it is small, the expansion is a roadmap announcement, not news, and the counterposition angle shrinks to a line.
- Where does "far larger in Asia" come from? Your own estimate or a third-party figure you can hand a reporter?
- Can any seller in the new categories go on record with a number?

**Next step:** `find-journalists`, but only after you pin down the $3B definition and pick one of the four angles. Four viable shapes exist; the list should be built for one of them, not all four.
