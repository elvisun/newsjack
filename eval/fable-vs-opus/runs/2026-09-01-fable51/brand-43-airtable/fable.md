**Down from its peak valuation, Airtable bets its identity on AI agents**

- **Story type:** trend
- **Why a journalist cares:** An enterprise-software or private-markets reporter at a business outlet who covers late-stage SaaS companies repricing and repositioning in the AI era. The hook is not the product — it's a privately held company last valued at $11B, down from a higher peak, formally retiring the "spreadsheet-database" identity it was built on. That reporter can use Airtable as the next data point in the "pre-AI decacorns rebrand to survive" story the business press is already telling. Not for product-review outlets, developer press, or general consumer tech desks.
- **Why now:** The messaging shift from "spreadsheet-database" to "AI-native app platform" is happening now, alongside the agent launch.
- **Decay:** week — the repositioning is anchored to this announcement, but the valuation-pressure framing can carry a context piece for days after launch day.
- **What makes it distinct:** News-values lens — follow-up plus conflict. The valuation-and-identity pressure is the protagonist, not the agents. The other kept angles are about the product category and the enterprise buyer.
- **Proof it needs:** an Airtable executive willing to address the valuation history on record rather than ducking it; specifics on what changes operationally with the repositioning beyond messaging; any revenue or usage trajectory the company will stand behind.
- **Facts it rests on:** privately held, last valued at $11B, down from a higher peak; messaging shift from "spreadsheet-database" to "AI-native app platform"; launch of agents that act on company data.
