**Duolingo says beta users talked to its AI character for nine minutes at a stretch**

- **Story type:** data
- **Why a journalist cares:** A consumer-AI reporter at a tech outlet covering voice agents, AI companions, and what people actually do with them. The hook is not the feature; it is the session length. A nine-minute spoken exchange with a branded character, inside a learning app, is a usage number this beat rarely gets from a public company. Not for education-policy reporters, markets desks, or chess press.
- **Why now:** The video-call feature is moving from beta to paid Max subscribers now, and the beta number is the first public engagement figure for it.
- **Decay:** week. The launch peg is a 24-hour event, but a usage figure holds up as a data point for a week if the methodology survives questions.
- **What makes it distinct:** Data lens, scale rung. The protagonist is the behavior, not the company, the critics, or the business model. The other kept angles do not depend on the nine-minute figure.
- **Proof it needs:** beta sample size and duration; whether nine minutes means one call or one app session; the comparable average length of a standard text lesson so the number has context; whether Lily's replies are model-generated or scripted; a Duolingo spokesperson willing to explain the measurement on record.
- **Facts it rests on:** paid Max subscribers can have live spoken video conversations with the character Lily; engagement on the video feature in beta averaged nine minutes per session.

**Duolingo's new split: chess for free, AI conversation for paying subscribers**

The hook is the tiering decision: a free chess course that widens the top of the funnel, paired with a compute-heavy video feature reserved for the Max tier, at a company that reports itself profitable with daily active users up 40 percent to a record. That reporter can ask whether AI video is a margin risk or a paid-tier driver. Not for chess press, ed-tech pedagogy reporters, or consumer-AI feature reviewers.
- **Why now:** The two launches land together now, and the next earnings report is the natural follow-up moment.
- **Decay:** week. The launch itself is 24-hour news, but the subscription-strategy read runs until the next quarterly numbers replace it.
- **What makes it distinct:** Perspective lens, investor-and-money protagonist, with the good-news-and-bad-news pairing of growth versus AI cost. It uses the DAU figure and the profitability fact as context, not as the story.

- **Facts it rests on:** the video feature is limited to paid Max subscribers; the chess course is free for all users; Duolingo says daily active users grew 40 percent year over year to a new high; Duolingo is publicly traded and profitable.

- **Why a journalist cares:** A chess or board-games reporter at a games outlet or chess-specialist publication. The hook is a large consumer learning app putting a free beginner chess course in front of its entire user base, which is a distribution story that community cares about regardless of Duolingo's language business. A general culture reporter covering hobbies could also take it, but only with detail on what the course actually teaches. Not for AI reporters, markets desks, or ed-tech policy reporters.

- **Decay:** 24hr. This is standard launch news with no trend behind it in the facts supplied.
- **What makes it distinct:** Perspective lens, category protagonist, relevance news value. It is the only angle whose audience is the chess community rather than tech, education, or finance readers.
- **Proof it needs:** the course's scope, from rules through tactics or beyond; whether learners can play against an opponent; who designed the curriculum; the DAU number's source so the reach claim can be stated precisely.
- **Facts it rests on:** a chess course launches free for all users; daily active users reached a new high.

**Uncomfortable questions**

- What does "nine minutes per session" actually measure, and how many beta users is it based on? If the sample is small or the definition is loose, the strongest angle in this set collapses on the first reporter question.
- Is there any evidence that conversation practice with Lily improves speaking ability? Without it, the response-to-critics angle is a concession dressed as a rebuttal.
- Is a beta user willing to be named and describe a session? That would unlock a customer-story angle this set does not have.
- Who are the specific educators who criticized gamification, and does Duolingo want to engage them by name? Vague "critics say" framing will be treated as straw.
- Is the 40 percent DAU growth in a filing or an earnings release, or is it a marketing claim? A markets reporter will check.
- Will the company discuss what AI video costs per session? If the answer is no, the subscription-strategy angle loses its most interesting question.
