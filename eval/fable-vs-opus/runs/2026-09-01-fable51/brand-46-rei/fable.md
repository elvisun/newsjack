**Resale and repair stop being a pilot: REI puts trade-in and gear repair in all 180+ stores**

- **Story type:** trend
- **Why a journalist cares:** A retail-operations or recommerce trade reporter covering circular retail — the desk that tracks resale programs graduating from marketing experiments to standard store infrastructure. The hook is the structural shift: this ran online and in select stores, and now it's in every one of 180+ locations, which means staffing, floor space, and reverse logistics at chain scale. Not for consumer gear-review press, general startup blogs, or sustainability lifestyle outlets that don't cover store operations.
- **Why now:** The expansion to all stores is the announcement; the pilot-to-default transition is the time hook.
- **Decay:** 24hr — standard company-expansion news; the operational-shift framing might stretch to a week if REI supplies program data.
- **What makes it distinct:** Ladder lens, zoomed up — the category (resale as core retail infrastructure) is the protagonist, not REI's members or its ownership structure. The other kept angles drop down to the member and over to the co-op model.
- **Proof it needs:** trade-in and repair volume from the prior online/select-store phase; how stores are being staffed or retrofitted for repair work; any number on used-gear sales — the share is currently undisclosed, and a reporter will ask first.
- **Facts it rests on:** members can trade used gear for credit and get repairs at any of REI's 180+ stores; the program previously ran online and in select stores; REI says resale and repair support its sustainability commitments.

**The retailer paying its owners a record dividend is the one betting on used gear**

- **Story type:** contrarian
- **Why a journalist cares:** A business reporter covering retail business models and alternative ownership structures — the beat that asks whether governance shapes strategy. The prevailing belief: resale and repair cannibalize new-product sales, so retailers keep them as side projects. REI is doing the opposite — putting both in every store — while issuing its largest-ever member dividend, and the co-op structure (no public shareholders demanding quarterly margin) is the candidate explanation. Not for outdoor gear press, local business desks, or markets reporters who only cover listed companies.
- **Why now:** The record dividend and the all-store expansion landed in the same year; together they're the evidence pair. Without the expansion announcement this is evergreen structure commentary.
- **Decay:** week — business-model analysis survives the announcement day if REI puts a finance or strategy lead on record.
- **What makes it distinct:** Inversion lens with the ownership structure as protagonist — it argues against a prevailing belief rather than reporting the expansion. Distinct from the trend angle (category) and the member angle (customer).
- **Proof it needs:** the actual dividend figure and prior-year comparison — "largest-ever" without a number dies on first contact; an on-record REI executive explaining how the co-op structure affects the resale/repair decision; any margin or sales data on used gear. The undisclosed used-gear share is the hole in this angle — show it, don't paper over it.
- **Facts it rests on:** REI is a member-owned cooperative, not publicly traded; the co-op issued its largest-ever annual member dividend this year; trade-in and repair are expanding to all 180+ stores.

**What an REI membership now gets you: store credit for old gear and a repair counter in every store**

- **Story type:** customer-story
- **Why a journalist cares:** A consumer-retail or outdoor-trade reporter doing service journalism on what membership programs are actually worth — the desk that explains trade-in values, repair access, and dividends in concrete member terms. The peg is that the benefit just became universal: any member, any of 180+ stores. Not for business-model desks, VC press, or policy reporters.
- **Why now:** The benefit is newly available at every store; before this it depended on living near a select location or using the online program.
- **Decay:** week — service pieces about a newly universal program hold up past the announcement day.
- **What makes it distinct:** Ladder lens, zoomed down — the member is the protagonist and the story is the concrete transaction, not the category shift or the co-op thesis.
- **Proof it needs:** real trade-in valuation examples (what a used tent or pair of boots fetches in credit); repair pricing and turnaround; a named member or store employee willing to walk through a transaction. Without at least one concrete example this is a press release, not a story.
- **Facts it rests on:** members can trade used gear for store credit and get repairs at any of REI's 180+ stores; the program previously ran online and in select stores; the co-op issued its largest-ever annual member dividend this year.

**Refused angles**

- "REI revolutionizes sustainable retail with nationwide circular-economy rollout" — `slop`.
- "Used gear is now a major driver of REI's sales" — `hallucinated_fact`: the share of sales from used gear is explicitly undisclosed.
- "REI's repair network is the largest in outdoor retail" — `hallucinated_fact`: no comparative data was supplied; 180+ stores is a count, not a ranking.
- "Repair counters arrive as right-to-repair momentum builds" — `no_why_now_but_required`: no current legislative or news signal was supplied, and a news-peg angle without a real signal is forced relevance.
- "Record dividend lands amid union drives — what REI owes its workers vs its members" — `off-beat`: a real story, but it's the story a labor reporter writes *about* REI, not one REI pitches. Pitching it invites the conflict framing on the company's own terms and timeline. Prepare for the question instead (see below).
- "The sustainability angle on REI's resale expansion" — `duplicate`: same lens and protagonist as the trend angle; sustainability is the second paragraph of that story, not a separate one.

**Uncomfortable questions**

- What is the dividend number? "Largest-ever" with no figure and no prior-year comparison will not survive a single reporter follow-up, and the contrarian angle collapses without it.
- Will REI disclose any used-gear sales or volume data? With the share undisclosed, the sustainability claim reads as marketing, and every reporter on the trend angle will ask why the number is being withheld.
- When a reporter pairs the record member dividend with the unionization activity at several stores — and one will — what is the on-record answer? Decide before pitching, not after.
- Does the prior online/select-store program have any results to show? An expansion of a program with no published outcomes is a thinner story than it looks.
