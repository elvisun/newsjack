The story isn't the announcement; it's that a national outdoor retailer is graduating resale/repair out of an online-plus-select-stores pilot into every store, which is an operational and floor-space commitment competitors get benchmarked against. Not for general consumer lifestyle desks, breaking-news wires, or sustainability-marketing newsletters that will just reprint the release.
- **Why now:** REI is announcing the all-store expansion now; the broader "is recommerce real retail infrastructure" question has a longer tail.
- **Decay:** week — the rollout is the timely peg, but the circular-retail trend angle holds for about a week before it needs fresh data.
- **What makes it distinct:** Ladder lens, zoomed up — the category (in-store resale/repair as standard retail operations) is the protagonist, not REI's program. Differs from the dividend angle (money), the member-owner angle (governance), and the labor angle (workers).

- **Why a journalist cares:** A labor/retail-workforce reporter covering union activity in retail and the gap between member/customer benefit and worker conditions. The prevailing belief this cuts against: a member-owned co-op is assumed to be the worker- and community-friendly alternative to corporate retail. The evidence against it, from the facts here, is unionization activity at several stores happening alongside a record member dividend — value flowing to members while workers organize. That tension is the story. Not for sustainability or product desks that won't touch the labor angle.
- **Why now:** The record dividend is being announced now against a backdrop of ongoing unionization activity at several stores — the timing juxtaposition is what makes it pitchable today.
- **Decay:** 24hr — the contrast is anchored to the dividend announcement; without fresh labor developments it cools within a day.
- **What makes it distinct:** Inversion/contrarian lens — names the "co-op = automatically good employer" belief and uses the dividend-versus-union-drive contrast as evidence against it. The only angle with workers as the stakeholder and the only one built on conflict.
- **Proof it needs:** which stores, how many, and the current status of the union activity (the facts say "several stores" but give no specifics — the reporter will need them); whether store workers share in the member dividend or only members do; REI's on-record response to the worker-versus-member-value framing. Pitching the contrast without current labor specifics is the failure mode — confirm them first.
- **Facts it rests on:** the co-op issued its largest-ever annual member dividend this year; REI is a member-owned cooperative, not publicly traded; the co-op has faced unionization activity at several stores.
