**REI puts used-gear trade-ins and repairs in all 180-plus stores**

- **Story type:** trend
- **Why a journalist cares:** A retail-operations or circular-commerce trade reporter covering resale programs, in-store services, and how retailers handle returns and used inventory. The hook is the move from pilot to standard infrastructure: resale and repair stop being a side channel and become something every store has to staff and stock. Not for general consumer tech press, sustainability opinion writers, or outdoor-gear review sites.
- **Why now:** The rollout to every store is the update. The program existed before online and in select stores; the news is that the experiment ended and the default changed.
- **Decay:** week. The rollout announcement is a 24-hour item, but the "resale as store infrastructure" framing holds for a week if REI can talk about how stores actually run it.
- **What makes it distinct:** Follow-up value plus the ladder lens zoomed up. This is the next chapter of a program the trade press has already seen in pilot form, told at the altitude of retail operations. The other kept angles move to the co-op's money, its workers, and a single store.
- **Proof it needs:** whether every store has repair technicians on site or is a drop-off point that ships gear elsewhere; what gear categories are accepted for trade-in and how credit is set; any pilot-phase numbers (items traded, repairs completed, credit issued); an on-record operator who ran the pilot and can say what changed in the rollout.
- **Facts it rests on:** members can trade used gear for store credit and get repairs at any of REI's 180+ stores; the program previously ran online and in select stores.

**REI's members, not shareholders, collect the co-op's biggest dividend yet**

- **Story type:** counterposition
- **Why a journalist cares:** A retail business reporter who covers ownership structures, cooperatives, and the alternative-to-public-company story. The protagonist is the co-op model. A retailer that answers to members rather than a stock price just posted its largest-ever payout to those members, in the same year it expanded a program that gives members store credit for old gear. That reporter can test whether the co-op structure is producing different decisions than a public retailer would make. Not for local hiring desks, gear reviewers, or labor reporters (they get the next angle).
- **Why now:** The record dividend is this year's number, and the annual dividend is the one moment a co-op's finances become news.
- **Decay:** 24hr. A dividend is standard company news. It stretches only if REI puts a partner-level explanation of the surplus on record.
- **What makes it distinct:** Perspective lens, money as protagonist. The rollout angle is about store operations; this is about where the surplus goes and who owns the company.
- **Proof it needs:** the dividend total and the per-member basis; the prior-year figure so "record" can be checked; whether it is a record in dollars, in rate, or only because membership grew; what drove the surplus; a named REI executive willing to explain the decision on record.
- **Facts it rests on:** the co-op issued its largest-ever annual member dividend this year; REI is a member-owned cooperative, not publicly traded; members can trade used gear for store credit.

**REI's record payout to members lands as workers organize at its stores**

- **Story type:** defensive-comment
- **Why a journalist cares:** A labor and workplace reporter covering retail unionization. That reporter will put the record dividend and the union activity in one sentence without any help from REI: the co-op had its best year for members while workers at several stores are organizing. Adding repair work to every store raises a second question about who does that labor and how it is paid. This is not an angle to pitch proactively to sustainability or retail-ops desks; it is the question REI must be ready to answer the moment the first two angles land.
- **Why now:** The dividend announcement gives labor reporters a fresh, dated reason to revisit the union story.
- **Decay:** week. The union activity is ongoing; the dividend gives it a peg that lasts as long as the announcement is being covered.
- **What makes it distinct:** Conflict lens, workers as protagonist. The other angles treat the dividend and the rollout as good news; this is the version where the same facts are a pressure point.
- **Proof it needs:** the current status of union activity (which stores, elections, negotiations, any complaints filed); whether the repair rollout creates new roles, retraining, or pay changes for store staff; a plain, approved on-record statement from REI about the dividend and store workers. Without that statement, do not pitch anything else.
- **Facts it rests on:** the co-op has faced unionization activity at several stores; the co-op issued its largest-ever annual member dividend this year; repairs are now available at any of REI's 180+ stores.

**Your old tent now has a trade-in price at the local REI**

- **Story type:** customer-story
- **Why a journalist cares:** A local business or outdoors-lifestyle feature reporter in a city with an REI store, writing the reader-facing version: what happens when you walk in with a worn pack, what you get for it, and who fixes the zipper. The rollout means every REI market now has this story available. Not for national business desks, ESG analysts, or trade press.
- **Why now:** The rollout to every store means a local reporter's nearest REI now offers it, which was not true when the program was online and in select stores.
- **Decay:** month. Local feature desks are not on a news clock, but the "newly available here" hook fades once the rollout is old.
- **What makes it distinct:** Ladder lens, bottom rung. One store, one member, one repair. The rollout angle is the same fact at the top of the ladder; this is the one that needs a named person and a photograph.
- **Proof it needs:** a named store willing to host a reporter; a repair technician who can be quoted; one member willing to be named who traded in gear or had it repaired; what the store accepts and refuses, and typical credit amounts.
- **Facts it rests on:** members can trade used gear for store credit and get repairs at any of REI's 180+ stores; the program previously ran online and in select stores.

**Refused angles**

- "Used gear is now a meaningful share of REI's sales" — `hallucinated_fact`. The share of sales from used gear was not disclosed. There is no data angle without the number.
- "REI's record dividend proves resale doesn't cannibalize new-gear sales" — `hallucinated_fact`. A contrarian angle needs a real prevailing belief and evidence against it. No link between resale and the dividend was supplied, and the resale share is unknown.
- "REI's resale expansion advances its sustainability commitments" — `duplicate`. Same rollout fact as the first angle in a sustainability envelope, and the only supporting fact is that REI says so. It becomes its own data angle only if REI supplies repair counts or diversion figures.
- "REI vs. publicly traded outdoor retailers on returning value to customers" — `hallucinated_fact`. No competitor facts were supplied. The co-op-model angle above stays on REI's own structure for that reason.
- "Peg the rollout to the start of summer hiking season" — `no_why_now_but_required`. A season is not a news signal. No current signal was supplied, so no 30-minute or 4-hour angle exists here.
- "REI revolutionizes circular retail for the outdoor industry" — `slop`.
- "REI is proud to return record value to its members" — `slop`.

**Uncomfortable questions**

- What is the dividend total and the per-member figure, and what was it last year? "Largest-ever" without a number will be the first thing a business reporter asks for, and "record because membership grew" is a different story than "record because the business did better."
- What share of sales is used gear? If REI will not disclose it, reporters will notice the omission in the same paragraph as the sustainability claim.
- Does every one of the 180-plus stores have repair capacity on site, or is it a drop-off counter that ships gear elsewhere? One is a rollout; the other is a logistics change.
- What is the current state of union activity, store by store? Is there an approved answer to "record dividend for members, what about the people on the floor?" Without one, pitching the first two angles invites the third.
- Does the repair rollout change store staffing, training, or pay? A labor reporter will ask; better to know the answer before a retail reporter does.
- Are there any pilot-phase numbers at all: items traded, repairs completed, credit issued? Those are the difference between the first angle being a story and being a topic.
