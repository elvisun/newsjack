# Opus 5 URL-to-panel evaluation — 2026-07-25

This evaluation used fresh Claude Code sessions addressed by the exact model ID
`claude-opus-5`. Executors could research the public web but could not see the
hidden gold set. Judges ran in separate tools-off sessions. A case passed only
when both the deterministic artifact contract and the semantic judge passed.

Raw model streams, assembled prompts, and logs are ignored. The committed run
directories contain the scored artifacts, manifests, grades, and verdicts.

## Results

| Stage | Cases | Deterministic | Semantic judge | Joint result |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Initial development test drive | Cloudflare, Zocdoc, Octopus Energy UK | 0/3 | Cloudflare: pass, 4.25/5 | 0/3 |
| Development retest after general fixes | Zocdoc | 1/1 | pass, 4.25/5 | 1/1 |
| Untouched held-out case | Airalo | 1/1 | pass, 4.25/5 | 1/1 |

The initial outputs looked semantically strong but failed machine contracts.
Cloudflare produced 50 cells and 92 candidates, yet its provisional hash
sentinels, source records, QA references, canonical-cell fields, enums,
contamination status, and selected IDs did not satisfy the validator. Zocdoc
and Octopus Energy UK emitted malformed YAML. The semantic Cloudflare pass did
not override those failures.

The general fixes made JSON-compatible YAML normative, filled every canonical
cell and candidate field, required exact cross-artifact references, reconciled
one QA decision per candidate, derived QA counts from decisions, defined honest
provisional `null` hashes, and tightened publisher-provenance classification.
The Zocdoc development retest then produced 44 cells, 86 candidates, and 78
accepted prompts and passed both gates.

The Airalo case was not used for tuning before execution. Its run researched 14
public sources, produced 34 cells and 68 candidates, accepted 61 prompts, and
passed the full deterministic validator. A fresh Opus 5 judge passed every hard
gate, found no critical failure or triggered anti-pattern, and scored the panel
4.25/5. The judge left one must-have partial: installation and activation timing
was present as a constraint but not modeled as its own buyer job and unaided
cell.

## Held-out limitations retained

The held-out artifacts were frozen rather than repaired after judging. The
verdict records:

- one report-row provenance mismatch and a charter-versus-panel locale mismatch;
- thin unaided global-package coverage;
- secondary rather than first-party provenance for two observed-language labels;
- no accepted wording-sensitivity variant;
- provisional `null` hashes and pending human gates.

These findings do not break a provisional directional panel, but they must be
resolved before freeze. General lessons from the development judge were added
to the skills after the held-out run: mechanically derived coverage totals,
market-side stratification for multi-sided products, lane-eligible wave-budget
math, a high-stakes professional-judgment boundary, priority-weight provenance,
raw-context privacy controls, disjoint partition math, mutually exclusive aided
statuses, and cluster-aware intervals. Those post-run wording changes are not
credited to the held-out score.

## Grader reliability

Before final aggregation, two pre-existing deterministic false positives were
fixed without changing the held-out output: lexical contamination now matches
normalized token sequences rather than substrings, and exact prompts containing
Markdown table pipes may be represented with the required `\|` escaping. The
eval grader now also checks exact accepted prompt text, not only prompt IDs.
